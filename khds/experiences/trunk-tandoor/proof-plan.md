# Proof Principle 1

> **Claims create proof obligations.**

If Trunk & Tandoor claims:

rooftop atmosphere

then:

the rooftop must be shown.

If it claims:

fire-led culinary identity

then:

fire and culinary craft must be evidenced.

If it claims:

a different experience of Nairobi

then:

Nairobi must remain:

visible

specific

and relevant.

---

# Proof Principle 2

> **Beautiful is not the same as believable.**

A beautiful photograph may create:

desire.

It does not automatically establish:

- culinary difference
- cultural meaning
- craft
- atmosphere
- social use
- operational quality

The proof must match:

the claim.

---

# Proof Principle 3

> **Minimum sufficient proof is better than maximum information.**

The guest does not need:

every dish

every corner

every process

every table

or:

every operating detail.

They need:

enough evidence

to make:

the relevant decision.

---

# Proof Principle 4

> **Different uncertainties require different evidence types.**

Photography may prove:

place.

It may help prove:

use.

It cannot prove:

CRM ownership.

Copy may explain:

meaning.

It cannot prove:

actual guest satisfaction.

Policy may prove:

a rule.

It cannot prove:

why management chose that rule.

---

# Proof Principle 5

> **Do not use production design to fabricate restaurant evidence.**

Design can:

organise

sequence

emphasise

and clarify:

evidence.

It must not:

invent it.

# Proof Validation Questions

During production review ask:

1. What is this section claiming?

2. Does it need proof?

3. What kind of claim is it?

4. Is the chosen evidence type capable of proving it?

5. Does the evidence show difference?

6. Is this evidence already provided elsewhere?

7. Could we remove something without losing confidence?

8. Are we revealing more than the guest needs?

9. Are we hiding something the guest needs?

10. Are we implying a fact we cannot verify?
