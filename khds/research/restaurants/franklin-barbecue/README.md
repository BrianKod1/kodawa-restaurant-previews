# Research Role

Franklin Barbecue is the eighth independent restaurant in the KHDS research set.

It was selected specifically to challenge the post-Dishoom framework.

Dishoom shifted KHDS from:

Reservation Architecture

toward:

Participation Architecture.

Franklin now tests whether that broader abstraction survives a hospitality model where:

* walk-up arrival is central
* service ends when product sells out
* waiting may be operationally significant
* preorder exists as a separate route
* founder and craft authority are unusually visible
* product scarcity is materially observable
* technical craft may carry more story weight than atmosphere or narrative complexity

---

# Primary Research Objective

> **Can KHDS survive queue-first, scarcity-constrained hospitality?**

---

# Secondary Research Objective

> **Can a founder-and-craft-led restaurant remain digitally simple because the product system itself carries the brand?**

---

# Direct Challenge

> **When does friction stop being a design problem and become a consequence—or potentially a ritual—of the hospitality model?**

This question must remain open.

The research should not assume that:

waiting

or

scarcity

automatically creates hospitality value.

# Why Franklin Matters

Franklin introduces a hospitality model that differs materially from:

* FYN
* Dishoom
* the Nairobi-led early research set

FYN tested:

high commitment.

Dishoom tested:

low-commitment, multi-intent participation.

Franklin introduces:

## Scarcity-Constrained Participation

The flagship restaurant currently operates Tuesday through Sunday from 11am until sold out, typically around 2–3pm.

That visible model means access is shaped by:

* arrival timing
* finite product
* physical participation
* demand
* uncertainty

rather than reservation alone.

## Route B — Preorder Pickup

↓

Selects future date

↓

Selects pickup time

↓

Orders substantial quantity

↓

↓

↓

This creates a materially different participation mode from normal restaurant arrival.

# Scarcity Research

Franklin introduces scarcity as a visible operating condition.

The official site states that service runs until sold out.

That creates several possible interpretations.

They must remain separate.

---

## Scarcity as Operational Reality

Finite production means:

product ends.

This is directly compatible with the visible site evidence.

---

## Scarcity as Guest Constraint

Late arrival may reduce access.

This is a reasonable inference from:

sold-out operation.

Actual guest impact requires further evidence.

---

## Scarcity as Brand Value

Scarcity might increase:

desire

or

prestige.

This is not established by the website.

Do not claim it without evidence.

---

## Scarcity as Manufactured Urgency

No current evidence should be used to make this claim.

Do not infer:

intentional artificial scarcity.

---

# Queue Research

Franklin is especially useful because KHDS has not yet studied a restaurant where physical waiting may be structurally important to participation.

The research must distinguish:

## Harmful Friction

Unnecessary:

* confusion
* delay
* difficulty
* poor communication

---

## Operational Constraint

Delay or limitation caused by:

* production capacity
* service model
* finite inventory
* demand

---

## Participatory Ritual

A behaviour that guests knowingly undertake and that may acquire social or symbolic meaning.

The third category remains:

hypothesis only.

Franklin research should not romanticise queues.
