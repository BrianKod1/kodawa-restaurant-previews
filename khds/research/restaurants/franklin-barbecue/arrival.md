# Research Objective

Study how Franklin Barbecue introduces the guest to a hospitality model shaped by:

* product reputation
* founder authority
* limited daily availability
* physical arrival
* queue-based access
* preorder as an alternative participation route
* counter-service simplicity

The central question is:

> **How does arrival work when the restaurant cannot guarantee access to every guest who wants to participate?**

---

# Primary Arrival Stress Test

> **Can KHDS distinguish harmful friction from unavoidable access constraints without romanticising inconvenience?**

Franklin is particularly useful because the visible guest journey is not built around:

reservation certainty.

Instead, normal restaurant participation appears to depend on:

* arriving
* entering the service flow
* product still being available

The official menu page states that Franklin operates Tuesday through Sunday from 11am until sold out, typically around 2–3pm. ([franklinbbq.com](https://franklinbbq.com/menu?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That creates a fundamentally different arrival problem from:

FYN

or

Dishoom.

# Final Arrival Statement

Franklin creates a useful correction to the assumption that sophisticated hospitality requires sophisticated digital arrival.

The visible experience may be comparatively simple:

Know the product.

Know the hours.

Know it can sell out.

Choose how to participate.

Arrive.

That simplicity does not automatically make the experience:

less designed.

It may instead reveal that the strongest digital system is sometimes the one that communicates:

the operating reality

with minimal distortion.

The important question is not:

> How do we make the queue feel premium?

It is:

> **How clearly and hospitably can the restaurant prepare the guest for a participation model where access is genuinely constrained?**

Franklin should not be used to glorify:

waiting

or

scarcity.

It should be used to understand whether:

clarity

can preserve hospitality when convenience cannot be guaranteed.
