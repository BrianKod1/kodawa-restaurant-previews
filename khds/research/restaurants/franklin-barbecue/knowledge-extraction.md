# Research Objective

Franklin was selected to challenge the post-Dishoom KHDS framework.

The primary question was:

> **Can KHDS survive queue-first, scarcity-constrained hospitality?**

The secondary question was:

> **Can a founder-and-craft-led restaurant remain digitally simple because the product system itself carries the brand?**

The direct challenge was:

> **When does friction stop being a design problem and become a consequence—or potentially a ritual—of the hospitality model?**

The dossier does not support romanticising:

* queues
* waiting
* scarcity
* inconvenience

The stronger outcome is more precise:

Franklin helps separate:

## Harmful Friction

Unnecessary:

* confusion
* ambiguity
* delay
* effort

from

## Operational Constraint

Limits created by:

* finite product
* production capacity
* service model
* access structure

and from

## Participatory Behaviour

What the guest physically does in order to enter the experience.

Whether participatory behaviour becomes:

ritual

or

brand value

requires stronger evidence.

# Final Knowledge Extraction

Franklin was selected to attack KHDS through:

queues

scarcity

founder authority

craft

and

operational simplicity.

It succeeded.

But the strongest result is not:

queues are hospitality.

The stronger result is:

> **KHDS needs to distinguish the design of the guest journey from the operating realities the guest journey must accommodate.**

Franklin cannot guarantee infinite product.

The correct digital experience therefore should not pretend:

availability is unlimited.

It should make:

the participation model

clear.

Franklin also reveals that:

simplicity

does not mean:

lack of architecture.

A guest can encounter a system with:

few steps

but

important decisions around:

* timing
* certainty
* quantity
* access
* effort

Franklin further challenges KHDS storytelling.

Dishoom showed:

a world can contain people.

Franklin shows:

people can create the world.

That puts:

`World Before Biography`

under its strongest pressure yet.

And Franklin challenges KHDS atmosphere research by showing that a restaurant may carry strong identity through:

craft

behaviour

product

and

participation

without obvious broad worldview.

Finally, Franklin strengthens the photography research.

A visually simple product does not eliminate the need to demonstrate:

difference.

It may make:

process

and

craft

more important.

The dossier therefore leaves KHDS with several high-value questions:

> Is worldview one atmosphere mechanism rather than a broadly reusable Candidate?

> Should people appear according to explanatory value rather than narrative sequence?

> Does system behind the plate include craft infrastructure?

> Should walk-in-first participation become a formal Candidate?

> Is atmosphere through use now independently repeated enough to promote?

> Is photography as use-case evidence now independently repeated enough to promote?

> Is complexity really the correct trigger for human assistance?

Those questions now belong in:

comparison.
