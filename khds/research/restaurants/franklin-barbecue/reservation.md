## Research Status

Independent Restaurant #8 Candidate

# Research Objective

Study how Franklin Barbecue structures guest participation across:

* normal dine-in
* physical line participation
* preorder pickup
* private events
* finite product availability
* commitment
* certainty
* human assistance
* operational complexity

The central question is:

> **What does participation architecture look like when the default restaurant experience does not depend on reservations at all?**

Franklin is especially important because Dishoom shifted KHDS from:

Reservation Architecture

toward:

Participation Architecture.

Franklin now tests whether that abstraction survives an even simpler and more capacity-constrained operating model.

---

# Primary Participation Stress Test

Franklin’s official dine-in model is straightforward.

The restaurant is open Tuesday through Sunday from 11am until sold out, typically around 2–3pm. The site instructs guests to join the line and notes that chairs are available for waiting.

For advance takeout, Franklin provides a separate preorder system with:

* ordering 2–56 days ahead
* 5-pound minimum
* 30-pound maximum
* advance payment
* selectable pickup date and time
* pickup between 10:30am and 2:00pm.

Franklin Backyard creates a third visible participation route for private events.

The visible architecture is therefore not:

Reservation

↓

Confirmation

↓

Visit.

It is closer to:

Guest Intent

↓

Choose Access Mode

↓

Accept Relevant Constraints

↓

Participate
