# Research Objective

Study how Alinea creates narrative meaning in a hospitality model where:

- the experience is highly differentiated
- Grant Achatz and his team carry strong public authority
- three distinct dining formats organise participation
- the restaurant has significant institutional recognition
- the product itself is already unusual
- long-form storytelling is not required to establish identity

The central question is:

> **Does Alinea need biography to explain the experience, or has the experience itself become strong enough to organise the story?**

Alinea’s official site foregrounds the restaurant’s innovative approach to modernist cuisine, its major awards and recognitions, Grant Achatz and his team, and the three nightly experiences: Kitchen Table, Gallery, and Salon. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

- awards replace story

- place is irrelevant
- heritage is weak

- founder storytelling is outdated
- minimal copy is universally better

- experience itself can organise story

- official positioning around innovative modernist cuisine
- Grant Achatz and team are publicly named
- three distinct nightly experiences
- major public awards and recognitions
- clear experience naming
- functional site language
- absence of heavy origin storytelling on the homepage

- how familiar guests already are with Grant Achatz

- whether people prefer experience-first storytelling
- whether founder biography would improve trust
- whether team recognition affects guest perception
- whether mystery increases anticipation

- guest research
