Canonical Research Position

SAME Café Denver is the twentieth independent hospitality case selected for the KHDS Track A research programme.

It follows:

Restaurant #19 — JBJ Soul Kitchen, Red Bank.

JBJ Soul Kitchen materially strengthened KHDS reasoning around:

hospitality without a fixed menu price

guesthood across different contribution states

suggested donation pressure

pay-it-forward participation

volunteering and meal access

economic-state privacy

The strongest Post–JBJ unresolved boundary was:

Invitation to Volunteer
≠

SAME Café retains:

community participation

volunteer-supported operation

a changing menu

mission embedded in the hospitality model

the need to preserve dignity across economic difference

food access as an explicit organisational purpose

It changes the participation condition.

JBJ Soul Kitchen describes volunteering as one route available to people unable to make the suggested donation, while parts of its public language leave the invitation / exchange boundary unresolved.

SAME Café publishes a more explicit rule:

every person participates by bringing:

time

money

or produce

to the table.

Its homepage describes guests as contributing one of those resources in exchange for a meal.

Its volunteer page gives one route a defined unit:

Restaurant #20 therefore asks whether:

when:

participation is universal in principle

and:

contribution routes differ in form, visibility, burden and operational consequence.

Its strongest initial boundary is:

≠

Primary Research Question

Can hospitality preserve equal guesthood when receiving a meal is explicitly connected to contributing money, time or produce—and what changes when participation becomes a stated exchange rather than an invitation?

Secondary Research Questions

Can universal participation remain pressure-free?

Are time, money and produce presented and performed as genuinely equivalent contributions?

Does contribution occur before the meal, after the meal or according to live capacity?

What happens when a guest wants to volunteer but no suitable shift is available?

What happens when a person cannot safely contribute time, money or produce?

Can staff explain the model without disclosing or inferring economic need?

Does participation create belonging, or must belonging precede participation?

Is the principal intent dining, contribution, community participation or a combination of these?

Does a changing menu require stable dietary and participation communication?

Initial Contradiction Hypotheses

1. Participation Required ≠ Guesthood Earned

A person should not have to prove contribution to deserve respectful hospitality.

2. Contribution Choice ≠ Absence of Pressure

Several options can still contain moral, social or practical coercion.

3. Exchange ≠ Rank

An explicit exchange can remain dignified, but it can also create visible categories.

4. Equal Meal ≠ Equal Burden

The same food can require materially different effort from different guests.

5. Thirty Minutes ≠ Equal Cost

A fixed time unit does not have the same personal cost for every person.

6. Time / Money / Produce ≠ Automatic Equivalence

The three resources differ in availability, measurability, privacy and operational use.

7. Visible Volunteering ≠ Permission to Infer Poverty

A volunteer may choose time for many reasons.

Photography, staff language and other guests must not turn that choice into a status label.

8. Universal Participation ≠ Universal Ability

Every-person language must survive disability, fatigue, scarcity and scheduling difference.

9. Belonging Through Participation ≠ Belonging Conditional on Participation

Belonging cannot become a reward granted only after contribution.

10. Human Routing ≠ Informal Rule

Human assistance can contain complexity but may introduce inconsistency or discretion.

11. Walk-In First-Class ≠ Immediate Capacity

No-reservation access does not guarantee a meal, task or timely handoff.

12. Volunteer Shift Available ≠ Accessible Shift Available

A task may exist without being suitable for a particular person.

13. Clear Exchange ≠ Comfortable Exchange

Operational clarity does not establish psychological safety.

14. No Fixed Price ≠ No Price

Time, produce, planning and disclosure all carry cost.

15. Work for a Meal ≠ Exploitative Labour

The wording alone does not determine whether the exchange is empowering, neutral or coercive.

16. Volunteer ≠ Employee

Task participation must not blur rights, responsibilities, training or safeguarding.

17. Guest-to-Worker Transition ≠ Loss of Guesthood

Changing roles should not reduce service, welcome or dignity.

18. Same Room ≠ Contribution Neutrality

Spatial sharing does not prevent status inference.

service expectations.

Activity / Outcome Evidence Separation

Meals, shifts, modules and participants are activity evidence.
