Purpose

This file studies reservation as allocation and participation architecture at SAME Café Denver.

It does not assume that reservation means:

selecting a table online

choosing a date and time

receiving a booking confirmation

or paying a deposit.

The principal weekday dining route appears to be:

walk-in.

That does not remove allocation.

It relocates allocation into:

the service window

the queue

the counter

meal availability

seat availability

contribution choice

volunteer-task availability

staff attention

produce acceptance

dietary capacity

and human exception handling.

SAME also operates participation routes that do require advance structure:

pre-scheduled volunteering

group volunteering

court-appointed community-service pre-approval

some Cook to Work scheduling

event participation

catering requests

Restaurant #20 therefore tests whether:

low digital commitment for dining

can coexist with:

high operational clarity and dignified recovery.

The central reservation boundary is:

No Dining Reservation
≠
No Allocation System

Contradiction Findings

Contradiction 1 — No Reservation Still Requires Complex Allocation

Removing the table-booking interface exposes live coordination work.

Contradiction 2 — Low Digital Friction Can Increase Physical Uncertainty

No form or account simplifies entry while shifting risk to travel and queueing.

Contradiction 3 — Three Contribution Routes Can Create More Capacity Dependencies

Choice expands access only when routes remain available.

Contradiction 4 — A Precise Half-Hour Rule Can Hide Total Time Burden

Waiting and orientation may exceed the visible commitment.

Contradiction 5 — Walk-In Volunteering Can Support Agency and Depend on Scarce Work

The route requires useful, safe and supervised tasks.

Contradiction 6 — Produce Can Expand Access and Create Rejection

Food safety may require declining the contribution.

Contradiction 7 — Minimal Disclosure Can Protect Privacy and Weaken Preparation

The café may not know accessibility or dietary needs before arrival.

Contradiction 8 — Human Routing Can Resolve Complexity and Create Inconsistency

Discretion requires governance.

Contradiction 9 — One Guest Can Hold Several Roles Without One Data Purpose

Identity continuity does not justify record merging.

Contradiction 10 — Same-Day Access Can Depend on External Platforms Elsewhere

Ordinary dining is low-tech while scheduled participation may require Point or forms.

Contradiction 11 — First-Come Fairness Can Produce Unequal Burden

Time, transport, disability and work schedules change the cost of waiting.

Contradiction 12 — No Fixed Price Can Require More Decision Support

Contribution choice must be understood and resolved live.

Contradiction 13 — Stable Participation Can Depend on Variable Human Capacity

Rules remain constant while staff and task availability change.

Contradiction 14 — A No-Route Exception Can Preserve Access and Weaken Rule Clarity

Exceptions must be governed without becoming a public test of need.

Contradiction 15 — Capacity Transparency Can Build Confidence and Reveal Scarcity

The system should show service status without displaying who was excluded.

Contradiction 16 — Drop-In Training Can Increase Access and Reduce Predictability

Instructional capacity may fluctuate during café service.

Contradiction 17 — Lunch Coins Can Recognise Progress and Create Entitlement Complexity

Stored future value requires redemption, expiry and loss rules.

Contradiction 18 — Events Can Demonstrate Flexible pricing and Confuse Café Rules

Full-price, volunteer and pay-what-you-can tickets belong to another context.

Contradiction 19 — Commercial Catering Can Support Mission and Distort Public Price Understanding

Estimated catering rates are not café meal prices.

Contradiction 20 — “All May Eat” Can Be True as Purpose and False as Live Capacity

Universal purpose does not create unlimited meals, seats or tasks.
