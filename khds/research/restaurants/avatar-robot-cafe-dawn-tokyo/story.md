Contradiction Findings

Contradiction 1 — The Most Visible Character Is Not the Protagonist

The robot dominates visual attention.

The remote pilot supplies narrative agency.

Contradiction 2 — A Technical Disclaimer Is the Primary Human Story

The non-AI statement is technical in form but human in consequence.

Contradiction 3 — Physical Absence Can Produce Local Protagonism

The pilot is not in the room but can remain the central actor.

Contradiction 4 — A Social Mission Is Most Credible as Paid Hospitality

The narrative gains force when pilots perform valued commercial roles rather than symbolic demonstration alone.

Contradiction 5 — The Machine Becomes Less Important When It Works Narratively

Successful attribution shifts attention from novelty to the person acting through it.

Contradiction 6 — Biography Can Humanise and Instrumentalise at the Same Time

Personal detail creates recognition while risking conversion of adversity into brand evidence.

Contradiction 7 — Permanent Experimentation Creates Both Credibility and Doubt

Repeated testing signals honesty but can weaken confidence if responsibility remains provisional.

Contradiction 8 — A Future Story Depends on Present Operational Detail

The wider proposition becomes credible through schedules, roles, constraints and service acts.

Contradiction 9 — Disability Is Central to the System but Insufficient as the Story

Constraint explains access architecture; personhood and expertise explain hospitality.

Contradiction 10 — The Guest Helps Complete a Worker-Centred Story

Guest recognition is necessary, but the guest should not replace the pilot as moral centre.

Contradiction 11 — A Robot Café Must Repeatedly Explain the Human
