Primary Research Question

How should hospitality create trust, participation and human connection when the person providing service is physically absent but relationally present through a technological body?

Secondary Research Questions

Does human assistance require physical co-presence?

Is OriHime an interface, an employee's embodiment, or both?

Can technological mediation strengthen human hospitality rather than reduce it?

When does visible technology prove capability, and when does it hide the human relationship behind it?

Can photography prove the robot while failing to prove the pilot's hospitality?

Does a dedicated remote pilot constitute structured assistance, experiential human service, or the hospitality product itself?

Can multiple service routes remain understandable without escalating interface complexity?

Is accessibility an accommodation layer, operating infrastructure, employment infrastructure, or the business model itself?

Does asking guests to assist during minor robot difficulties weaken hospitality or create reciprocal participation?

Can an admission fee establish that interaction and atmosphere are part of the purchased product?

When does technology create relational distance, and when does it overcome geographic distance?

Can a visible robot carry an invisible human biography without reducing the pilot to a supporting brand story?

How should the website distinguish remote human operation from autonomous AI?

Can a hospitality system openly acknowledge malfunction risk without reducing confidence?

Does the café prove social participation, or only publish a mechanism intended to support it?

How should KHDS distinguish mediated human presence from automated service?

Identity and Operating Purpose

The official name is BUNSHIN Robot Café DAWN ver.β.

The café describes itself as a permanent experimental café operated by OryLab Inc.

The official site explicitly states that its robots are not AI.

OriHime and OriHime-D are remotely operated by people described as pilots.

The stated operating aim is to create new forms of social participation through technology.

The permanent café was established in 2021 after earlier temporary experiments.

OryLab describes its broader mission as addressing human loneliness.

Pilots

Café staff operating OriHime are called pilots.

The official site reports more than 80 pilots working from Japan and elsewhere.

Pilots may operate from their homes, hospital rooms or other remote locations.

The first BUNSHIN Robot Café DAWN experiment in 2018 involved ten pilots.

The pilot programme is presented as an evolving experiment rather than a completed model.
