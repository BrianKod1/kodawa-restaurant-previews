# Reservation

## Observation

FYN uses a structured, high-commitment reservation model.

Routine reservations are handled through an online booking path.

The reservation is not treated as a casual expression of interest.

To secure the booking, the guest must accept:

- a financial deposit
- defined seating windows
- cancellation conditions
- lateness conditions
- restaurant policies

At the same time, FYN preserves direct human access for situations that fall outside the standard booking path.

The clearest visible example is solo dining.

Solo diners cannot complete the standard online booking flow and are directed to contact FYN through email or WhatsApp instead. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The reservation model therefore appears to distinguish between:

Standard Participation

↓

Structured Self-Service

and

Exception

↓

Direct Human Assistance

This gives KHDS one of its strongest current tests of reservation complexity.

# Evidence

FYN’s current published booking terms state that a deposit is required to secure a reservation.

Current deposit requirements are:

## Lunch

R500 per guest

## Dinner

R1,000 per guest

The deposit is deducted from the guest’s final bill. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The restaurant also publishes a cancellation and change policy.

Changes or cancellations made before the 48-hour threshold may receive a partial or full refund.

Changes, cancellations, or no-shows within 48 hours can result in part or all of the deposit being forfeited.

If fewer guests arrive than were reserved, the deposit associated with the absent guests may also be forfeited. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

FYN publishes a 15-minute seating grace period.

Late arrival may require the guest to wait until the restaurant is ready to seat and serve the table. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Current seating windows are:

## Lunch

Monday–Saturday

12:00–14:00

## Dinner

Monday–Saturday

18:00–20:30. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

The restaurant also publishes several guest conditions:

- children are welcomed from age 13
- smart-casual dress is requested
- mobile phones should be placed on silent
- flash photography is not permitted
- FYN is cashless and accepts credit or debit cards
- online solo-diner bookings are not available
- solo diners are directed to email or WhatsApp. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Direct visible contact channels include:

- email

# Emerging Pattern Stress Test

## Commitment Without Pressure

### Observation

Cancellation timing has financial consequences.

No-shows can result in forfeiture.

The restaurant also defines lateness and other participation expectations. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

### Pattern Effect

Strongly Supported With Major Refinement

### Status

Emerging

### Confidence

High

### Interpretation

FYN provides decisive evidence that:

Commitment Without Pressure

cannot mean:

Low commitment.

Instead, it should distinguish between:

## Legitimate Commitment

A transparent requirement connected to the real operational structure of the experience.

and

## Manipulative Pressure

Artificial urgency or persuasion designed to force action.

A deposit does not automatically violate the pattern.

A cancellation consequence does not automatically violate the pattern.

The key questions become:

- Is the requirement visible?
- Is the consequence understandable?
- Is the guest allowed to decide knowingly?
- Is the rule connected to a legitimate operational need?

### Working Refinement

> Hospitality may require significant commitment while still avoiding manipulative pressure when expectations and consequences are made clear before the guest commits.

Do not change the canonical pattern definition until cross-restaurant reconciliation.

# Current Reservation Insight

FYN gives KHDS its strongest evidence yet that good hospitality is not synonymous with removing every constraint.

The restaurant asks the guest to accept:

- a deposit
- a cancellation window
- a defined seating period
- behavioural expectations

before arrival. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}

That does not automatically make the booking experience inhospitable.

Instead, FYN forces a more precise distinction.

## Harmful Friction

The guest performs unnecessary work or faces:

- ambiguity
- avoidable delay
- confusing steps
- poor feedback
- hidden consequences

## Meaningful Constraint

The guest knowingly accepts a condition that exists to define or protect the experience.

This substantially strengthens `Commitment Without Pressure`.

The pattern should not teach KHDS:

> Ask as little as possible from the guest.

It should increasingly teach:

> **Ask only for commitment the experience genuinely requires, explain it clearly, and never manufacture pressure around it.**

FYN also deepens the case for `Escalating Reservation Complexity`.

The standard path is digital.

A known exception—solo dining—is deliberately moved to human contact. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}

That does not prove FYN has sophisticated backend routing.

But it gives us strong visible evidence for the design principle underneath it:

> **Automation should handle the predictable. Hospitality should know when the situation is no longer predictable.**

That could become extremely important when we eventually return to ReserveAI.

For now, it remains research.

Evidence wins.
