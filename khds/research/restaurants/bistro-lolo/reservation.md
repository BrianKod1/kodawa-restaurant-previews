# Reservation

## Observation

Bistro Lolo supports several ways for a guest to move from interest into a visit.

The current digital experience presents:

- walk-in access
- booking through EatApp
- direct WhatsApp contact
- direct phone contact
- direct email contact

This gives the guest meaningful channel choice.

At the same time, the visible experience does not clearly establish one fully unified reservation journey.

The restaurant therefore appears to operate through a hybrid model:

Spontaneous Visit

↓

Walk In

Planned Routine Visit

↓

EatApp

Direct Contact

↓

WhatsApp / Phone / Email

The current website repeatedly tells guests they may either walk in or book via EatApp, while Bistro Lolo’s direct phone, WhatsApp, and email details remain visible on the contact page. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## Evidence

The official Bistro Lolo page and wider Céline & Lolo site repeatedly use language equivalent to:

- walk in
- book via the EatApp
- book through WhatsApp

The contact page provides:

- Bistro Lolo email
- Bistro Lolo call / WhatsApp number
- a “Book a Table” action

The wider site also repeatedly promotes spontaneous walk-in behaviour alongside formal booking. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Digital Self-Service

and

Direct Human Contact

# Current Reservation Insight

Bistro Lolo gives KHDS an important correction.

Premium hospitality does not require choosing between:

Automation

or

Human Hospitality.

Bistro Lolo visibly supports:

Self-Service Booking

+

Human Contact

+

Walk-In Spontaneity

That makes the reservation model more closely aligned with guest intent than with one fixed technology philosophy.

The most important contradiction concerns Human Confirmation as Hospitality.

Talisman and Mawimbi showed repeated human-confirmation behaviour.

Bistro Lolo shows that a premium, high-character restaurant can preserve human accessibility while still directing ordinary reservations toward a self-service platform. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

This does not invalidate Human Confirmation as Hospitality.

It reveals its likely boundary.

Human involvement may become most valuable when:

- complexity rises
- flexibility is needed
- special requests appear
- the guest wants conversation

For routine bookings, immediate self-service may better preserve confidence.

Bistro Lolo therefore strengthens a more useful emerging reservation question for KHDS:

**What level of hospitality does this guest intent actually require?**

That is more powerful than automatically choosing either automation or human confirmation.
