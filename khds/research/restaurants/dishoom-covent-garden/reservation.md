# Reservation

## Observation

Dishoom Covent Garden does not organise participation around reservation alone.

Its visible reservation system deliberately preserves several legitimate guest routes:

* walk in
* reserve during the day
* reserve as a larger evening group
* use group-booking pathways for larger gatherings
* contact the restaurant directly for sufficiently large or specific event requests

Dishoom therefore treats reservation as one participation tool inside a broader hospitality model.

This is a major contrast with FYN.

## FYN

Participation normally begins with structured advance commitment.

## Dishoom

Participation may begin with either:

Planning

or

Arrival.

The guest does not need a reservation to be considered a valid participant.

---

# Evidence

Dishoom’s current reservation page states that guests may book before 6pm and that after 6pm reservations are available for groups of six or more.

It also explicitly says that most tables are kept for walk-ins and encourages guests to simply come along when their preferred reservation time is unavailable.

Dishoom’s FAQ provides a slightly more precise operational threshold:

* groups of any size may book until 5:45pm
* after that, reservations are accepted for groups of six or more
* reservable tables are available up to four months ahead.

The Covent Garden page describes Dishoom as mostly a walk-in café and states that all guests are welcome without reservations.

Dishoom has a separate group-feasting pathway.

For groups of 10 or more:

* group feasting is available
* card details are requested to secure the table
* no payment is taken at booking
* cancellation within 24 hours or non-arrival may result in a £50 charge.

For parties of 16 or more, or specific event requests, Dishoom directs guests toward direct enquiry.

For groups of 30 or more, Dishoom states that a deposit is required.

This creates a visible escalation from:

Walk-In

↓

Standard Reservation

↓

Group Reservation

↓

Direct Enquiry

↓

Deposit / Higher Commitment

as complexity increases.

# Current Reservation Insight

Dishoom changes the reservation research more than any restaurant since FYN.

FYN taught KHDS:

> Commitment can be hospitable when it is clear, proportionate, and meaningful.

Dishoom adds:

> Commitment itself can change according to operational consequence.

A spontaneous two-person visit may require almost nothing.

A standard daytime reservation requires planning.

A group feast requires a card guarantee.

A very large group requires a deposit.

That is not inconsistent hospitality.

It is differentiated hospitality.

Dishoom therefore gives:

`Escalating Reservation Complexity`

its strongest support so far.

But it simultaneously creates the strongest argument that the name is too narrow.

The actual structure is:

Participation Need

↓

Complexity

↓

Appropriate Route

↓

Appropriate Commitment

↓

Appropriate Human Support

That is broader than reservations.

Dishoom also creates the strongest contradiction yet for:

`Human Confirmation as Hospitality`.

Human involvement remains valuable.

But not because:

human confirmation is intrinsically hospitable.

It becomes valuable when:

judgment

and

coordination

become necessary.

That means the likely deeper abstraction is:

`Human Assistance When Complexity Requires It`.

Finally, Dishoom puts direct pressure on:

`Reservation Throughout the Journey`.

The restaurant keeps most tables for walk-ins and explicitly tells guests they can simply arrive.

So the transferable behaviour increasingly looks like:

> **Keep participation accessible throughout the journey.**

Sometimes that means:

Reserve.

Sometimes:

Walk In.

Sometimes:

Group Book.

Sometimes:

Ask for Help.

We should carry all four possible abstraction changes into the comparison layer together.

They may turn out to be one larger correction to KHDS reservation language rather than four unrelated renames.

For now, no governance change.

Evidence wins.
