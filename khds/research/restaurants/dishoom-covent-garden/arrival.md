# Arrival

## Observation

Dishoom Covent Garden introduces the guest through a combination of:

* Bombay identity
* food
* social hospitality
* daypart flexibility
* walk-in accessibility
* reservation choice

The digital arrival does not appear to treat booking as the sole objective.

Instead, Dishoom establishes a broader invitation:

Come and eat.

Come for breakfast.

Come for chai.

Come for lunch.

Come for dinner.

Come with friends.

Reserve when appropriate.

Or simply arrive.

The arrival therefore feels less like a conversion funnel and more like an invitation into an active café culture.

---

# Evidence

Dishoom describes its cafés as paying homage to the food of Bombay and serving throughout the day from breakfast through dinner and later.

The Covent Garden menu presents the food as drawing from across Bombay:

* cafés
* grills
* street stalls
* homes

and explicitly invites guests to eat through the city across:

* breakfast
* lunch
* afternoon chai
* dinner
* late-night drinks.

Reservation guidance states that:

* bookings are available before the evening threshold
* groups of six or more may reserve later
* most tables are kept for walk-ins
* guests are encouraged simply to come along when a reservation is unavailable.

Covent Garden-specific material also explicitly presents daytime participation as either:

* make a booking
* simply walk in.

The restaurant’s wider digital identity connects the cafés to Bombay and the social heritage of the city’s Irani cafés.

# Current Arrival Insight

Dishoom gives KHDS its strongest challenge yet to the word:

Reservation.

The current pattern:

`Reservation Throughout the Journey`

has survived six completed restaurants because reservation was a meaningful participation mechanism in each.

Dishoom is different.

The restaurant explicitly keeps most tables available for walk-ins and tells guests to come along when reservation inventory is unavailable.

That means the deeper requirement may not be:

> Keep reservation accessible.

It may be:

> **Keep participation accessible.**

Sometimes participation means:

Reserve.

Sometimes:

Walk In.

Sometimes:

Group Book.

That same pressure appears inside:

`Dual-Path Hospitality`

and

`Escalating Reservation Complexity`.

Dishoom may eventually force all three to become more general and more useful.

But we should not make that governance change from `arrival.md`.

We still need:

* atmosphere
* photography
* reservation
* story
* five-dimension comparison

before deciding.

The second major arrival insight is equally important.

FYN showed us that hospitality can legitimately require more commitment.

Dishoom shows us that premium hospitality can also deliberately protect spontaneity.

So KHDS should not optimise restaurants toward:

Maximum Convenience

or

Maximum Commitment.

The better question is:

> **What participation model belongs to this hospitality experience?**

That is the pressure Dishoom brings.

Evidence wins.
