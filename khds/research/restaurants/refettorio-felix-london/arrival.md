Primary Arrival Question

How should an open-door hospitality system create sufficient confidence and dignity when the principal guest does not reserve, pay, qualify or call ahead, but must still navigate time, place, capacity, role and possible support continuation?

Secondary Arrival Questions

What is the minimum information required for referral-free arrival?

Does first-come-first-served access reduce friction or transfer capacity risk to the guest?

Can open-door access remain structured without becoming exclusionary?

How should the website clarify who may attend without requiring someone to publicly classify their vulnerability?

Is arrival complete when the guest reaches the address, enters the hall, receives a meal, or reaches the correct support route?

Can the lunch route remain minimal while volunteer, venue, donor and therapeutic routes become progressively structured?

Does a lack of reservation strengthen Walk-In as First-Class Participation?

Does a lack of capacity visibility weaken arrival confidence?

How should accessibility be prepared when the venue-hire page lists wheelchair accessibility but the lunch page does not provide a complete access statement?

When does human orientation become essential rather than compensatory?

Does the guest need to understand the charity and surplus-food system before arriving?

Can arrival preserve dignity without requiring need-based disclosure?

How should support-service schedules relate to the lunch arrival window?

Can a guest move from an open lunch route into a private or appointment-based service without repeating unnecessary personal information?

Does a venue with multiple stakeholder routes require layered arrival or only role-specific clarity?

Contradiction Findings

Contradiction 1 — Open Access Can Retain Capacity Risk

Formal eligibility may disappear while visit-level uncertainty remains.

Contradiction 2 — Minimal Guest Arrival Can Exist Inside a Complex Organisation

The lunch route can remain simple while other stakeholder routes become structured.

Contradiction 3 — No Reservation Can Still Require Strong Preparation

Time, place, capacity and accessibility remain consequential.

Contradiction 4 — Human Assistance Can Be Intended Arrival Infrastructure

Orientation may carry hospitality rather than repair interface failure.

Contradiction 5 — Arrival Can Continue Beyond the Meal Threshold

Lunch may lead into advice, counselling, activity or therapeutic support.

Contradiction 6 — Accessibility Evidence Can Be Route-Bounded

A venue-hire accessibility statement does not automatically describe lunch arrival.

Contradiction 7 — No Qualification Can Retain Felt-Eligibility Friction

A person may remain unsure whether the service is intended for them.

Contradiction 8 — Role Breadth Can Falsify Multi-Intent Overreach

Many arrival routes may reflect stakeholder architecture rather than guest intent.

Contradiction 9 — Published Capacity Can Coexist With Admission Uncertainty

Aggregate capability does not reveal real-time access.

Contradiction 10 — Arrival Clarity Can Preserve Privacy

The system can state what happens without demanding why the person needs it.
