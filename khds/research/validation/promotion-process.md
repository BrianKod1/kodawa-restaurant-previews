# KHDS Pattern Promotion Process

## Purpose

This document defines how hospitality observations become formal KHDS knowledge.

The objective is to prevent:

- assumptions becoming principles
- repeated design preferences being mistaken for evidence
- Kodawa Labs concepts validating themselves
- patterns being promoted too early
- business opportunities being confused with universal hospitality behaviours

Every promotion must be supported by traceable evidence.

---

# Evidence Types

KHDS recognises several different forms of evidence.

They must not be treated as interchangeable.

---

## 1. Independent Hospitality Evidence

Evidence gathered from restaurants not designed by Kodawa Labs.

Examples:

- Ankole
- Cultiva
- Talisman
- future independent restaurant studies

Independent evidence is the primary basis for pattern maturity.

---

## 2. Kodawa Labs Validation

Evidence gathered from Kodawa Labs concepts or production work.

Examples:

- Trunk & Tandoor Concept

Purpose:

Determine whether KHDS knowledge can be translated successfully into a digital hospitality experience.

Kodawa Labs validation does not increase independent restaurant evidence counts.

---

## 3. Guest Behaviour Evidence

Future evidence may include:

- analytics
- heatmaps
- booking completion
- CTA conversion
- bounce rate
- time on page
- scroll depth
- usability testing
- direct guest feedback

Guest behaviour can strengthen or challenge research conclusions.

---

## 4. Operational Evidence

Some patterns cannot be validated through interface observation alone.

Examples include:

- guest-data ownership
- CRM ownership
- booking-system control
- payment ownership
- follow-up communication
- operational workload
- reservation efficiency

These require technical, operational, or restaurant-provided evidence.

---

# Stage 1 — Observed

## Definition

A behaviour, tension, or opportunity has been noticed in research.

It has not yet earned formal Candidate Pattern status.

Examples:

- Food as Sensory Entry Point
- Product as Entry Point
- Reservation as Confidence Signal
- Place as Emotional Evidence

## Requirements

An observation should include:

- what was observed
- where it was observed
- evidence supporting it
- interpretation
- confidence

## Promotion Question

Does this behaviour appear meaningful and transferable enough to test across additional restaurants?

If yes:

Observed

↓

Candidate

---

# Stage 2 — Candidate

## Definition

A behaviour appears potentially transferable but currently lacks sufficient independent evidence.

## Typical Evidence

A Candidate Pattern may have:

- strong evidence from one independent restaurant
- partial evidence from additional restaurants
- a clear explanation of why the behaviour may matter

## Requirements

A Candidate Pattern must include:

- definition
- primary evidence
- partial evidence where available
- confidence
- validation targets
- known limitations

## Current Examples

- Promise Before Proof
- One Idea Can Organise the Brand Story
- Atmosphere Through Worldview
- Show the System Behind the Plate
- The Guest as Participant
- Layered Arrival
- Heritage Builds Confidence
- Growth as Narrative
- Photography Through Layers of Experience
- Atmosphere Through Continuity
- Human Confirmation as Hospitality

## Promotion Question

Has the pattern appeared independently across meaningfully different restaurant experiences?

If yes:

Candidate

↓

Emerging

---

# Stage 3 — Emerging

## Definition

A pattern has repeated across multiple independent restaurant experiences and appears transferable.

Emerging does not mean proven.

It means the evidence is becoming meaningful.

## Typical Evidence

An Emerging Pattern should normally have:

- evidence across at least two independent restaurants
- stronger confidence when supported by three or more
- evidence across different brand identities or approaches
- no major contradiction that invalidates the behaviour

## Current Examples

- Emotion Before Interface
- Story Before Features
- Photography Before Decoration
- Reservation Throughout the Journey
- Atmosphere Through Consistency
- Dual-Path Hospitality
- Commitment Without Pressure
- Photography as Proof
- Story Through Place

## Important Rule

Three supporting restaurants do not automatically create a Validated Pattern.

The quality and diversity of the evidence matter more than the raw count.

## Promotion Question

Has the pattern survived broader independent research, meaningful variation, and contradiction testing?

If yes:

Emerging

↓

Validated

---

# Stage 4 — Validated

## Definition

A pattern has survived broad independent testing and can reasonably be treated as durable KHDS hospitality knowledge.

## Minimum Expectation

Validation should normally consider approximately:

5–10 or more independent restaurant studies

However, restaurant count alone is not sufficient.

Validation should also consider:

- restaurant-category diversity
- geographic diversity
- brand diversity
- different guest journeys
- different reservation models
- contradictory evidence
- implementation viability
- guest behaviour where available

## Validation Questions

Before promotion, ask:

### Transferability

Does the pattern survive across different restaurant identities?

### Necessity

Is this genuinely useful, or merely common?

### Contradiction

Have credible examples challenged the pattern?

### Flexibility

Can the pattern appear in different forms without losing its underlying value?

### Business Value

Does the pattern improve:

- guest confidence
- conversion
- experience
- brand expression
- operational effectiveness

### Guest Evidence

Where possible, does actual guest behaviour support the conclusion?

---

# Stage 5 — KHDS Principle

## Definition

A Validated Pattern becomes a KHDS Principle when it is sufficiently important and transferable to guide future hospitality design decisions.

Not every Validated Pattern must become a Principle.

Some may remain:

- specialised patterns
- component guidance
- reservation models
- storytelling behaviours

## Principle Requirements

A KHDS Principle should include:

- definition
- evidence base
- design implications
- implementation guidance
- anti-patterns
- supported components
- evolution history

## Promotion Question

Should this knowledge influence decisions across a wide range of future KHDS implementations?

If yes:

Validated Pattern

↓

KHDS Principle

---

# Stage 6 — Production Experience

Validated KHDS knowledge should eventually influence:

- components
- experiences
- templates
- reservation systems
- storytelling systems
- mobile journeys
- Kodawa Labs client work

The implementation should always preserve restaurant identity.

KHDS defines the intelligence.

The restaurant defines the expression.

---

# Partial Support

Some findings cannot progress normally through the lifecycle because critical evidence is unavailable.

Example:

## Own the Guest Relationship

Website observation can show:

- direct booking entry points
- restaurant-branded communication
- contact methods

It cannot prove:

- guest-data ownership
- CRM ownership
- commercial rights
- payment ownership
- third-party dependencies

Such findings should be labelled:

Partially Supported

until the required operational evidence exists.

---

# Existing Implementation Patterns

Some KHDS patterns were created before the current research methodology was formalised.

Example:

- Invitation

These patterns should not be deleted.

They should be classified as:

Implementation Pattern — Pending Formal Validation

The research process may eventually:

- validate them
- revise them
- split them
- merge them with another pattern
- reject them

Existing implementation does not equal independent evidence.

---

# Validation Studies

Kodawa Labs concepts may have dedicated validation studies.

Example:

`khds/research/validation/trunk-tandoor/arrival-pattern-study.md`

These studies answer questions such as:

- Did KHDS work in implementation?
- Does the sequence feel coherent?
- Is the pattern reusable?
- Does implementation reveal a weakness in the research?

They must never be counted as independent hospitality evidence.

---

# Research Tensions

A tension is not a pattern.

Current examples include:

- Richness Versus Restraint
- Implicit Story Versus Explicit Story
- Curation Versus Completeness
- Personal Service Versus Immediate Certainty
- Direct Relationship Versus Third-Party Convenience

Tensions should remain open questions until research reveals a transferable recommendation.

---

# Opportunities and Gaps

Business gaps should remain separate from pattern maturity.

An opportunity may become:

Observed

↓

Potential Opportunity

↓

Validated Business Gap

↓

Kodawa Labs Solution Opportunity

↓

Prototype

↓

Client Validation

↓

Production Offering

A profitable opportunity does not automatically become a KHDS hospitality principle.

Similarly, a strong hospitality pattern does not automatically represent a commercial opportunity.

---

# Promotion Review Checklist

Before changing the status of any pattern, confirm:

□ Independent evidence has been reviewed

□ Kodawa Labs validation has been separated from independent evidence

□ Partial evidence is clearly labelled

□ Contradictory examples have been considered

□ Confidence has been updated

□ Restaurant diversity has been considered

□ Research limitations are documented

□ Supporting behaviour has not been mistaken for a pattern

□ Business opportunity has not been mistaken for a pattern

□ Technical claims are supported by technical evidence

□ Candidate Patterns file has been updated

□ Pattern Registry has been updated

□ Validation Dashboard has been updated

---

# Promotion Authority

Pattern promotion should only occur after reviewing:

1. restaurant research dossiers
2. cross-restaurant comparisons
3. knowledge extraction
4. candidate-patterns.md
5. registry.md
6. validation dashboard
7. contradictory evidence

No individual restaurant study should automatically promote a pattern by itself.

# Next Validation Phase

Continue Sprint 5 research until KHDS has examined a broader range of restaurant experiences.

Priority research should include:

- heritage-led restaurants
- chef-led restaurants
- farm-to-table restaurants
- high-volume restaurants
- fine dining
- destination hospitality
- international premium restaurants
- restaurants using different reservation technologies

The objective is not to prove current KHDS thinking correct.

The objective is to discover where it is correct, where it fails, and where it must evolve.

---

# Core Rule

Evidence before promotion.

A pattern earns its status through repeated independent observation, contradiction testing, and practical value.

KHDS should become more confident only when the evidence deserves it.
