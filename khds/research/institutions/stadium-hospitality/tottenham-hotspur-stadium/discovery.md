# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How clearly does Tottenham Hotspur Stadium distinguish event type, ordinary ticket access, Premium hospitality, seasonal relationships, hospitality products and event-specific operating rules before commitment?

## Discovery object

Tottenham Hotspur Stadium is not one product.

A prospective guest may discover:

- football match attendance;
- ordinary home tickets;
- One Hotspur membership;
- Ticket Exchange inventory;
- Premium matchday hospitality;
- seasonal Premium membership;
- concerts and other events;
- official reseller packages. [S1][S2][S7][S17]

Stadium Visit != One Purchase Object.

## Multi-event institution

The stadium supports more than football.

Official material identifies:

- football;
- NFL;
- boxing;
- rugby;
- concerts;
- visitor attractions;
- conferences and events. [S1]

A visitor must determine not only where the event occurs, but what operating model applies to that event.

Same Venue != Same Guest Journey.

## General Admission and Premium discovery

Ordinary ticketing and Premium hospitality are presented through materially different product structures. [S2][S4][S17]

General Admission discovery can involve:

- membership priority;
- ticket-sale windows;
- seat availability;
- Ticket Exchange.

Premium discovery can involve:

- seat location;
- lounge or suite;
- included dining;
- private or semi-private space;
- guest count;
- pre/post-event access;
- seasonal relationship.

Event Attendance != Uniform Product Comparison.

## Seasonal relationship

Seasonal Premium membership is not merely repeated purchase of individual match tickets.

Published products can include:

- recurring home-match access;
- hospitality access;
- account management;
- transferability;
- Ticket Exchange;
- event priority;
- additional privileges. [S3][S5][S6]

Recurring Access != Repeated Single Tickets.

The current evidence does not establish how clearly this difference is understood by first-time purchasers.

These can differ in:

- privacy;
- seat;
- lounge relationship;
- dining;
- drinks;
- guest capacity;
- account service;
- parking;
- access time.

Premium != One Hospitality Tier.

## Official reseller pathway

The club maintains an official reseller route. [S7]

The same underlying event may therefore be discovered through:

- direct club inventory;
- membership-linked inventory;
- exchange inventory;
- official reseller inventory.

Some reseller products may add accommodation.

Same Event != Same Acquisition Relationship.

The current evidence does not establish differences in:

- margin;
- commission;
- customer ownership;
- refund ownership;
- complaint ownership;
- data sharing;
- support quality.

## Ticket Exchange

Ticket Exchange creates another inventory state.

Eligible season-ticket or Premium relationships may release designated seats for resale under club rules. [S17]

Seat Available != Original Inventory State.

## Event-specific rules

A significant discovery risk is assuming stadium-wide uniformity.

The official bag policy explicitly states that football policy may differ for other stadium events. [S9]

The Disability Access Scheme also has football-specific boundaries. [S13]

Venue Information != Universally Applicable Event Information.

## Commercial Journey Economics lens

A useful research sequence is:

Discovery source
→ entitlement type
→ purchase channel
→ event attendance
→ ancillary spend
→ retention
→ economic contribution

The public evidence establishes the existence of different pathways.

It does not establish their comparative economics.

Different Channel != Different Proven Net Contribution.

No profitability conclusion should be drawn.

## First-time guest burden

A first-time guest may need to answer:

- Which event?
- Is membership required or advantageous?
- Is ordinary admission sufficient?
- Is Premium hospitality desired?
- Is the product one-off or seasonal?
- Is food included?
- Is the lounge private, shared or absent?
- How long does hospitality remain open?
- Is transfer or resale allowed?
- Which event-specific policies apply?

That burden may be reasonable.

The current research does not establish whether the digital experience resolves it efficiently.

## Competing explanation

This complexity may still be ordinary event-commerce segmentation.

Large venues normally sell:

- different ticket categories;
- hospitality;
- memberships;
- resale inventory;
- event-specific products.

That remains a sufficient explanation.

Case #39 becomes architecturally stronger only if those distinctions materially affect:

- responsibility;
- information;
- permissions;
- recovery;
- capacity;
- relationship persistence.
