# Stay / Participation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do seat entitlement, hospitality-space access, internal circulation, accessibility, payment, service timing and event-specific rules coexist during participation inside Tottenham Hotspur Stadium?

## Interpretation

Tottenham Hotspur Stadium is not an overnight institution.

For Case #39, the required KHDS stay dimension is interpreted as:

event participation after admission and before departure.

## Participation contains multiple simultaneous states

A guest may simultaneously hold:

- event admission;
- seat entitlement;
- hospitality-space entitlement;
- dining entitlement;
- payment capability;
- accessibility entitlement;
- time-bounded access. [S3][S4][S5][S6][S10][S13]

Therefore:

Inside Venue != One Participation State.

## Seat entitlement

The seat is an important participation object.

But for Premium guests, the seat may be only one part of the product. [S3][S4][S5][S6]

A guest may also have:

- lounge access;
- suite access;
- included dining;
- drinks;
- account support;
- extended access time.

Therefore:

Premium Seat != Seat Only.

## Hospitality-space entitlement

Premium hospitality can involve:

- shared lounges;
- private suites;
- semi-private spaces;
- members-club style environments. [S3][S4][S5][S6]

These spaces are not interchangeable.

Therefore:

Premium Access != Universal Premium Access.

The public evidence does not establish whether one Premium credential can enter multiple hospitality spaces.

## Time-bounded participation

Premium access can begin before the sporting event and continue after full-time. [S4][S5][S6]

This means the guest relationship changes over time.

Before kick-off:
hospitality may be active.

During play:
seat entitlement becomes central.

After full-time:
some hospitality may remain active.

Therefore:

One Event
!=
One Uniform Service State.

## Internal circulation

The stadium contains multiple levels and controlled routes. [S10]

Guests may depend on:

- concourse access;
- stairs;
- lifts;
- designated accessible routes;
- staff guidance.

Access to the venue does not mean every circulation option is available to every guest.

Therefore:

Physical Proximity != Operational Access.

## Accessibility during participation

The Disability Access Scheme and accessible facilities create role-specific participation states. [S12][S13]

Eligible guests may require:

- accessible viewing;
- personal assistance;
- lift access;
- other supported routes.

The public evidence does not establish actual availability or service success.

Therefore:

Accessibility Entitlement != Accessibility Fulfilment Proven.

## Cashless operating condition

The stadium operates cashlessly for purchases inside the venue. [S10]

This creates another participation dependency.

A guest may have valid admission but be unable to transact using cash alone.

Therefore:

Admitted Guest != Transaction-Ready Guest.

The current evidence does not establish recovery options for payment failure.

## Multiple relationship roles

Within one event, a person can potentially be:

- ticket holder;
- member;
- Premium guest;
- suite guest;
- accessibility-scheme user;
- final attendee after transfer;
- purchaser;
- guest of purchaser.

These roles may not be identical.

Therefore:

One Person
!=
One Operational Role.

This distinction is important for future system design.

## Service coordination

Potential service owners during participation include:

- event operations;
- Premium hospitality;
- security;
- catering;
- accessibility staff;
- ticketing support;
- customer service.

The current evidence does not establish whether these teams share a unified guest state.

Therefore:

Shared Venue
!=
Shared Operational Context Automatically.

## Commercial Journey Economics lens

Premium participation can involve materially more service than ordinary admission.

Potential service inputs include:

- dedicated hospitality space;
- dining;
- drinks;
- account management;
- staff attention;
- longer occupancy period.

But:

More Service != Lower Proven Margin.

Higher Price != Higher Proven Margin.

No comparative profitability should be inferred.

## Failure states

Potential participation failures include:

- wrong seat;
- denied lounge access;
- suite mismatch;
- inaccessible circulation;
- lift unavailable;
- payment failure;
- hospitality service unavailable;
- guest entitlement not recognised;
- lost digital credential after entry;
- crowd-routing problem.

The current public evidence does not establish complete recovery ownership.

## Competing explanation

These distinctions may simply reflect normal large-venue zoning and service segmentation.

That remains a sufficient explanation.

The stronger KHDS interpretation requires evidence that these roles and states materially affect information continuity, service ownership and recovery.
