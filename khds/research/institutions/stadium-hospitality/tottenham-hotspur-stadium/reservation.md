# Reservation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What exactly becomes committed when a guest purchases a match ticket, Premium hospitality package, seasonal Premium relationship or resale/transfer-based entitlement?

## Reservation is entitlement creation

For Case #39, reservation should not be understood as one generic booking.

Potential commitment objects include:

- Match Ticket;
- membership;
- Premium Package;
- seasonal Premium membership;
- Ticket Exchange seat;
- transferred ticket;
- official reseller product. [S3][S5][S6][S7][S16][S17]

One Purchase != One Entitlement Type.

## Match Ticket

Current ticketing terms distinguish a Match Ticket from membership and Premium Package relationships. [S16]

The Match Ticket can represent:

- event;
- date;
- seat;
- entrance relationship;
- digital credential.

It does not automatically establish access to every hospitality environment.

Match Ticket != Premium Hospitality Entitlement.

## Premium Package

Premium products can combine multiple commitments.

Depending on package, these may include:

- event admission;
- specified seating;
- lounge access;
- suite access;
- dining;
- drinks;
- private or semi-private environment;
- defined pre-match access;
- defined post-match access. [S3][S4][S5][S6]

Premium Package != Seat Upgrade Only.

## Seasonal Premium relationship

Seasonal products can persist beyond one event.

Published entitlements may include:

- recurring home-match access;
- account management;
- ticket transferability;
- Ticket Exchange;
- event priority;
- hospitality space;
- dining;
- parking. [S5][S6]

Seasonal Relationship != Collection of Unrelated Match Tickets.

The evidence does not establish the internal system that maintains these entitlements.

## Time entitlement

Premium products can include time as part of the product.

Examples include hospitality access before kick-off and after full-time. [S4][S5][S6]

Event Ticket Time != Hospitality Access Time.

## Seat versus hospitality space

A guest can hold at least two different spatial commitments:

1. event-viewing seat;
2. hospitality space.

These may be physically separate, governed by different access rules and active at different times.

Seat Assignment != Hospitality Space Assignment.

## Guest count and capacity

Some Premium products are sold:

- per person;
- by booth;
- by loge;
- by suite;
- for specified guest counts. [S4]

Venue Capacity != Hospitality Product Capacity.

The public evidence does not establish how these capacities are coupled internally.

## Ticket transferability

Some Premium relationships permit transfer of tickets. [S5]

Original Purchaser != Necessarily Final Attendee.

The current evidence does not establish the complete identity, consent or data-handling flow during transfer.

## Ticket Exchange

Ticket Exchange allows eligible seats to re-enter market inventory under club rules. [S17]

Purchased Seat can later become Resale Inventory.

This creates different roles:

- original entitlement holder;
- club;
- resale purchaser;
- final attendee.

The precise financial and responsibility relationships remain unverified.

## Official reseller relationship

Official resellers create another commitment pathway. [S7]

Some products may combine:

- ticket;
- Premium hospitality;
- hotel.

This raises questions about:

- merchant relationship;
- confirmation ownership;
- refund ownership;
- accommodation responsibility;
- customer support;
- event-change recovery.

The current evidence establishes the authorised route.

It does not establish the complete operational contract chain.

## Digital-ticket state

Current terms normally require individual digital tickets on individual smartphones, subject to stated exceptions. [S16]

Purchase Confirmed != Entry Credential Ready.

This distinction becomes important for pre-arrival and arrival recovery.

## Identity validation

The current terms permit identity validation in specified circumstances. [S16]

Ticket Possession != Absolute Identity Independence.

The current evidence does not establish when or how frequently identity checks occur.

- direct ticket;

A future commercial question is:

The public evidence does not answer that question.

Higher Price != Higher Proven Contribution Margin.

## Failure states

Potential commitment failures include:

- payment failure;
- wrong ticket type;
- wrong event;
- wrong seat;
- missing Premium entitlement;
- duplicate identity;
- failed transfer;
- failed exchange listing;
- digital ticket not delivered;
- reseller confirmation mismatch;
- event cancellation or rescheduling.

The current public source set does not establish complete recovery for each.

## Competing explanation

That remains a sufficient explanation.

The stronger KHDS interpretation requires evidence that these roles materially alter:

- access;
- responsibility;
- recovery;
- information;
- capacity;
- service ownership.
