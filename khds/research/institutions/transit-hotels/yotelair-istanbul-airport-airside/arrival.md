# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How are airport eligibility, wayfinding, boarding-pass verification and accommodation check-in combined at physical arrival?

## Evidence

YOTELAIR Airside is located inside Istanbul Airport's secure international transit environment. [S1][S2]

Physical arrival at the hotel therefore occurs only after the traveller satisfies the airport-side conditions necessary to reach that part of the terminal.

A valid boarding pass for an international flight is presented by the operator as an access requirement. [S1][S2]

Sleep Cabin users operate under the same broad airside constraint. [S3][S4]

The public material therefore separates at least two layers of arrival:

- eligibility to reach the airside environment;
- check-in or admission to the booked hospitality product.

The Landside sister hotel creates a contrasting arrival model. It is positioned before security and passport control and can serve travellers who do not satisfy or require the same airside state. [S5]

This means two properties under the same wider operator identity can require materially different physical arrival conditions.

## Access boundary

The strongest bounded arrival distinction is:

Reservation entitlement != physical airport access.

A traveller may hold a valid YOTELAIR booking while still lacking the airport conditions required to reach the property.

Airside eligibility != Landside eligibility.

Hotel identity therefore does not remove the external airport-access boundary.

## Interpretation

The arrival evidence is compatible with role partition because different users may reach the broader YOTEL airport system in materially different states:

- international airside travellers;
- hourly-stay guests;
- overnight guests;
- Sleep Cabin users;
- travellers who instead require the Landside property.

However, the current evidence does not establish that frontline operations treat hourly guests, overnight guests and Sleep Cabin users as distinct operational roles once they reach the property.

A simpler interpretation remains possible:

airport eligibility may be an external location constraint layered onto an otherwise conventional hotel check-in process.

Case #33 therefore must not treat secure-area access alone as proof of a multi-relationship architecture.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish:

- exact physical wayfinding from gates or transfer routes;
- how boarding-pass eligibility is verified at the hotel;
- handling when a guest arrives through the wrong side of the terminal;
- handling for guests who need access to checked baggage;
- frontline exception rules for changed or cancelled flights;
- whether hourly, overnight and Sleep Cabin guests use identical check-in processes.

No on-site arrival observation or transaction test was performed.

## Next evidence

If permitted in later research, separately test how hourly, overnight and Sleep Cabin arrivals are processed and how staff handle passengers whose airport status conflicts with their accommodation booking.
