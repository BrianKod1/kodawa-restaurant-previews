# Dining

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How are food-and-beverage services distributed across accommodation and lounge relationships, and what is included versus separately purchased?

## Evidence

Food and drink appear within more than one part of the wider YOTELAIR Airside proposition.

S1 presents food-and-drink service through Revolve Lounge. [S1]

Revolve Lounge access can be purchased separately from accommodation, so dining availability cannot automatically be treated as a consequence of holding a room reservation. [S1]

Sleep Cabin material also references a Grab+Go offer. [S3]

This means the public proposition exposes at least two food-and-beverage touchpoints:

- lounge-associated food and drink;
- Grab+Go associated with the Sleep Cabin environment.

General YOTEL terms further distinguish accommodation from complementary or ancillary services that may carry separate conditions or charges. [S6]

## Entitlement boundary

The inspected evidence supports a bounded distinction between:

- a food-and-beverage service being present;
- a guest holding an accommodation booking;
- a user holding lounge access;
- the exact dining entitlement attached to either relationship.

The strongest bounded observation is:

Service presence != universal inclusion.

Accommodation booking != proof of dining entitlement.

Lounge access != proof that every food-and-beverage item is included.

## Interpretation

Dining is relevant to Case #33 because the wider institution can contain people with different primary relationships:

- standard-room guests;
- hourly-stay guests;
- Sleep Cabin users;
- lounge-only users.

The current evidence does not establish whether those groups receive identical food-and-beverage rights.

That uncertainty is important because different dining entitlement would strengthen the argument that service rights are role-dependent rather than universally attached to physical presence.

## Competing explanation

The simpler explanation remains strong:

Revolve Lounge and Grab+Go may simply be ordinary hotel food-and-beverage products available under standard commercial conditions.

Nothing in the current source set proves that dining is operationally partitioned by guest role.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish:

- the current menu;
- operating hours for all food-and-beverage services;
- exact Revolve Lounge inclusions;
- exact Grab+Go conditions;
- dietary accommodation procedures;
- allergen handling;
- whether standard-room guests receive included lounge dining;
- whether Sleep Cabin users receive different dining rights;
- whether lounge-only visitors receive the same items as accommodation guests.

No meal-service observation or purchase test was performed.

## Next evidence

Inspect current menus, operating periods, Revolve Lounge package conditions, Grab+Go terms and explicit food-and-beverage inclusions.

Determine whether room guests, Sleep Cabin users and lounge-only visitors receive materially different dining entitlements.
