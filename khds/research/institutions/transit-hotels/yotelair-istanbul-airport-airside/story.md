# Story

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Does YOTELAIR Airside demonstrate a genuinely role-partitioned, multi-relationship hospitality architecture, or is it better explained as one conventional airport hotel selling several durations and ancillary products?

## Evidence

The top-level institutional story is simple:

YOTEL provides hospitality inside Istanbul Airport through an Airside hotel proposition for eligible international travellers. [S1][S2]

Under that shared operator identity, however, the inspected sources expose several distinguishable relationships.

A traveller may book conventional overnight accommodation. [S1][S2]

A traveller may use hourly accommodation. [S1][S2]

A traveller may book a single-guest Sleep Cabin with shared supporting facilities. [S2][S3][S4]

A user may purchase Revolve Lounge access separately rather than entering only through an accommodation relationship. [S1]

A traveller whose airport state does not fit the Airside property may instead require the separate Landside sister hotel. [S2][S5]

General YOTEL terms also distinguish direct and authorised-OTA reservations, group bookings and ancillary facilities that may carry separate conditions or charges. [S6]

## Institutional narrative

The current public story can therefore be represented as:

One airport-hospitality operator -> several ways to stay, use shared hospitality services and qualify for physical access.

This does not prove several independent institutions.

It shows that the broader operator environment supports relationships whose eligibility, duration, facility use and commercial conditions are not automatically interchangeable.

Shared operator identity != identical customer relationship.

Accommodation booking != universal ancillary-service entitlement.

Airside eligibility != accommodation preference.

Hotel reservation != guaranteed physical reachability.

Physical co-location != uniform service access.

## Falsification pressure

Case #33 follows Case #32 by keeping the research inside airport hospitality while reintroducing lodging.

The case therefore tests whether the provisional Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture still appears when the institution contains:

- private accommodation;
- different stay durations;
- a distinct Sleep Cabin product;
- shared supporting facilities;
- separately purchasable lounge use;
- airport-controlled eligibility;
- a related Landside property with different access conditions.

Current evidence is directionally compatible with the broader architecture because relationships can differ in access prerequisites, duration, accommodation format, facility entitlement and booking conditions.

However, those differences are not enough by themselves to establish the architecture.

## Competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation remains:

YOTELAIR Airside is one conventional airport hotel offering several accommodation formats, lengths of stay and optional ancillary products.

Under that explanation, hourly stays, overnight stays, Sleep Cabins and lounge access are simply product segmentation inside an ordinary hotel business.

That explanation remains viable.

For Case #33 to provide stronger architectural support, the research would need to show that the relationships materially change operational entitlement, capacity allocation, service recovery, responsibility or system ownership rather than merely changing room format, duration or price.

## Source conflicts

The public institutional story contains unresolved internal inconsistencies.

S2 describes Sleep Cabins as bookable by the hour for a maximum of four hours. [S2]

S3 describes Sleep Cabins as bookable by the hour with a minimum of four hours. [S3]

The dossier preserves this conflict.

S1 also contains inconsistent Revolve Lounge duration language: one section describes the EUR 80 proposition as four hours while another advertises 24-hour lounge access from EUR 80. [S1]

The dossier does not silently choose between those statements.

One operator identity != one internally consistent public information state.

## Limits

The evidence does not establish:

- backend reservation architecture;
- whether room, Sleep Cabin and lounge inventory share one capacity system;
- internal staffing allocation;
- revenue allocation;
- customer-database integration;
- frontline responsibility boundaries;
- service-recovery ownership;
- how incorrect Airside bookings are operationally recovered;
- representative guest understanding;
- representative guest outcomes;
- financial performance;
- that other YOTELAIR properties use the same operating structure.
