# Departure

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does each lodging relationship end, and what happens when booked duration and the traveller's onward flight no longer align?

## Evidence

Case #33 contains more than one expected endpoint to the accommodation relationship.

Hourly accommodation is explicitly distinguished from conventional overnight accommodation. [S1][S2]

Sleep Cabins are also presented as a time-bounded accommodation product, although the inspected public sources disagree about whether four hours represents a maximum or minimum duration. [S2][S3]

The Sleep Cabin booking flow publishes a cancellation rule providing a full refund when cancellation occurs before 15:00 on the day before arrival. [S4]

General YOTEL terms state that reservation conditions can vary by selected rate and property-specific Special Conditions. [S6]

These facts show that the end of the commercial relationship cannot be inferred from one universal rule.

## Airport-journey boundary

Departure from YOTELAIR Airside remains embedded in the traveller's international-airport journey.

The property exists within the secure international airside environment and requires an international-flight boarding pass for access. [S1][S2]

The accommodation relationship can therefore end while the traveller's broader airport journey continues.

Hotel-stay duration != airport-journey duration.

Accommodation checkout != completion of passenger journey.

This is materially different from treating the hotel as an isolated destination.

## Interpretation

Case #33 may contain several distinct departure triggers:

- expiry of an hourly accommodation period;
- completion of an overnight stay;
- expiry of a Sleep Cabin period;
- voluntary departure;
- progression toward an onward international flight.

The evidence does not establish that these boundaries are governed by identical procedures.

This is compatible with the broader multi-relationship hypothesis, but does not prove it.

A conventional hotel may also offer different checkout periods and rate rules without requiring a separate architecture.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish:

- hourly-stay extension procedures;
- overstay handling;
- conversion from hourly to overnight accommodation;
- re-entry rules after leaving the hotel area;
- treatment of delayed flights;
- treatment of cancelled or missed flights;
- what happens when a flight disruption extends beyond the booked accommodation period;
- whether unused accommodation time is recoverable;
- how departure disputes are escalated.

The Sleep Cabin maximum-versus-minimum four-hour conflict remains unresolved.

## Next evidence

Determine whether the end of each accommodation relationship is managed differently when the traveller's onward airport journey changes.
