# Photography

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Does official visual material clarify accommodation types, shared-space differences and airport-access boundaries, or primarily promote the property?

## Current evidence position

The inspected textual sources identify several visual questions that should be tested.

The source material distinguishes standard hotel accommodation from Sleep Cabins. [S1][S2][S3][S4]

Sleep Cabins are described as single-guest accommodation using shared shower and bathroom facilities. [S2][S3][S4]

The public proposition also identifies Revolve Lounge as a separate shared hospitality setting. [S1]

Those textual findings create visual questions concerning:

- relative scale of standard rooms and Sleep Cabins;
- private versus shared facilities;
- Revolve Lounge configuration;
- practical wayfinding;
- the relationship between hotel accommodation and terminal space;
- whether Airside access restrictions are visually explained;
- whether the separate product relationships are visibly distinguishable.

## Evidence limitation

Case #33 has not completed a systematic image-by-image inspection of official YOTEL photography.

Accordingly, this dossier does not claim that published images prove any particular room scale, layout, access boundary, privacy condition, crowd level or service-entitlement distinction.

Textual evidence about accommodation types and shared facilities is not converted into a photographic finding without direct visual inspection.

The appropriate boundaries are:

Visual availability != completed visual evidence.

Promotional image != representative operating condition.

Spatial description != verified photographic explanation.

## Interpretation

Photography is potentially important because YOTELAIR can be presented visually as one coherent airport-hospitality brand while containing materially different accommodation and service relationships.

Official imagery could either clarify those distinctions or visually flatten them into one seamless proposition.

That remains a research question, not a finding.

No conclusion is drawn about whether YOTEL photography explains, obscures or exaggerates operational differences.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish:

- the complete official image set;
- image dates;
- whether images represent the current configuration;
- desktop versus mobile image ordering;
- actual visual size differences between standard rooms and Sleep Cabins;
- whether shared shower and bathroom facilities are clearly depicted;
- whether Revolve Lounge access boundaries are shown;
- whether terminal wayfinding is visually explained;
- whether ordinary crowded or disrupted conditions are represented.
