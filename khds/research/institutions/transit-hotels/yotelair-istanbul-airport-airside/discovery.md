# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How is YOTELAIR Airside presented to travellers, and how clearly are its different accommodation, lounge and airport-access relationships exposed before commitment?

## Evidence

YOTEL publicly presents more than one airport-hospitality relationship around Istanbul Airport.

The operator distinguishes YOTELAIR Airside from YOTEL Landside as separate properties with materially different access conditions. The Airside hotel is inside the international secure area and requires a valid boarding pass for an international flight. The Landside property is before security and passport control and does not depend on the same airside eligibility state. [S1][S2][S5]

Within the Airside proposition, the public material also distinguishes more than one accommodation relationship.

The operator presents hourly accommodation alongside conventional overnight stays. [S1][S2]

Sleep Cabins are separately presented as a single-guest short-stay product rather than merely another standard room category. Their public description includes shared shower and bathroom facilities. [S2][S3][S4]

The wider Airside environment also includes Revolve Lounge access that can be purchased separately rather than being reducible to a room booking alone. [S1]

General YOTEL terms further distinguish accommodation from complementary or ancillary services that may carry separate conditions or charges. [S6]

## Interpretation

The public discovery layer therefore exposes several distinctions before operational execution is considered.

At minimum, it distinguishes:

- Airside versus Landside hotel access;
- hourly accommodation;
- overnight accommodation;
- standalone Sleep Cabins;
- standard hotel accommodation;
- separately purchasable lounge use;
- ancillary services that may not be included in the accommodation relationship.

The strongest bounded observation is:

Shared operator identity != single discoverable hospitality relationship.

A second important boundary is:

Airport journey state != accommodation preference.

A traveller may prefer the Airside product but still be unable to use it if the required airport-side conditions are not satisfied.

This does not establish that the different products are operationally independent. Several may ultimately share staff, capacity systems, commercial infrastructure or service-recovery processes.

## Limits

The prominence and ordering of the different products across desktop and mobile discovery have not been systematically inspected.

No user study establishes whether travellers consistently understand the difference between YOTELAIR Airside and YOTEL Landside before booking.

The evidence does not establish how often users misunderstand the international-flight boarding-pass requirement or select the wrong property.

The relationship among standard hourly rooms, overnight rooms and Sleep Cabins is publicly distinguishable, but the discovery evidence does not establish whether users perceive them as separate operational products or simply different room options.

Discovery clarity therefore remains partial rather than sufficient.
