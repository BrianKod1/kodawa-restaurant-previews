# Reservation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do hourly, overnight, Sleep Cabin, direct, OTA and group reservations differ in commitment, duration, inventory and cancellation conditions?

## Evidence

The operator exposes more than one accommodation commitment model.

YOTELAIR Airside presents hourly accommodation alongside conventional overnight stays. [S1][S2]

Sleep Cabins are separately presented as a single-guest product rather than merely another named standard-room category. [S3][S4]

The inspected public material contains an unresolved duration conflict.

S2 describes Sleep Cabins as bookable by the hour for a maximum of four hours. [S2]

S3 states that Sleep Cabins are bookable by the hour with a minimum of four hours. [S3]

The dossier does not resolve those statements without stronger evidence.

The Sleep Cabin booking flow also publishes a cancellation condition providing a full refund when cancellation occurs before 15:00 on the day before arrival. [S4]

General YOTEL terms add further reservation distinctions. Direct YOTEL reservations and bookings made through authorised online travel agencies can operate through different booking relationships, while bookings above five rooms, cabins or pads become subject to group conditions. [S6]

Optional or ancillary facilities may also carry separate conditions or charges rather than following automatically from the accommodation reservation. [S6]

## Interpretation

The reservation evidence supports several bounded distinctions.

Hourly stay != overnight stay.

Sleep Cabin relationship != standard-room relationship.

Direct booking != OTA booking relationship.

Individual booking != group booking relationship.

Accommodation booking != automatic entitlement to every ancillary service.

The important question is not merely whether several prices or channels exist. It is whether the different commitments materially change duration, entitlement, responsibility, capacity or recovery.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation remains viable:

YOTELAIR may be one conventional airport hotel using several room formats, stay durations and distribution channels.

Under that explanation, hourly rooms, overnight rooms and Sleep Cabins would represent commercial packaging rather than a broader multi-relationship hospitality architecture.

Case #33 must therefore test whether the differences extend beyond pricing and booking format into materially different access, entitlement, operational responsibility or service-recovery conditions.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish whether hourly and overnight accommodation draw from one common room inventory.

They do not establish whether Sleep Cabins participate in the same inventory system as standard rooms.

The maximum-versus-minimum four-hour Sleep Cabin conflict remains unresolved.

Complete rate-specific no-show, refund, modification, early-arrival and extension rules have not been established across all booking channels.

## Next evidence

Inspect live booking pathways for hourly rooms, overnight rooms and Sleep Cabins.

Record rate rules, inventory presentation, payment timing, cancellation conditions, modification options, group thresholds and whether direct and OTA channels materially alter entitlement or responsibility.
