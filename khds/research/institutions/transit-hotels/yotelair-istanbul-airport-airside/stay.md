# Stay

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Once inside the institution, how do duration, accommodation type, shared facilities and separately purchasable services create different guest relationships?

## Evidence

YOTELAIR Airside does not present one fixed-duration lodging relationship.

The operator presents both hourly accommodation and conventional overnight stays. [S1][S2]

Sleep Cabins introduce a different accommodation form. They are described as single-guest spaces and use shared shower and bathroom facilities rather than the same private-facility arrangement implied by standard hotel accommodation. [S2][S3][S4]

This establishes at least two different accommodation configurations within the wider airside proposition.

Accommodation form != identical facility entitlement.

Duration also differs.

Hourly accommodation and overnight accommodation create different expected periods of occupancy. [S1][S2]

The inspected sources contain an unresolved Sleep Cabin duration conflict: S2 describes a maximum of four hours, while S3 describes a minimum of four hours. [S2][S3]

The dossier therefore does not assign one definitive Sleep Cabin duration rule.

## Ancillary-service boundary

The wider Airside proposition includes Revolve Lounge access that can be purchased separately rather than following automatically from a room booking. [S1]

General YOTEL terms also distinguish accommodation from complementary or ancillary facilities that may carry separate conditions or charges. [S6]

The strongest bounded distinction is:

Accommodation entitlement != universal service entitlement.

Physical presence within the same operator environment != identical service access.

## Interpretation

Case #33 therefore contains several potentially independent stay conditions:

- accommodation duration;
- accommodation type;
- private versus shared facilities;
- lounge entitlement;
- ancillary-service entitlement.

That structure is compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture because two people within the same wider institution may hold materially different lodging and service relationships.

However, the evidence does not establish that these differences are managed through separate operational systems.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation remains viable:

YOTELAIR may simply be a conventional airport hotel offering several room formats, lengths of stay and optional add-ons.

Under that explanation, the differences would remain product-tier variation rather than evidence of a broader hospitality architecture.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish:

- whether hourly and overnight stays use the same underlying room inventory;
- whether hourly stays can be extended;
- whether an hourly booking can convert into an overnight booking;
- how Sleep Cabin occupancy limits are enforced;
- whether standard-room guests receive any automatic Revolve Lounge entitlement;
- whether lounge-only visitors share operational systems with hotel guests;
- how unavailable ancillary services are recovered or compensated.

No representative stay behaviour was observed.

## Next evidence

Inspect room-category inclusions, hourly extension rules, overnight conversion rules, Sleep Cabin operating conditions, lounge entitlement, shower entitlement and re-entry conditions.

Determine whether accommodation and ancillary-service differences materially change guest rights and operational responsibility or remain ordinary product segmentation.
