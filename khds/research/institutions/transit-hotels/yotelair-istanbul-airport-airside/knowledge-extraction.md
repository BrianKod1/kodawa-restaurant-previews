# Knowledge Extraction

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Case #33 research role

Case #33 examines YOTELAIR Istanbul Airport (Airside) as a transit-hospitality institution combining private lodging, hourly accommodation, overnight accommodation, standalone Sleep Cabins, shared facilities, separately purchasable lounge access and airport-dependent eligibility.

The primary falsification question is:

Does Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture recur in an airside transit hotel where short stays, overnight stays, airport access conditions and shared hospitality spaces overlap, or do those differences collapse into one conventional hotel-room product?

## Evidence-backed findings

### 1. One operator environment exposes multiple lodging and service relationships

The inspected sources distinguish hourly accommodation, overnight accommodation, Sleep Cabins, separately purchasable Revolve Lounge access and a related Landside hotel with different access conditions. [S1][S2][S3][S5]

Shared operator identity != single hospitality relationship.

### 2. Airport eligibility is independent of accommodation preference

YOTELAIR Airside requires a valid boarding pass for an international flight because the property is inside the secure international transit environment. [S1][S2][S3][S4]

A traveller may want or reserve the Airside product while still lacking the airport status needed to reach it.

Hotel reservation != guaranteed physical accessibility.

Airport journey state != accommodation preference.

### 3. Airside and Landside are materially different access relationships

YOTEL publicly distinguishes YOTELAIR Airside from YOTEL Landside.

The Airside property depends on international-flight and secure-area eligibility, while the Landside property is before security and passport control. [S2][S5]

Shared operator identity != identical physical-access conditions.

Airside eligibility != Landside eligibility.

### 4. Stay duration is not uniform

The operator presents both hourly accommodation and conventional overnight stays. [S1][S2]

This creates different expected occupancy periods within the same broader operator environment.

Hourly stay != overnight stay.

Stay duration can depend on product relationship.

### 5. Sleep Cabins constitute a distinct accommodation form

Sleep Cabins are publicly presented as single-guest accommodation and use shared shower and bathroom facilities rather than the same facility configuration as standard hotel accommodation. [S2][S3][S4]

Sleep Cabin relationship != standard-room relationship.

Private sleeping space != private entitlement to every supporting facility.

### 6. Accommodation does not establish universal ancillary-service entitlement

Revolve Lounge access can be purchased separately from accommodation, while YOTEL's general terms distinguish accommodation from ancillary or complementary services that may carry separate conditions or charges. [S1][S6]

Accommodation entitlement != entitlement to every ancillary service.

Physical co-location != uniform service entitlement.

### 7. Dining is distributed across more than one service relationship

Revolve Lounge contains a food-and-drink proposition, while Sleep Cabin material separately references Grab+Go. [S1][S3]

The inspected evidence does not establish identical dining entitlement for standard-room guests, hourly guests, Sleep Cabin users and lounge-only visitors.

Service presence != universal inclusion.

Accommodation booking != proof of dining entitlement.

### 8. Booking relationships also differ

General YOTEL terms distinguish direct bookings from authorised OTA bookings and apply separate group conditions above five rooms, cabins or pads. [S6]

Direct booking != OTA booking relationship.

Individual booking != group booking relationship.

The evidence does not yet establish whether those commercial distinctions materially alter operational treatment.

### 9. The accommodation relationship can end before the airport journey ends

Hourly accommodation, overnight accommodation and Sleep Cabin stays create time-bounded hospitality relationships inside a wider international travel journey. [S1][S2][S3]

Hotel-stay duration != airport-journey duration.

Accommodation checkout != completion of passenger journey.

### 10. Public duration information is internally inconsistent

The inspected sources contain an unresolved Sleep Cabin duration conflict.

S2 describes Sleep Cabins as bookable by the hour for a maximum of four hours. [S2]

S3 describes Sleep Cabins as bookable by the hour with a minimum of four hours. [S3]

The dossier preserves this conflict.

S1 also contains inconsistent Revolve Lounge duration language: one section describes the EUR 80 proposition as four hours while another describes 24-hour lounge access from EUR 80. [S1]

Shared operator identity != one internally consistent public information state.

## Competing explanation

The principal competing explanation is:

YOTELAIR Airside is one conventional airport hotel offering several accommodation formats, stay durations, booking channels and optional ancillary products.

The public evidence does not eliminate that explanation.

Hourly stays may simply be shorter versions of ordinary room inventory.

Sleep Cabins may simply be a compact accommodation tier.

Revolve Lounge may simply be an add-on hospitality product.

Airside eligibility may be an external airport constraint rather than an internally designed role architecture.

A stronger architecture conclusion would require evidence that these relationships materially alter operational entitlement, capacity allocation, priority, service recovery, responsibility or system ownership.
