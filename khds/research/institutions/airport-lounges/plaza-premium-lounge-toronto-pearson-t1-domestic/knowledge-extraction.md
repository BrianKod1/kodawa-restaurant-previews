## Case #32 research role

Case #32 examines Plaza Premium Lounge Toronto Pearson Terminal 1 Domestic as a compact, non-lodging transit-hospitality institution.

The primary falsification question is:

Does Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture recur where direct-pay guests, walk-ins, passholders, partner-credential users, groups and optional-service users overlap, or do the differences collapse into one simple paid-access lounge product?

## Evidence-backed findings

### 1. One lounge identity exposes multiple access relationships

The inspected sources document direct purchase, advance booking, walk-in use, partner-card or travel-pass eligibility, PPL Pass, group booking and Lounge To Go. [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]

Shared institutional identity != single access pathway.

### 2. Eligibility does not guarantee admission

Partner-card and travel-pass users may present an eligible credential and boarding pass without an ordinary reservation, but entrance remains subject to lounge capacity and program conditions. [S4]

Credential eligibility != guaranteed admission.

Entitlement != available capacity.

### 3. Commitment mechanisms are not uniform

Advance booking, walk-in purchase, voucher use and group booking represent different forms of commitment. [S1][S3][S5]

Advance booking != walk-in relationship.

Group commitment != individual commitment.

### 4. Admission does not prove universal service entitlement

Showers and other facilities can remain chargeable or availability-dependent. [S1][S3][S5]

Lounge admission != entitlement to every service.

Service availability != admission availability.

### 5. Time entitlement can depend on product relationship

Pearson publishes two-, three- and six-hour direct-pay packages, while PPL Pass typically provides up to three hours per visit. [S2][S5]

Physical presence != indefinite lounge entitlement.

Product relationship can affect duration conditions.

### 6. Shared space contains documented segmentation

S1 describes three seating zones within a 4,900-square-foot lounge for up to 110 guests. Pearson separately identifies an optional VIP-section upgrade. [S1][S2]

Physical co-location != proof of uniform service conditions.

The evidence does not yet establish exactly how those spatial distinctions affect entitlement.

### 7. An adjacent operator product has an explicit entitlement boundary

Lounge To Go is offered within the broader Plaza Premium environment but explicitly does not include lounge access or lounge services. [S7]

Shared operator identity != identical product entitlement.

### 8. Public information is not internally uniform

The operator and airport publish conflicting daily closing times. [S1][S2]

S1 states 05:30-01:00.

S2 states 05:30-22:30.

The conflict remains unresolved.

Shared institutional identity != consistent public operating information.

## Architecture assessment

Case #32 is directionally compatible with the provisional:

Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture.

The case is useful because the observed distinctions occur without a hotel room, residential relationship or private-club membership acting as the primary organizing mechanism.

Potential role partition instead appears through:

- direct purchase;
- advance booking;
- walk-in status;
- partner credential;
- pass entitlement;
- group status;

- capacity exposure;
- optional-service access;
- adjacent non-lounge product boundaries.

This creates additional transfer pressure against an explanation in which the broader architecture exists only in accommodation or membership-led hospitality.

## Competing explanation

The principal competing explanation is:

Plaza Premium Lounge is one conventional airport-lounge product distributed through several payment, partner and pass channels.

The public evidence does not eliminate that explanation.

A stronger architecture conclusion would require evidence that the different pathways materially alter operational rights, priority, recovery, responsibility or service access rather than merely changing the method of payment or qualification.
