# Stay

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Once admitted, how do visit duration, seating, capacity and optional services shape different lounge-use relationships?

## Evidence

The lounge is not represented as unlimited-duration hospitality.

Pearson publishes direct-pay packages structured around two-, three- and six-hour use periods. [S2]

PPL Pass uses a different entitlement model and typically provides up to three hours per visit. [S5]

This means the duration of lounge use can depend on the access product rather than merely on physical presence inside the lounge.

Admission != indefinite occupancy.

The operator describes a 4,900-square-foot facility for up to 110 guests with three seating zones. [S1]

Pearson also identifies an optional VIP-section upgrade. [S2]

These facts establish spatial differentiation but do not prove how access to each zone is governed in practice.

## Optional services

Lounge admission does not automatically establish entitlement to every available service.

The operator identifies showers as an available service while also attaching charge or availability conditions. [S1]

The general terms state that services requested at the lounge may remain subject to availability. [S3]

PPL Pass guidance likewise warns that facilities differ by location and that some services may be chargeable or availability-dependent. [S5]

The strongest bounded distinction is:

Lounge admission != guaranteed access to every optional service.

Service presence != service availability.

## Interpretation

Case #32 therefore contains at least three potentially independent stay conditions:

- duration entitlement;
- spatial access;
- optional-service access.

A passenger may have valid lounge admission while still facing limits on time, capacity, zone access or optional-service availability.

That structure is compatible with the broader multi-relationship hypothesis, but it does not yet prove a distinct operating architecture.

A simpler lounge-product explanation remains viable if the differences amount only to package tiers and ordinary service availability.

## Limits

The public evidence does not establish:

- how time limits are enforced;
- whether extensions can be purchased;
- whether passengers may leave and re-enter;
- whether flight delays change duration entitlement;
- whether all three seating zones are equally accessible;
- the exact entitlement attached to the VIP upgrade;
- how shower queues or unavailable showers are handled.

No representative in-lounge behaviour was observed.

## Next evidence

Inspect detailed extension, re-entry, zone-access and optional-service rules and determine whether different access products produce materially different in-lounge rights.
