# Dining

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What food-and-beverage services are represented as part of lounge admission, and where do entitlement, availability or dietary limits remain unclear?

## Evidence

Food and beverage are presented as part of the lounge proposition on the operator's Terminal 1 Domestic page. [S1]

This makes dining part of the hospitality experience rather than an unrelated external product.

However, the public evidence inspected for Case #32 does not support the stronger claim that every food or beverage item is included for every admitted passenger.

PPL Pass guidance states that facilities and service inclusions can vary by location and that some services may remain chargeable or subject to availability. [S5]

That warning prevents the dossier from converting a general lounge feature into a universal entitlement claim.

## Interpretation

For Case #32, dining currently demonstrates a distinction between:

- a service being offered within the lounge;
- a passenger being admitted to the lounge;
- the exact food-and-beverage entitlement attached to that admission.

The bounded principle under test is:

Service presence != universal inclusion.

Lounge admission != proof of identical dining entitlement.

This distinction is potentially relevant to Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture because different access products could theoretically carry different inclusions.

The current evidence does not establish that they actually do.

## Competing explanation

The simpler explanation remains strong:

Food and beverage may simply be a standard shared lounge amenity available under substantially the same conditions to all admitted users.

Nothing in the current source set disproves that explanation.

## Limits

The inspected evidence does not establish:

- the current menu;
- buffet or service periods;
- exact alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverage conditions;
- dietary accommodation procedures;
- allergen handling;
- replenishment behaviour during high demand;
- whether premium items require payment;
- whether partner, pass and direct-pay users receive identical dining inclusions.

No meal-service observation or purchase test was performed.

## Next evidence

Inspect current lounge-specific menus, dietary guidance, beverage rules, service periods and explicit inclusion or exclusion conditions for the principal access pathways.
