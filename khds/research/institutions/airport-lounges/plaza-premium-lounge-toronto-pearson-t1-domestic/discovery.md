# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How is the Terminal 1 Domestic lounge presented, who is told they may use it, and how clearly are distinct access relationships exposed before booking?

## Evidence

The public material presents one Plaza Premium Lounge identity while exposing several ways a passenger may approach the product.

Pearson describes the lounge as open to all passengers and publishes direct-pay time packages. This means airline or cabin class is not presented as the only discovery route. [S2]

The operator page also supports advance booking and walk-in use. [S1]

Separate operator guidance describes partner credit-card and travel-pass access. Eligible users can present the relevant credential and boarding pass without making an ordinary lounge reservation, although access remains capacity-dependent. [S4]

PPL Pass creates another discoverable route. It uses a voucher-based entitlement rather than an ordinary direct lounge purchase. [S5]

The eligible-lounges page confirms that this specific Toronto Pearson Terminal 1 Domestic lounge participates in the relevant pass network. [S6]

Group use also has a distinct route. The operator defines a group booking as ten or more persons and applies separate group conditions. [S3]

Finally, Lounge To Go is presented within the broader Plaza Premium environment but explicitly does not include lounge access or lounge services. [S7]

## Interpretation

The public discovery layer therefore does not expose only one relationship.

At minimum, it distinguishes:

- direct purchase;
- advance reservation;
- walk-in purchase;
- partner-card or travel-pass eligibility;
- PPL Pass;
- group booking;
- Lounge To Go without lounge admission.

The strongest bounded observation is:

Shared operator identity != single discoverable relationship.

This does not establish that each route is operationally independent. Several pathways may ultimately enter the same capacity and service system.

## Limits

The inspected evidence does not establish how prominently each pathway appears to a first-time passenger.

Desktop and mobile discovery journeys have not been systematically compared.

No user study establishes whether passengers understand the difference between purchasing lounge access, presenting a partner credential, using a PPL Pass or buying Lounge To Go.

Discovery clarity therefore remains partial rather than sufficient.
