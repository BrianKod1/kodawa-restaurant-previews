# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How are location, eligibility verification, credential checking, walk-in admission and capacity decisions handled at physical arrival?

## Evidence

The lounge is presented as a post-security Terminal 1 Domestic facility at Toronto Pearson. [S1][S2]

Arrival does not begin from one universal customer state.

A direct-booking passenger can arrive with an existing prepaid lounge relationship. [S1][S3]

The operator also accepts walk-in use, meaning some passengers may reach the lounge before making a lounge-access purchase. [S1][S3]

Partner-card and travel-pass users use a different verification route. The operator states that eligible users may present the relevant credential together with a boarding pass without an ordinary reservation. [S4]

That entitlement does not guarantee physical admission. S4 explicitly keeps entry subject to lounge capacity and notes that partner quotas or visit limits may apply. [S4]

PPL Pass creates another arrival state based on a voucher entitlement rather than an ordinary direct purchase. [S5]

These relationships can therefore converge on one physical entrance while carrying different proofs of eligibility.

## Capacity boundary

The operator publishes a maximum capacity of up to 110 guests. [S1]

The existence of a valid credential, voucher or purchase therefore does not by itself establish that admission will occur at a particular moment.

Credential eligibility != guaranteed physical admission.

Entitlement != available capacity.

## Interpretation

The arrival evidence is compatible with role partition because the lounge can receive:

- prepaid direct customers;
- walk-in customers;
- partner-card users;
- travel-pass users;
- PPL Pass users;
- group users.

However, the evidence does not establish that frontline staff operationally treat these groups as separate roles after admission.

The simpler interpretation remains possible: several commercial pathways may terminate in one common admission process.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish:

- queue priority among access pathways;
- whether advance bookings receive priority over walk-ins;
- whether partner users receive a separate capacity allocation;
- whether a waitlist is used;
- how denied admission is communicated or recovered;
- how credential exceptions are handled;
- whether frontline verification differs materially among partner programs.

No on-site arrival observation or transaction test was performed.

## Next evidence

Inspect official queue, capacity, denied-entry and waitlist guidance and, if permitted in a later research phase, separately test how different credentials are processed at arrival.
