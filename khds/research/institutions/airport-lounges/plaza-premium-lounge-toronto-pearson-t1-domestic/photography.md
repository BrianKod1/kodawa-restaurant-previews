# Photography

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Does official visual material explain spatial layout, access boundaries and service differences, or primarily promote the lounge?

## Current evidence position

The inspected textual sources establish several visual questions that should be tested.

S1 describes a 4,900-square-foot lounge with three seating zones and capacity for up to 110 guests. [S1]

S2 identifies an optional VIP-section upgrade. [S2]

Those statements imply that official imagery could potentially help explain:

- differences among seating zones;
- spatial density;
- service areas;
- the relationship between ordinary and VIP space;
- shower or optional-service areas;
- food-and-beverage presentation;
- practical wayfinding.

## Evidence limitation

Case #32 has not yet completed a systematic image-by-image inspection of the official lounge photography.

Accordingly, this dossier does not claim that the published photographs prove any particular layout, access boundary, crowd condition or entitlement distinction.

Textual evidence about multiple zones is not converted into a photographic finding without direct inspection.

The appropriate boundary is:

Visual availability != completed visual evidence.

Promotional image != representative operating condition.

Spatial description != verified photographic explanation.

## Interpretation

Photography remains important to the case because a lounge can present itself visually as one seamless premium environment while operating through different access products, capacities or service conditions.

That possibility is currently a research question, not a finding.

No conclusion is drawn about whether official photography hides, explains or exaggerates operational distinctions.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish:

- the complete official image set;
- image dates;
- whether imagery represents the current physical configuration;
- desktop versus mobile image ordering;
- whether the VIP section is visibly distinguished;
- whether practical wayfinding is shown;
- whether crowded conditions are represented;
- whether shower or service boundaries are visually explained.
