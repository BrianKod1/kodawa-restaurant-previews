# Reservation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do advance booking, walk-in, pass, partner-card, group and optional-service pathways differ in commitment, capacity and cancellation conditions?

## Evidence

The operator distinguishes advance booking from walk-in access. S1 describes advance booking and identifies walk-in use as an alternative. [S1]

The general terms distinguish confirmed prepaid orders from services requested on arrival. Services requested at the lounge remain subject to availability. [S3]

Partner-card and travel-pass users operate under a different commitment model. S4 states that eligible users may arrive with the relevant credential and boarding pass without making an ordinary reservation, but entrance is not guaranteed and remains subject to lounge capacity. Partner quotas or visit limits may also apply. [S4]

PPL Pass creates a voucher-based relationship. The pass can create lounge entitlement without being the same transaction as a direct lounge booking. [S5]

Group bookings are also separated. The terms define ten or more persons as a group and apply specific cancellation conditions to that category. [S3]

Optional services create another layer. Shower access may carry additional charges or availability conditions rather than following automatically from lounge admission. [S1][S3][S5]

## Interpretation

The reservation evidence supports a bounded distinction between access products and access certainty.

Advance booking != walk-in relationship.

Credential eligibility != guaranteed admission.

Pass entitlement != unlimited capacity.

Lounge admission != automatic entitlement to every optional service.

Group commitment != individual commitment.

These differences matter because they can change what has been purchased, what must be presented, what is guaranteed and what remains capacity-dependent.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation remains viable:

These are merely different payment or distribution channels feeding one lounge product.

Case #32 must therefore test whether the pathways materially change entitlement, priority, service conditions or responsibility, rather than assuming that commercial-channel differences constitute a broader hospitality architecture.

## Limits

The inspected sources do not establish whether all access pathways draw from one common admission pool.

They do not establish a complete priority order when the lounge approaches capacity.

Individual no-show, refund, denied-entry and change rules remain incomplete across all pathways.

## Next evidence

Inspect channel-specific cancellation, refund, denied-entry, priority and capacity rules and determine whether access route changes actual entitlement or only payment method.
