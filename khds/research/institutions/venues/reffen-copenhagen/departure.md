1. Purpose of this file

This file studies how a guest completes participation at Reffen and transitions from the venue into the next physical, financial and relational state.

Departure is not treated as crossing the venue boundary.

A guest may need to complete several objects before leaving:

a food or drink transaction,

product collection,

eating or drinking,

clearing or disposal,

an event or activity,

a reserved group period,

group-card administration,

gift-card balance decisions,

reunion with companions,

property retrieval,

accessibility assistance,

complaint or refund initiation,

and transport preparation.

Different Reffen services can close at different times.

The guest can therefore be finished with one part of the venue while still active in another.

This file asks what must be complete before leaving becomes a usable hospitality outcome rather than an unsupported handoff.

2. Departure boundary

Departure begins when the guest starts moving from active participation toward completion.

It may begin when the guest:

finishes a meal,

decides not to make another purchase,

reaches the end of an event,

leaves a reserved table,

checks a remaining card balance,

starts gathering companions,

retrieves a bicycle or vehicle,

checks the next bus or harbour bus,

or decides that weather, crowding or fatigue requires leaving.

Departure ends when the relevant guest can:

exit the venue,

enter an appropriate onward route,

understand any unresolved transaction or entitlement,

retain a usable recovery path,

and know whether the relationship continues after the visit.

3. Primary research question

How should a distributed hospitality venue support coherent departure when guest activities, operator responsibilities, stored value, event end states and transport options close on different timelines?

6. Verified public departure facts

Current official evidence establishes that:

Reffen is reachable and leaveable by bicycle, bus, harbour bus, walking and car.

Its practical page identifies bus 2A and harbour buses 991 and 992 as nearby routes with approximate service frequencies.

The same page gives an approximate 25–30 minute walk to or from Nyhavn.

Paid car parking exists nearby but Reffen states it does not operate the car parks.

Own-boat mooring is described nearby under a separate parking-ticket condition, and Reffen states it does not operate the mooring facilities.

Reffen asks bicycles to be parked outside the venue.

Food stalls, bars and the coffee shop have different published operating states.

Current official Reffen pages contain inconsistent closing times across sections.

Reffen publishes that lost-property assistance can be initiated through a bar by asking for a floor manager.

Lost items are described as being collected in Beer Garden and BnW and moved to Werkstatt on Mondays.

Reffen gift-card terms publish balance, validity, refund, loss and data-handling rules.

The current Reffen-card FAQ separately publishes balance, supplementary payment, validity and refund information.

Group dining can use individually distributed Reffen cards and consolidated billing.

Venue staff are described as available to assist guests with special needs.

Food and bar areas are described as flat and asphalted with ramps where needed, while the car parks have no marked disabled parking zones.

Busy sunny days can create crowd bottlenecks.

Reffen publishes general, company-event, collaboration and press contact routes.

Werkstatt publishes separate contact details.

These facts establish departure mechanisms and boundaries.

They do not establish live availability or successful recovery.

45. Departure waiting taxonomy

The canonical waiting taxonomy remains unchanged.

Access Waiting

Waiting to reach an exit, stop, parking area, mooring, help point or refund desk.

Resource Waiting

Waiting for transport, shelter, accessible space, a floor manager or group member.

Product Waiting

Waiting for a final order or included event item before leaving.

Service Waiting

Waiting for balance check, lost-property help, payment closure or assistance.

Exception Waiting

Waiting for complaint routing, refund, transport disruption, missing-person resolution or cancellation recovery.

No sixth waiting category is created.

The departure case shows that several existing waiting types can persist after primary participation ends.

The planned bicycle, walking, water or waiting route becomes unsuitable.

The internal accessible route does not connect to usable onward transport.

The guest leaves with no usable recovery or feedback route.

47. Recovery hierarchy

A useful departure-recovery sequence is:

Identify Unclosed Object
→
Identify Current Guest State
→
Identify Responsible Layer
→
Choose On-Site or Remote Route
→
Preserve Necessary Proof
→
Resolve / Refund / Recover / Re-route
→
Confirm Closure or Next State

The hierarchy should prevent two common failures:

leaving the guest at a closed physical help point;

sending every issue to a general venue contact regardless of ownership.

60. Falsification programme

60.1 Distributed closure

Hypothesis: Guests must close several materially distinct objects before departure.

Could be falsified if: Most visits require only straightforward physical exit with no relevant unresolved objects.

Needed evidence: Journey observation and exit interviews.

60.2 Multiple closing clocks

Hypothesis: Different service clocks create meaningful departure confusion or opportunity loss.

Could be falsified if: Live on-site communication makes the sequence consistently obvious.

Needed evidence: Closing-period observation and support logs.

60.3 Group reassembly

Hypothesis: Distributed participation materially complicates group departure.

Could be falsified if: Groups naturally reunite without meaningful delay or venue support.

Needed evidence: Group observation and organiser interviews.

60.4 Stored-value differentiation

Hypothesis: Similar card naming creates material misunderstanding of balance, validity or refund rules.

Could be falsified if: Products are operationally identical or guests receive clear product-specific terms at issue.

Needed evidence: Product governance, support contacts and comprehension testing.

60.5 Last-connection confidence

Hypothesis: Static route frequency is insufficient for event and late-night departures.

Could be falsified if: Service is frequent and reliable enough that live evidence rarely changes guest action.

Needed evidence: Timetable comparison, disruption history and guest interviews.

60.6 Event-release capacity

Hypothesis: Event endings create meaningful transport and egress pressure.

Could be falsified if: Audience size, route diversity and staggered departure absorb demand.

Needed evidence: Event egress counts and transport occupancy.

60.7 Accessible egress

Hypothesis: Venue-level accessibility can fail at the onward-transport handoff.

Could be falsified if: Continuous accessible routes and assistance reliably connect all relevant modes.

Needed evidence: End-to-end accessibility audits at different times.

60.8 Lost-property temporal handoff

Hypothesis: Changing collection location creates avoidable recovery burden.

Could be falsified if: Staff communication and remote support make the transfer seamless.

Needed evidence: Lost-property case tracking.

60.9 External responsibility boundary

Hypothesis: Guests misattribute parking, mooring or ticket problems to Reffen.

Could be falsified if: Ownership is consistently understood and recovery is direct.

Needed evidence: Complaint categorisation and wayfinding tests.

60.10 Waste completion

Hypothesis: Unclear guest-facing clearing rules weaken departure completion.

Could be falsified if: On-site design makes correct action obvious without digital publication.

Needed evidence: Waste-area observation and sorting outcomes.

60.11 Departure responsibility router

Hypothesis: Object-based routing reduces guest effort and incorrect handoffs.

Could be falsified if: One common team already owns all departure recovery effectively.

Needed evidence: Resolution-time comparison and escalation mapping.

60.12 Post-visit continuity

Hypothesis: Separating recovery, feedback and marketing increases trust and usable follow-up.

Could be falsified if: Current contact and consent systems already create clear, voluntary relationships.

Needed evidence: Consent-flow audit and post-visit response data.

69. Working conclusion

Reffen demonstrates that departure from a multi-operator hospitality venue is not one reversed arrival path.

It is the closure of several participation objects followed by a handoff into external systems the venue may describe but not control.

The strongest current abstraction is:

→
Complete / Carry Forward / Escalate
→
Reassemble People and Property
→
Check Live Onward State
→
Accessible Physical Egress
→

→
Post-Visit Recovery or Relationship

≠

Guest Complete

End the visit by closing or clearly carrying forward every relevant object, not by assuming the relationship ends at the gate.
