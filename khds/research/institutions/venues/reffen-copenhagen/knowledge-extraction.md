3. Primary research question

Do restaurant-derived and institution-transfer KHDS patterns survive a generally open, seasonal and self-directed hospitality venue where one guest-facing place is delivered through independent operators, shared infrastructure, common resources and temporary event states?

4. Extracted answer

Yes.

Many existing KHDS patterns transfer.

They do not transfer unchanged.

Reffen shifts the research unit from one principal hospitality operator toward a distributed venue system.

The guest may experience:

one destination,

one broad atmosphere,

one venue map,

and one overall visit,

while participating through:

several independent food stalls,

bars,

shared seating,

common infrastructure,

venue-managed practical systems,

external transport,

event organisers,

ticketing routes,

and Werkstatt.

The decisive case boundary is:

One Guest-Facing Place

≠

One Hospitality Operator

Reffen therefore tests whether hospitality coherence can exist without:

one reservation object,

one menu,

one transaction,

one service clock,

one queue,

one atmosphere,

one narrative owner,

or one recovery endpoint.

The extracted answer is:

Difference Can Remain Distributed.

Responsibility Cannot Become Indecipherable.

The venue does not need to centralise every operator.

It does need to make the relationships among operators, shared resources and guest states legible enough for participation and recovery.

The strongest reusable rule is:

Preserve operator difference, but never make the guest discover responsibility only after something fails.
