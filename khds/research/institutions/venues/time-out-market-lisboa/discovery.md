1. Purpose and discovery boundary

This file studies how a prospective guest recognises Time Out Market Lisboa, identifies an appropriate route, and distinguishes a published possibility from a currently available experience. It does not evaluate whether the food, atmosphere or service is good.

Discovery can start from a Time Out story, a named eatery, a course, an event, a search for Mercado da Ribeira, or a group-planning need. It does not end merely because the guest lands on the market homepage. It ends when the guest can make an appropriately bounded next decision: visit as a walk-in diner, investigate an eatery and dietary fit, check a specific programme, enquire about a group/private use, follow an external course or delivery route, or decide not to visit.

The principal question is:

Does editorial curation make the shared market easier to recognise and navigate while preserving the different freshness, access and responsibility conditions of each guest route?

The official navigation exposes distinct Food & Drink, What's On, Find Us, Who We Are, Order Now, Cooking Courses and Events & Groups routes. The last two purchase/fulfilment routes may leave the immediate market-information surface; a navigation label is not proof that all guest intents have the same booking or service terms. Market overview, concept.

3. Public identity: a market selected by an editorial brand

Time Out presents the market as the original Time Out Market, founded by its Portugal team in 2014 inside Mercado da Ribeira. Its explanation links the publishing brand's editorial judgments to the food projects chosen for the market and describes the possibility of a changing lineup. This is an observable selection story, not independent verification that each selected offer is best, open or suitable for a particular guest. Who we are, market overview.

The editorial idea performs three discovery jobs:

Category recognition: the visitor understands that the market is a selected collection rather than one restaurant.

Choice framing: the collection promises many culinary and cultural possibilities under a familiar media brand.

Story continuity: the venue's identity can survive a changed lineup because the curatorial rule, not a fixed menu, is its primary public narrative carrier.

The third job is an inference from the published concept, to be tested against the actual update cadence and guest-facing choices. It does not establish vendor-level quality or a permanent roster. Who we are, eatery guide.

3.1 The building is not one undifferentiated institution

Time Out's own concept description distinguishes its food and culture side from long-running meat, fish, fruit and flower vendors elsewhere in Mercado da Ribeira. A search result or map pin for the historic building may therefore lead to more than one commercial and spatial identity. A visitor looking for traditional produce and a visitor looking for a Time Out eatery should not be assumed to need the same entrance, hours, policy or contact. The public page identifies the coexistence, but does not supply a complete boundary map. Who we are, directions.

Discovery correction: first identify the building; then identify the intended sub-institution and guest route. One address is insufficient proof that every occupant shares an operating model.

4.1 A meaningful route is more than another offer

Different cuisine choices may share an ordinary walk-in visit but still change vendor-specific dietary and product checks. A course, private event, event card or delivery order may change preparation, allocation, payment or provider. KHDS should classify guest intent only where it materially changes route or consequence; a long list of eateries alone does not prove a different hospitality intent for each item.

This is a Multi-Intent Hospitality transfer pressure, not a confidence change or a new formal pattern. The route descriptions come from the market overview and events-and-groups page.

5. Discovery before an ordinary walk-in visit

The older FAQ describes ordinary market dining as casual, with communal tables and no table reservations. That published route gives a first-time visitor a useful expectation: do not treat this like booking a particular restaurant table. It does not guarantee a table, a specific eatery's opening, accessible circulation, or admission to a programme occurring within the building. FAQ, market overview.

For an appropriate visit decision the guest needs four separately fitting layers:

Place fit: correct Time Out Market identity and Mercado da Ribeira address.

Time fit: today's market and intended eatery or programme states, not just evergreen hours.

Route fit: ordinary communal seating versus a ticket, course or private enquiry.

Personal fit: dietary needs, mobility needs, group size and expected crowd tolerance where applicable.

Official directions identify the building address. The FAQ advises discussing food sensitivities and allergies with staff; this is a published referral to a human interaction, not a promise that every stall can safely serve every guest. A food listing can narrow cuisine choice, but cannot substitute for a current vendor-level answer. Directions, FAQ, eatery guide.

6. Editorial listings and attribution

The Time Out eatery guide names individual food businesses within the market and includes descriptions and photographs associated with particular outlets. This helps a visitor see that the venue contains more than a single product or kitchen. The guide is dated 2019 despite some entries discussing later changes; the page should be used as a discovery directory and editorial story, not a live inventory. Eatery guide.

7. Events and group discovery

The market's public shows page frames events in the food hall or Time Out Studio as part of its curated cultural offer. Its index was dated 2025 and contains event cards with their own descriptions; an index is not a current ticket or admissions record. The group page separately describes the Academy, Studio, private hire, event enquiries and pre-loaded group cards. These are materially different from casually taking an open communal seat. Shows and events, events and groups, FAQ.

An event-directed guest must determine, for the specific date:

whether the listing still describes a future occurrence;

whether it is in the food hall, Academy, Studio or another space;

whether entry is free, ticketed, reserved, invitation-based or enquiry-led;

whether a dining visit and event admission are the same entitlement;

and who to contact if the listing or arrangement changes.

The pages inspected do not independently verify admission performance, event cancellation handling or Academy booking success. Those are obligations for later reservation.md and events.md, not findings claimed by this discovery file.

Route boundary: “No table reservations” is not “no arrangements of any kind.” FAQ, events and groups.

11. Unresolved discovery questions for later files

Which official surface supplies the current operating hours and vendor-specific opening state for the intended visit date?

Can a guest find a current menu and accessibility information for their selected eatery rather than rely on a 2019 guide?

Are market navigation and Mercado da Ribeira wayfinding sufficiently explicit about the two commercial areas?

What, if anything, does a group event card entitle its holder to at a specific outlet and on a specific date?

Do particular events provide ticket, capacity and cancellation details that the general What's On index does not?

When a visitor follows an external course or delivery link, which provider takes the next commercial and recovery responsibility?

Is there a published contact route for conflicting official page information before a guest travels?

An unresolved question is a research target, not evidence of operational failure.
