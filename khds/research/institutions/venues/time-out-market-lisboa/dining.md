1. Purpose and scope

participation.md covers the full venue, including events, workshops, groups and shops. This file isolates the meal: how someone chooses a relevant food/drink offer, establishes product fit, orders, receives it, shares space and resolves a food-specific failure. Time Out markets the hall as an editorially selected cross-section of Lisbon's food scene, while its historical FAQ presents communal tables without ordinary table reservations. Those public claims orient dining, but neither is an observation of one actual meal. Portuguese food-and-drink guide, 2019 FAQ.

Can one curated market provide a coherent meal when editorial choice, the specific eatery's product, transaction, wait and venue-level seating are different objects?

Dining begins when a person tries to work out what and where they can appropriately eat or drink, potentially before buying. It includes cuisine and menu interpretation, dietary confirmation, price and payment, order acceptance, preparation/collection, shared-space use, eating, clearing and recovery. It ends when the food/drink activity and its immediate unresolved obligations end, even if a guest then attends an event or purchases another meal. Collection, cleaning and waste practices are research questions; no on-site process was observed.

2. Published dining structure, without inferred contracts

Time Out's Portuguese 2024 food guide describes a food-and-drink collection chosen, tasted and approved by Time Out critics and journalists, presenting a wide range of culinary categories. Its English market overview publishes a food-hall offer along with named eateries and bars, and its concept page distinguishes the curated Time Out part of Mercado da Ribeira from longstanding fresh-product market vendors in the building. This establishes a common editorial identity and plural food presentations, not the legal status of each eatery, shared kitchen standards, common payment processor or a single organisation responsible for every meal. 4. Choice, compatibility and the responsible food answer

Editorial curation reduces the initial which eatery question. It can increase pressure when a guest needs an item-specific answer: what is on the live menu, what it costs, which components can change, who prepares it, or whether it is compatible with a restriction. Time Out's historical FAQ says vegan and gluten-free possibilities exist but explicitly routes sensitivities and allergies to staff. It does not name who speaks for each item, document cross-contact controls or promise an allergy-safe result. Market food guide, FAQ.

5. Ordering and payment: published facts versus live decisions

The 2019 standalone FAQ describes no cash acceptance, card payments and a rechargeable card. Time Out's current English homepage embeds a different FAQ extract saying cash is accepted as well as credit/debit cards. The public pages therefore disagree on a consequential operational rule. The research cannot treat either excerpt as a safe universal instruction at every counter; ask about current seller-level acceptance and what happens when a chosen method fails before assuming an order exists. Standalone FAQ, market homepage.

Time Out's general TOM card terms describe a market-of-purchase prepaid instrument acquired and loaded at Time Out Shop on the ground floor, usable at participating spaces under its terms. Stored value is not an accepted item order, sufficient balance, guaranteed seating or proof the particular seller accepts the card right now. Separately advertised pre-loaded group event cards should not be governed by the general card's terms without establishing product identity and the actual group agreement. General TOM card terms, Events & Groups.

The public materials inspected do not establish whether all food sellers share one point-of-sale system, issue a uniform receipt, offer a common refund window or take an accepted order at the same stage. A published selected-eatery badge is not an order-processing policy.

6. Preparation, collection and the meal's multiple clocks

A guest may select food at one counter, drink at another, and desserts or a second dish later. The common venue brand can present those as one social meal, while each choice may create a separate seller commitment. The public pages support the plurality of offerings but provide no observed vendor-to-vendor timing, pager/receipt system, collection point or coordinated kitchen workflow. These are explicit testing needs, not assertions that guests self-collect at every counter. Time Out Market overview, food-and-drink guide.

7. Seating, social meal and floor-specific caution

The historical FAQ's communal-table/no-ordinary-reservation rule gives a diner freedom to walk in and join shared dining if a suitable place is actually available. It does not address table holding, party-size needs, individual accessibility, noise, standing while waiting, food-hall event interruptions, common-area cleaning or removal of dishes and packaging. These must be observed or obtained from a responsible current venue source; they are not known failures. FAQ, food-hall event example.

Dining companions can hypothetically make different purchases and still need a shared seating state. A good test distinguishes:

seller capacity — can each intended item be produced and fulfilled?

shared seating capacity — is a usable group/individual place available?

time coordination — can the party enjoy the meal without one person's product being long finished before another's arrives?

appropriate evidence — which promises can current menus and visible seating prove, and which require live admission or staff knowledge?

The upstairs Pap'Açôrda profile is a reminder that different dining places in the same building may have different space and service rules. Its dated first-floor description needs current confirmation; it cannot be treated as another communal-table counter on the basis of address alone. Pap'Açôrda, market FAQ.

9. Reffen contrast and KHDS transfer pressure

Case #23 Reffen's food research records independent outdoor stall businesses and explicitly described shared operator infrastructure. The Time Out research has an editorially coherent indoor marketplace and food-hall communal seating, but the inspected pages do not disclose an equivalent supplier/merchant contract or back-of-house infrastructure. Therefore, one should compare the guest-facing responsibility boundary without assuming identical operational organisation. Time Out food guide, Time Out concept, Reffen dining research.
