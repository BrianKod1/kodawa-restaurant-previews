1. Purpose and scope

For this institution, reservation.md means Booking / Access / Participation. It is not shorthand for reserving a table. This file compares how a guest may enter the ordinary market, obtain an individual food offer, join a public event, buy a workshop ticket, arrange a group/private event, use an event card, or follow a delivery link. Each creates a different access, capacity, commitment and recovery question.

What does each participation route actually secure, at which stage, for which person and space, and what published route remains when it cannot complete?

An older official FAQ says the market does not take ordinary communal-table reservations. The current Time Out Academy store separately sells dated workshop tickets; the market also solicits group and private-event enquiries. These statements are not a single contradictory “reservation policy.” They refer to different reservation objects. FAQ, Academy workshops, events and groups.

This research records published terms and public-interface states, not whether staff consistently fulfil them, how payments settle, whether a seat is available, or whether recovery succeeds in practice.

3. Ordinary market dining: walk-in without table reservation

The 2019 Time Out FAQ states that communal tables are not reserved and a visitor may take a seat. This is useful advance participation information but not a live seat-count or an admission guarantee. The FAQ does not explain peak-time queue management, accessible-seat allocation, conflict resolution between groups, or how the market handles a temporarily unavailable seat. Those questions remain unverified, not known failures. Market FAQ.

An ordinary diner can therefore be in several different states:

Intends to visit: no formal table inventory has been secured.

Enters the market: presence in the venue; no particular offer or seat follows.

Finds a communal seat: a current place to sit; no vendor product follows.

Chooses a vendor: product and dietary fit still require vendor-specific information.

Places an accepted order: a transaction may create product-level terms, not a venue seating promise.

Receives the product: that transaction completes; other intended activities may remain open.

The published no-reservation sentence governs the ordinary seating route. It must not be projected onto Academy tickets, group/private arrangements, event cards or market buyout enquiries. FAQ, events and groups.

Capacity correction: building occupancy, communal seats, counter throughput, a particular food item and a workshop edition are separate capacity objects. No public page inspected establishes their live aggregate availability.

4. Eatery transactions: distinct product and responsibility objects

The market's guide identifies numerous named eateries under the shared Time Out identity. The guide is editorial and older; it does not give a universal current ordering, payment, allergy, refund or product-replacement policy for each outlet. The FAQ says dietary sensitivities and allergies should be discussed with staff; it does not identify the responsible staff member for every outlet or guarantee accommodation. Eatery guide, FAQ.

At a counter, later research must distinguish:

who accepts the order and holds the product inventory;

who determines whether an allergy or restriction can be handled safely;

whose receipt or payment surface is presented;

who corrects an inaccurate, unavailable or delayed item;

and whether venue staff can route an issue without owning the vendor transaction.

The published venue brand cannot settle those ownership questions. The counter route may complete independently of seating and independently of a second counter's transaction. One visit can contain several separately recoverable product states. This is a KHDS architecture inference, not a verified account of vendor contracts or service.

5. Academy workshops: an independently specified booking contract

The current Time Out Academy store explicitly says workshop tickets are purchased on its website and cannot be reserved by email. It labels some editions “Coming Soon,” which are not yet available to purchase, and others out of stock or addable to cart. A visible date is therefore not itself a place, and an add-to-cart state is not a completed payment or issued ticket. The store also describes a monthly release cadence. These are store-published states as inspected on 2026-09-15, not durable availability promises. Academy workshop store.

Two safety and access boundaries matter:

The sample page says a guest with a food intolerance, restriction or allergy should email the Academy before buying so the Academy can assess adaptability. This is a published assessment route, not a verified promise of safe accommodation.

The page's age, language and late-arrival terms materially change who can participate and what a purchased ticket secures. These terms should be visible before checkout; a general market visit does not transfer them. Sample Academy workshop edition.

Another advanced Kitchen Skills product publishes a prerequisite of successful completion of earlier levels, showing that “Academy workshop” itself is not one uniform eligibility state. That product was marked Coming Soon when inspected, so the prerequisite is evidence of an advertised route, not an available edition. Kitchen Skills III, Academy workshop store.

Contact freshness: the store's general contact and the sample product's dietary and cancellation instructions show differing Academy email addresses. This file does not resolve which inbox governs a particular present request; the operative product page and checkout documents need route-specific verification before a guest relies on one. 6. Voucher objects are not interchangeable

The Academy's own voucher page says its workshop gift vouchers can be redeemed toward eligible cooking-workshop tickets, with a stated term, one-time redemption, value-difference rules and a confirmation email after ticket checkout. It excludes the “other workshops” category. A voucher is thus not yet a dated workshop ticket, and unused voucher value does not necessarily create a cash refund. These are published Academy voucher rules; issuance and redemption outcomes were not tested. Academy vouchers.

The market's group page separately advertises pre-loaded event cards for group logistics in the food hall. It does not publish an equivalent complete cardholder policy on the inspected group page. Do not carry the Academy voucher's eligibility, expiry or cancellation terms over to these cards, nor assume the card buys workshop entry or reserves a table. Events and groups, Academy vouchers.

There is also a general Time Out Market card (Cartão TOM) with a separate Portuguese-language terms page, stated effective 2024-09-02. The terms name MC Mercados da Capital LDA as the card issuer and describe it as a loaded prepayment for purchases in participating spaces of the market where it was bought. They say purchase/load takes place at Time Out Shop, identify a one-year validity from first load, and publish restrictions on unused-balance reimbursement and lost/stolen cards. These are statements of an official card agreement, not a determination of enforceability, universal counter acceptance, performance or every vendor's payment settlement. TOM card terms.

The market's group-card advertisement may refer to the same physical or contractual card product; its page does not explicitly equate them. Until that relationship is demonstrated, do not apply the general TOM card policy to a specific negotiated group-event arrangement. Conversely, the existence of the general card terms is stronger evidence of a defined instrument than the group page alone. Events and groups, TOM card terms.

7. Events: published access is occurrence-specific

The market's events index mixes programme discovery with particular event pages. One current Salsa at the Market page advertises free entrance for an occurrence, but its visible weekday and date descriptors do not clearly align. That page is a useful example of an event-specific advertised route with temporal ambiguity, not a basis for assigning free admission to every programme or repeating its date as a reliable guest instruction. Shows and events, Salsa event page.

For each occurrence the guest must distinguish:

general market entry versus entry to the event space;

free, ticketed, registered, guest-list or private entitlement;

location in the food hall, Studio, Academy or another area;

occurrence date/time, publication date and live change state;

venue capacity versus audience capacity;

and the organiser or contact responsible for cancellation or inaccurate listing.

The inspected overview does not establish a universal event ticketing or refund rule. events.md must inspect individual programmes before claiming how guests enter or recover.

8. Private events, group spaces and buyout: enquiry is not allocation

The official private/corporate page invites enquiries about Time Out Studio, Time Out Academy, market buyout and group event cards, using contact and form links. It describes possible use cases and spaces but does not present a public live calendar, accepted booking, price quote, required deposit or complete cancellation rules. Events and groups.

The group route has at least four different potential states:

Interest: organiser reviews an advertised possibility.

Enquiry: organiser requests an arrangement; no booking secured yet.

Offer/agreement: institution may specify space, time, access, fees, catering and other terms; these terms are not visible in the inspected public page.

Confirmed participation and recovery: only a documented agreement could define entitlements and modification/cancellation consequences; none was inspected.

For a market buyout, a public promise of potential exclusive use does not prove that a particular date, every vendor, every menu or every area is available. For a group card, a group budget does not prove seating or food inventory. Any specific inclusion must be evidenced in the eventual route-specific arrangement. Events and groups.

9. Payment and data boundaries remain unresolved

The 2019 FAQ includes statements about cash, cards and ATMs that differ from another Time Out presentation of the FAQ on the market overview. This file does not choose one current counter policy or infer that all eateries share a single settlement system. The general TOM card terms explicitly identify a card issuer and market-specific prepaid purchase instrument. The sample Academy product visibly displays its own ticket price and add-to-cart flow; the event page advertises group cards; the market navigation links outward for delivery. These are different payment or information surfaces, not evidence of one universal commercial contract. Stand-alone FAQ, market overview, TOM card terms, Academy store, events and groups.

Unknown until more direct source evidence:

whether counter payments settle to each outlet, the venue or a shared processor;

whether ordinary diners can pay cash at a given counter today;

whether the general TOM card and advertised group event cards share one contract;

how group cards are issued, accepted, replenished or refunded;

which party holds workshop versus counter versus event attendee information;

whether a delivery order is a market, vendor or platform responsibility;

and how payment disputes or failed fulfilment are routed.

A shared brand, common counter layout or card does not establish data ownership, contract ownership or first-party guest-relationship control.
