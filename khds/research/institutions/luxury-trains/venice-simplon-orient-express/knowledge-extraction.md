## Case purpose

Case #38 tests whether the provisional Mobile / Multi-System Hospitality Architecture survives outside cruise hospitality in a luxury overnight rail institution.

It also tests whether Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture gains meaningful pressure when accommodation tier changes not only space but also service, dining, planning and transfer relationships.

Primary falsification question:

Does luxury overnight rail hospitality create a genuinely distinct responsibility architecture because cabin accommodation, dining, steward service, luggage custody, station transfers, railway infrastructure, immigration, timetable disruption and destination components cross different operational boundaries, or can the observed structure be sufficiently explained as ordinary bundled passenger transport with premium onboard hospitality?

## Falsification requirements

### Journey Continuity Across External Operating Control

Would weaken if railway, transfer, excursion and hospitality components are operationally isolated and failures do not materially propagate across them.

### Transport-Embedded Hospitality

Would weaken if railway state rarely changes hospitality responsibility.

### Cabin-Tier Service Relationship

Would weaken if service additions are ordinary upsell benefits without meaningful differences in responsibility, permissions, information or recovery.

### Luggage Custody State

Would weaken if luggage distinctions have no meaningful operational consequence for guest access, responsibility or recovery.

### Schedule Change / Hospitality Consequence

Would weaken if timetable change has no meaningful effect on dining, transfers, luggage, accommodation or destination commitments.

### Cross-Provider Guest Information Continuity

Would weaken if relevant providers operate independently without shared guest-information dependencies.

### Persistent Departure Responsibility

Would weaken if all meaningful obligations end at physical disembarkation.

## Contradiction record

### One branded journey without one operating owner

### Included dining without one dining state

Meals can be included while seating time and service format remain differentiated.

### Premium cabin without only a room upgrade

### Scheduled journey without fully controlled timing

The operator sells a scheduled journey whose timing remains exposed to railway infrastructure.

### Guest luggage without continuous guest possession

Through luggage can remain inaccessible during the journey.

### Physical departure without complete closure

Luggage, transfers, complaints and other obligations can persist after disembarkation.

### Heritage continuity without operational-continuity proof

The narrative remains continuous while the operating environment changes.

### One guest journey without one proven data system

Relevant information may need to move across providers, but the public evidence does not establish one synchronized data architecture.
