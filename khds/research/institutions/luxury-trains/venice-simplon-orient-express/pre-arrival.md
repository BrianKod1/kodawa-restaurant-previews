# Pre-arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What responsibilities must the guest and institution resolve before departure, including documents, luggage, dietary needs, mobility, transfers and journey-specific preparation?

## Pre-arrival is operational

For Case #38, pre-arrival preparation is not only informational.

A traveller may need to resolve:

- passport and visa requirements;
- luggage categories;
- cabin-luggage limits;
- through-luggage separation;
- dietary requirements;
- mobility or special-assistance needs;
- dress expectations;
- transfer arrangements;
- journey timing;
- accommodation-specific service arrangements. [S5][S7][S8][S10]

A confirmed reservation therefore does not automatically create a journey-ready guest.

Confirmed booking
!=
departure readiness.

## Falsification relevance

Pre-arrival is one of the strongest places to test whether Case #38 is genuinely multi-system.

If the journey requires materially different parties to receive, preserve and act on different guest information before departure, the architecture signal becomes stronger.

If one operator simply packages routine rail-travel preparation and premium-hotel requests, the conventional bundled-transport explanation remains sufficient.
