# Stay

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do cabin class, private and shared facilities, steward service, railway movement and ancillary service entitlements coexist during the onboard stay?

## A moving accommodation environment

The stay occurs inside an institution that is simultaneously:

- accommodation;
- transport;
- dining environment;
- social venue;
- heritage environment;
- service workplace;
- and passenger carriage.

That makes the guest's condition dependent on both hospitality operations and railway movement. [S1][S3][S6][S9]

The central Case #38 question is whether those systems are operationally integrated or merely bundled.

## Accommodation hierarchy

The published accommodation structure distinguishes:

### Historic Cabins

Historic Cabins represent the traditional sleeper configuration and use shared restroom facilities. [S6]

This means:

Private sleeping space
!=
fully private accommodation infrastructure.

### Suites

Suites add private en-suite facilities and more self-contained accommodation. [S6]

### Grand Suites

Grand Suites add larger residential space and materially different services, including:

- private dining;
- 24-hour butler service;
- personalised pre-journey planning;
- private arrival and departure transfers. [S7]

### L'Observatoire

L'Observatoire creates an entire private-carriage state rather than only a larger cabin. [S8]

This supports:

Cabin hierarchy
!=
space hierarchy only.

## Railway dependency

Unlike a stationary hotel, the accommodation state is embedded in movement.

Published timings remain subject to railway constraints. [S3][S4]

This means that hospitality conditions can potentially be affected by:

- railway delay;
- route change;
- infrastructure failure;
- border timing;
- station sequencing;
- external carrier conditions.

The public evidence does not establish how cabin or service commitments are recovered if railway conditions alter the planned journey.
