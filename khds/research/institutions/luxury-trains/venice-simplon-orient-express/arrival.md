# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Where does institutional responsibility begin across accommodation transfer, station arrival, luggage handoff, identity or document checks and boarding?

## Arrival is a chain, not one moment

For Venice Simplon-Orient-Express, arrival cannot be reduced to walking through a hotel entrance.

Depending on the journey and accommodation tier, the guest may move through:

1. accommodation or pickup point;
2. transfer vehicle;
3. railway station;
4. check-in or luggage handoff;
5. platform;
6. carriage;
7. cabin;
8. onboard service environment.

Public evidence establishes several of these states. [S3][S5][S7][S8]

It does not establish one universal operational owner across them.

Therefore:

Arrival
!=
one responsibility handoff.

## Transfer state

The inspected Paris-Venice journey includes transfers between accommodation and the railway station as part of the published journey. [S3]

Grand Suites and L'Observatoire can add private transfer entitlements. [S7][S8]

This creates at least two transfer questions:

- whether transfer is included for the selected product;
- who owns execution and recovery if that transfer fails.

The public evidence establishes inclusion.

It does not establish:

- dispatch ownership;
- driver assignment;
- tracking;
- delay recovery;
- missed pickup handling;
- luggage custody inside the transfer;
- coordination with railway departure time.

Transfer included
!=
transfer execution verified.

## Station transition

A rail-hospitality institution depends on infrastructure that is not equivalent to a private hotel lobby.

The journey enters:

- public or controlled railway space;
- station access rules;
- platform timing;
- railway operating constraints;
- other carrier infrastructure.

The legal material distinguishes VSOE from railway carriers and other providers. [S10][S11]

Therefore:

One guest journey
!=
one physical operating environment.

The exact station check-in process was not directly observed.

## Luggage handoff

The FAQ distinguishes three luggage states:

- hand luggage;
- cabin luggage;
- through luggage. [S5]

Cabin luggage may be taken during check-in and delivered to the guest's cabin.

Through luggage enters a materially different state because it is not expected to remain accessible during the journey. [S5]

This creates:

Guest owns luggage
!=
guest possesses luggage continuously.

And:

Luggage accepted
!=
one uniform custody state.

The public evidence does not establish the complete custody chain.

## Cabin access

Boarding the train does not necessarily mean every service state has already been activated.

A guest still transitions into:

- assigned accommodation;
- steward relationship;
- shared dining;
- luggage state;
- journey-specific service entitlement.

Different cabin classes also produce materially different arrival relationships.

Grand Suites can include private transfers and enhanced personalised service. [S7]

L'Observatoire creates an even more private arrival and carriage relationship. [S8]

Therefore:

Boarded passenger
!=
uniformly situated guest.

## Documentation boundary

Passport and visa readiness remains a guest responsibility under the inspected EEA/UK booking conditions. [S10]

The current evidence does not establish:

- when documents are checked;
- who performs each check;
- what information VSOE verifies;
- what railway or border authorities verify;
- what happens if documentation is incomplete;
- whether the guest can remain in another journey state after a documentation failure.

This distinction matters because:

Document obligation
!=
document-control ownership.

## External infrastructure dependency

Published journey timings are described as approximate and subject to national railway constraints. [S3][S4]

The guest may therefore arrive correctly while the infrastructure state changes.

This creates:

Guest ready
!=
train ready.

Train ready
!=
network unconstrained.

A hospitality recovery model must therefore distinguish guest-caused and infrastructure-caused disruption.

The current evidence does not show that recovery process.

## Responsibility stack under test

Potential arrival responsibility owners include:

- guest;
- transfer provider;
- luggage handler;
- station operator;
- railway carrier;
- VSOE;
- onboard steward or butler;
- other journey suppliers.

The public evidence supports the existence of several parties. [S10][S11]

It does not establish exact responsibility assignment for every arrival event.

## Failure scenarios not yet evidenced

The current public evidence does not establish what happens if:

- a transfer arrives late;
- the guest reaches the wrong station;
- station access changes;
- the train is delayed;
- luggage is misrouted;
- cabin luggage is unavailable;
- a guest misses boarding;
- documents are insufficient;
- mobility assistance fails;
- a premium transfer does not appear;
- cabin assignment is unavailable.

These remain operational questions.

## Architecture pressure

Arrival strengthens the provisional multi-system hypothesis because the guest journey crosses several control domains before the stay is fully active.

However:

Many handoffs
!=
distinct architecture.

Conventional rail travel also contains:

- station handoffs;
- baggage;
- carriers;
- schedules;
- boarding.

The stronger KHDS question is whether hospitality responsibility remains coherent across those handoffs.
