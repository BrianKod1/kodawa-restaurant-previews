# Departure

## Research question

How are final settlement, disembarkation, luggage, onward travel and unresolved service responsibilities closed at the end of a Scarlet Lady voyage?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S7 — Onboard Payments FAQ
- S20 — Disembarkation FAQ
- S21 — Post Voyage Support FAQ

Supporting evidence:

- S2 — Port Information FAQ
- S15 — Sailor Services FAQ

This file documents the published end-of-voyage process. It does not establish actual queue duration, customs processing time, billing accuracy or successful post-voyage recovery.

## Departure is not one moment

The end of the voyage contains several separate closure events:

1. final onboard spending;
2. folio review;
3. payment settlement;
4. disembarkation-slot selection;
5. luggage preparation and collection;
6. customs or immigration processing;
7. onward travel;
8. post-voyage issue recovery.

For KHDS, departure should therefore be treated as a sequence of handoffs rather than a single exit.

## Disembarkation timing

Virgin states that final disembarkation can extend through 10:45 a.m.

Sailors are told to refer to their booking confirmation and Sailor App for the applicable timing.

Virgin also states that disembarkation cannot begin until local authorities clear the ship.

That creates a dependency on an external authority.

The ship may be physically alongside the terminal while guest exit is still unavailable.

## Disembarkation slots

Virgin allows Sailors to select a disembarkation time slot in the Sailor App.

The published route is:

Book a Shore Thing
→ final port of call
→ Disembarkation Group
→ choose time.

Virgin also says reminders are sent if the Sailor has not yet selected a slot.

This gives departure the same broad structure seen at arrival:

**institution-wide operating window**

plus

**individual scheduled access time.**

## Earliest exit

Virgin states that departure begins once local authorities grant clearance.

This means the earliest practical exit is not determined solely by the Sailor or ship schedule.

It depends on:

ship arrival
→ authority clearance
→ guest release.

That distinction matters when planning onward travel.

## Onward flights

Virgin recommends allowing enough time for:

- disembarkation;
- customs;
- travel to the airport;
- airport procedures.

The operator also notes that home-port arrival timing can vary by itinerary.

This is intentionally non-specific.

Public evidence does not establish a universal safe flight-departure interval for every port.

KHDS should therefore avoid converting this into a fixed rule.

## Final folio

S7 states that Sailors can monitor their folio and onboard purchases during the voyage through the Virgin Voyages App.

On the final day, Virgin sends a finalized folio by email.

The finalized folio can also remain available in the app after disembarkation.

That creates continuity beyond the ship itself:

onboard account
→ final folio
→ email/app record
→ post-voyage review.

## Final payment

Virgin states that the final bill is automatically charged to the active credit card on file.

Alternatively, Sailors can settle some or all of the bill in cash through Sailor Services on Deck 5.

Onboard purchases made through The Band are added to the Sailor's folio and ultimately charged through the settlement process.

This makes final settlement dependent on the synchronization of:

Band activity
→ onboard account
→ folio
→ payment method.

## Authorization hold

Virgin states that a $250 USD authorization hold is placed on the card on file at embarkation.

The operator says only the final folio total is posted on disembarkation day.

Release timing for the hold depends on the card provider.

This creates a responsibility boundary between:

Virgin settlement
and
bank/card-provider release timing.

A guest may therefore experience a post-voyage financial effect that Virgin does not directly control.

## Billing disputes

Virgin instructs Sailors with onboard billing concerns to visit Sailor Services on Deck 5.

This makes pre-disembarkation review important.

The current evidence does not establish:

- whether all disputed charges must be resolved before leaving;
- the exact post-voyage dispute route for every billing issue;
- response times;
- refund processing time;
- chargeback or card-provider escalation behavior.

Those remain unresolved.

## Luggage

Departure also involves physical property handoff.

Current Case #26 evidence establishes that luggage is institution-managed during embarkation, but the current source set does not yet fully document standard final-night luggage collection and terminal retrieval for every home port.

Virgin's Seattle Alaska operations introduce a special case: Port Valet can transfer eligible luggage directly from the ship toward the departing flight.

That service is port-specific and must not be generalized to all Scarlet Lady itineraries.

The standard disembarkation luggage process remains a research gap.

## Accessibility at departure

Virgin states that wheelchair assistance is available at home ports on disembarkation day.

As at embarkation, the published boundary is between the gangway and terminal entrance.

This means accessibility responsibility changes again as the guest leaves the ship.

The evidence does not establish:

- wait time;
- queue priority;
- baggage assistance;
- continuity into ground transportation;
- onward airport assistance.

## Lost property after leaving

S21 provides a post-voyage recovery route for property left onboard.

Virgin instructs former Sailors to submit a Lost & Found form.

The guest can then track the status of the request.

This is significant because the service relationship does not end completely at the gangway.

Some recovery functions persist after physical departure.

No evidence establishes recovery rate or time.

## Final identity transition

During the voyage, the guest is actively represented through:

- The Band;
- cabin;
- Sailor App;
- onboard account;
- ship systems.

At final departure, some of those relationships terminate while others persist.

For example:

- cabin access ends;
- active onboard use ends;
- folio records remain accessible;
- billing can still have downstream effects;
- Lost & Found can continue;
- post-voyage support remains available.

Departure is therefore an identity and responsibility transition, not simply a physical exit.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports these bounded findings:

1. Final disembarkation can extend through 10:45 a.m.
2. Sailors can select a disembarkation slot in the app.
3. Ship clearance by local authorities controls when exit can begin.
4. Virgin recommends allowing time for customs, transport and airport procedures.
5. Folio activity can be viewed during the voyage.
6. A finalized folio is emailed and remains viewable after disembarkation.
7. The final bill is charged to the active card unless another settlement route is used.
8. A card authorization hold may remain subject to the card provider's release timing.
9. Sailor Services handles onboard billing concerns.
10. Post-voyage Lost & Found continues after the guest leaves the ship.
11. Home-port accessibility assistance extends through the disembarkation transition.

## What remains unresolved

The current evidence does not establish:

- standard luggage-offloading and terminal-collection procedure across home ports;
- customs wait time;
- immigration processing time;
- missed disembarkation-slot handling;
- post-voyage billing-dispute procedure in detail;
- refund time;
- erroneous folio correction after leaving the ship;
- lost-property recovery performance;
- onward-travel protection when disembarkation is delayed;
- continuity between terminal accessibility assistance and later transportation.

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady's departure process closes several systems at different times.

A useful model is:

final onboard use
→ folio review
→ payment settlement
→ scheduled disembarkation
→ authority clearance
→ physical exit
→ luggage/customs
→ onward travel
→ post-voyage recovery.

The guest may be physically off the ship while financial or property issues remain unresolved.

That means:

**physical departure is not necessarily service closure.**

This is an important Case #26 finding.

The institution's responsibility may continue digitally or administratively after the guest has crossed the gangway.

## Evidence rating

Disembarkation timing, slot selection, folio settlement and post-voyage Lost & Found are documented.

The full luggage, customs, billing-recovery and onward-travel chain remains incomplete.
