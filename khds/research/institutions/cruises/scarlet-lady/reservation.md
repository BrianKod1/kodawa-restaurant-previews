# Reservation

## Research question

Which pre-voyage choices can a Scarlet Lady Sailor reserve, when do they unlock, and how do fare, accommodation class and service type affect access?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S3 — Virgin Voyages My Account FAQ
- S4 — Virgin Voyages Dining FAQ
- S8 — Virgin Voyages Shore Things FAQ
- S11 — Virgin Voyages Shore Things Terms and Conditions

Supporting evidence:

- S1 — Scarlet Lady ship page
- S5 — Sailor App FAQ

This file documents published reservation structure. It does not establish actual inventory availability, booking success, waitlist performance or service fulfilment.

## Reservation is not one system

Scarlet Lady exposes multiple reservation layers with different timing rules.

The current public evidence distinguishes at least three timing classes:

1. voyage and accommodation booking;
2. pre-voyage dining and Shore Things reservations;
3. experiences that become reservable only after embarkation.

The guest therefore does not receive access to all voyage inventory at one moment.

That sequencing matters because it creates a temporal dependency between purchase, fare type, cabin class, app/account use and later access to scarce onboard experiences.

## Dining reservation windows

For bookings made on or after 7 October 2025, Virgin publishes dining access according to the selected fare option and accommodation class:

- Lock It In or Base, Standard Cabins — 15 days before sailing;
- Essential, Standard Cabins — 45 days before sailing;
- Premium, Standard Cabins — 60 days before sailing;
- RockStar and Mega RockStar Quarters — 120 days before sailing.

S3 and S4 therefore establish that guests on the same voyage may receive materially different advance access to dining inventory.

This is a documented access distinction, not evidence that later-opening guests necessarily receive worse dining outcomes.

## Other onboard experiences

Virgin states that other onboard experiences — including spa treatments, entertainment and tattoos — are generally available to reserve once the Sailor is onboard.

This creates a second reservation boundary.

A guest may be able to organize dining weeks or months before sailing while still needing to wait until embarkation to reserve other activities.

The intended guest journey therefore contains both:

- pre-arrival reservation planning; and
- onboard reservation competition.

No evidence currently establishes how scarce those onboard experiences become after embarkation.

## Shore Things

Virgin publishes a different window for Shore Things.

S8 states that:

- all Sailors may generally book Shore Things 120 days before the voyage begins;
- Sailors in RockStar Quarters receive access 135 days before voyage start;
- payment is due at booking;
- booking is available through the Sailor App, My Account, Sailor Services or a travel partner.

This means excursion access follows its own reservation clock rather than the dining clock.

The practical effect is that one voyage can contain multiple reservation calendars that a Sailor must understand and monitor.

## Modification and cancellation

Virgin publishes a general Shore Things cancellation deadline of 48 hours before the scheduled service unless the individual Shore Thing states a different rule.

Rescheduling is also published as available before the applicable cancellation deadline unless the excursion's own Need to Know information says otherwise.

For dining, Virgin states that reservations can be managed in the Sailor App and through onboard restaurant or Sailor Services channels.

These public rules establish available channels. They do not establish:

- response times;
- successful modification;
- refund timing in every case;
- inventory recovery after cancellation;
- whether an alternative experience remains available.

## Availability and waitlists

Virgin explicitly states that dining space is limited and recommends booking when the applicable reservation window opens.

If preferred dining inventory is unavailable, guests are advised to:

- continue checking for cancellations;
- check for additional inventory;
- once onboard, visit the restaurant or Sailor Services to join a waitlist.

Virgin also states that additional seats are not guaranteed.

This is important for KHDS because the reservation system includes a recovery pathway but does not promise recovery.

A waitlist should therefore be treated as:

**an opportunity mechanism, not a confirmed entitlement.**

## Fare and accommodation asymmetry

Case #26 introduces a strong distinction between:

**purchase entitlement**

and

**reservation opportunity.**

Two Sailors may both hold valid accommodation on Scarlet Lady while receiving different advance access windows to dining or Shore Things because of fare or accommodation class.

The public evidence does not establish that this produces unequal completed service.

It does establish unequal timing of access to reservation inventory.

KHDS should preserve that distinction.

## Cross-channel reservation architecture

Reservation activity can occur across several guest-facing channels:

- VirginVoyages.com / My Account;
- Sailor App;
- travel advisor / First Mate;
- Sailor Services;
- restaurant staff onboard;
- Shore Things desk onboard.

This creates potential continuity benefits because guests have fallback channels.

It also creates potential synchronization pressure because changes made through one channel must remain visible and actionable through others.

No synchronization test has been performed.

## Third-party responsibility boundary

Shore Things create a particularly important reservation boundary.

Virgin permits Shore Things to be purchased through Virgin-controlled channels, including its website, app, Sailor Services and onboard systems.

However, S11 states that Virgin does not act on behalf of, supervise or control the third-party parties who own, furnish or operate those excursions.

For KHDS, the guest-facing journey therefore contains a distinction between:

**commercial coordination**

and

**operational control.**

The guest may experience the reservation as part of one Virgin journey even though service execution crosses into a separate operator responsibility domain.

This boundary should be tested further in the port-transition and knowledge-extraction work.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports the following bounded findings:

1. Dining reservation windows differ by fare and accommodation class.
2. Some onboard experiences cannot be reserved until embarkation.
3. Shore Things use a separate advance-booking window.
4. RockStar status can create earlier Shore Things access.
5. Dining capacity is limited and waitlist recovery is not guaranteed.
6. Reservation management occurs across multiple digital and human channels.
7. Shore Things can be sold through Virgin channels while being operated by third parties outside Virgin's direct supervision or control.

## What remains unresolved

The current research has not established:

- live inventory levels;
- booking completion rates;
- whether favored time slots disappear before later windows open;
- app/account synchronization reliability;
- payment failure recovery;
- excursion modification execution;
- dining waitlist conversion;
- cancellation/refund timing in practice;
- whether reservation rules are consistently surfaced during purchase;
- whether guests understand third-party responsibility before buying Shore Things.

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady does not appear to have a single reservation moment.

Instead, the voyage contains a sequence of access gates.

A useful provisional model is:

voyage purchase
→ entitlement established
→ timed reservation windows open
→ inventory selection
→ modification / cancellation
→ onboard fallback
→ service execution

That model produces several possible failure points even when the original voyage booking itself is valid.

The most important distinction at this stage is:

**having purchased the voyage is not the same as having secured every desired experience within the voyage.**

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

The operator clearly documents reservation windows, channels, cancellation rules and some fallback mechanisms.

The evidence does not establish actual inventory conditions, successful recovery or operational outcomes.
