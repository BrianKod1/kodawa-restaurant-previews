# Discovery

## Research question

What can a prospective Sailor establish about Scarlet Lady, its current offer and the physical/digital structure of the ship before booking?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S1 — Scarlet Lady ship page

Supporting current operator context:

- S2 — Port Information
- S3 — My Account

The discovery rating in `coverage.json` is intentionally bounded to public identity and offer representation. It does not rate actual booking usability, onboard navigation or service performance.

## Institution identity

Virgin Voyages presents Scarlet Lady as part of its current operating fleet and continues to publish future deployment for the vessel.

Current Virgin material describes Scarlet Lady as the brand's flagship vessel and confirms future itinerary deployment beyond the present season.

The institution studied here is therefore an active cruise-ship hospitality system rather than a historical or closed case.

## Offer structure

The public Scarlet Lady page exposes several major parts of the experience before booking or sailing:

- itineraries;
- ship spaces;
- cabins and quarters;
- dining;
- entertainment;
- visual gallery material;
- deck-plan orientation;
- refreshed onboard spaces and programming.

This is significant for KHDS because discovery is not limited to choosing a room or destination. The prospective guest is being asked to understand a temporary multi-day environment containing accommodation, food, entertainment, mobility, destination transitions and digital service systems.

## Physical orientation before arrival

Virgin provides 3D deck-plan material intended to help users understand where cabins, restaurants, bars, pool areas and other ship spaces are located.

That creates a pre-arrival orientation layer that is more consequential than a typical static venue map because the guest will live inside the institution for multiple days.

The presence of the deck plan does not establish that guests can successfully navigate the ship after embarkation. No real wayfinding test has been performed.

## Digital discovery boundary

The public website explains the institution, but later stages of the journey increasingly shift into account and app systems.

Virgin's account guidance indicates that account users can:

- access booking information;
- make payments;
- book add-ons and experiences;
- complete Ready to Sail information.

The Port Information FAQ separately directs Sailors to use the app for check-in and Terminal Arrival Time selection.

This suggests a progressive channel shift:

public website → account → app → terminal → ship systems

KHDS should treat that sequence as a potential continuity mechanism rather than assuming each channel operates independently.

## Current proposition

The public proposition is broader than transportation.

Scarlet Lady is presented as an integrated adults-only leisure environment combining:

- accommodation;
- dining;
- entertainment;
- social spaces;
- destinations;
- onboard services;
- digital planning;
- port transitions.

That breadth increases the amount of information a prospective Sailor may need to understand before committing to the voyage.

## What is clear from public evidence

A prospective Sailor can establish that:

1. Scarlet Lady is an active Virgin Voyages ship.
2. Virgin publishes current and future itinerary material for the vessel.
3. The ship is presented as an integrated accommodation, dining, entertainment and destination experience.
4. Public deck-plan material is available for spatial orientation.
5. Booking management and preparation later move into account/app systems.

## What remains unresolved

Public evidence inspected so far does not establish:

- how easily a new user can compare Scarlet Lady against another Virgin ship;
- how clearly mandatory versus optional costs are distinguished across the discovery journey;
- whether mobile and desktop presentation expose the same practical information;
- whether deck plans are understandable to first-time cruise guests;
- whether cabin-category differences are easy to interpret;
- whether accessibility needs are visible early enough in discovery;
- whether itinerary changes are consistently reflected across all channels;
- how much of the journey can be understood without creating an account or installing the app.

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady creates a discovery problem that is partly spatial and partly systemic.

The guest is not simply choosing a place to stay. They are choosing entry into a temporary operating system whose rules, locations and service channels continue to unfold after purchase.

A useful KHDS distinction is therefore:

**offer discovery** — understanding what the voyage promises;

versus

**system discovery** — understanding how the guest will actually operate inside the voyage.

The current public evidence is strong enough for the first question but incomplete for the second.

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

Scarlet Lady's identity, current public offer and broad physical/digital structure are documented, but important discovery and usability questions remain unresolved.

It does not mean:

- booking is easy;
- the app is reliable;
- the deck plan produces successful wayfinding;
- pricing is transparent;
- guests understand the service system;
- the onboard experience matches the marketing.

Those remain separate research questions.
