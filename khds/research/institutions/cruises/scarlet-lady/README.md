# Scarlet Lady — Case #26

Virgin Voyages · Adults-only cruise ship / integrated hospitality vessel · Official operator: Virgin Voyages.

## Scope and dates

Public-source study of Scarlet Lady's discovery, reservation, pre-arrival preparation, embarkation, onboard stay, dining, departure, atmosphere, photography and institutional story.

Research access date: 2026-09-23.

This is not an onboard audit, booking test, accessibility assessment, safety inspection, transaction test or proof of guest outcomes. Published operator instructions are treated as statements about intended service design unless execution is independently observed.

Case #26 is the sixth institution in the post-Case-20 comparison sequence and the first cruise-ship institution in the current KHDS research set. It follows hotel, wellness, shared-venue and hostel/social-stay cases and introduces a mobile hospitality institution whose location, service environment and responsibility boundaries change throughout a multi-day journey.

## Research role

Scarlet Lady was selected to test whether KHDS findings transfer into an environment where accommodation, dining, entertainment, payments, reservations, identity, navigation, shore transitions and departure must be coordinated across one moving institution.

The central research question is:

> How does a hospitality system maintain continuity when guest identity, physical location, reservations, service access and responsibility boundaries repeatedly change during one multi-day journey?

This case pays particular attention to the relationship between:

- pre-voyage account and app preparation;
- terminal arrival and embarkation;
- The Band as cabin-access and payment credential;
- onboard reservations and service channels;
- dining access and waitlist recovery;
- accessibility and advance-assistance requirements;
- shore-excursion and port-day responsibility boundaries;
- onboard financial settlement;
- final disembarkation and return to land-side travel.

## Public-evidence position

The current evidence set establishes a substantial intended operating model but does not establish actual execution.

Virgin publishes:

- Ready to Sail check-in and Terminal Arrival Time selection through the app;
- general boarding guidance;
- differentiated dining reservation windows based on fare and accommodation class;
- onboard-only reservation timing for some experiences;
- cabin access and touchless payments through The Band;
- personal onboard accounts for purchases;
- accessibility and medical-request channels;
- Shore Things booking and port-transition guidance;
- current Scarlet Lady itinerary and ship-positioning material.

These materials show how Virgin says the system should work. They do not demonstrate successful check-in, reservation availability, accessibility fulfilment, app reliability, service recovery, dining waitlist performance, billing accuracy or shore-excursion execution.

## Initial KHDS pressure

Scarlet Lady appears to place unusually high coordination pressure on a small number of shared guest-facing systems.

The Sailor's identity may need to remain coherent across:

1. online account;
2. Sailor App;
3. terminal check-in;
4. The Band;
5. cabin access;
6. onboard account;
7. dining reservations;
8. onboard experiences;
9. Sailor Services;
10. port and Shore Things transitions.

A failure in one layer could therefore propagate across multiple parts of the journey. This is a research hypothesis, not an observed failure.

## Current limitations

The current research has not yet tested:

- live booking or account flows;
- app reliability or offline fallback;
- terminal queues or missed-arrival recovery;
- Band issuance, replacement or failure recovery;
- onboard wayfinding performance;
- dining inventory or waitlist execution;
- housekeeping or cabin-service execution;
- accessibility fulfilment;
- shore-excursion operator performance;
- billing disputes or final folio closure;
- disembarkation sequencing;
- guest outcomes.

Those limits remain explicit throughout the dossier.
