# Dining

## Research question

How are dining discovery, reservation access, spontaneous alternatives, in-cabin dining and dietary needs represented and coordinated aboard Scarlet Lady?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S3 — My Account FAQ
- S4 — Dining FAQ
- S10 — Accessibility FAQ
- S18 — Cruise Dining
- S19 — Dietary Requirements

Supporting evidence:

- S1 — Scarlet Lady ship page
- S5 — Sailor App FAQ

This file documents published dining architecture. It does not establish actual meal quality, wait times, food safety, inventory or successful dietary accommodation.

## Dining as a distributed system

Virgin does not present Scarlet Lady dining as one central dining room.

Its current dining material describes more than 20 eateries and explicitly positions the ships as having no traditional buffet or large main dining hall.

That means dining is distributed across:

- reservable signature restaurants;
- walk-in venues;
- grab-and-go options;
- food-hall style service;
- in-cabin Ship Eats;
- bars and specialty spaces.

For KHDS, dining is therefore a network of service modes rather than one restaurant operation.

## Included dining proposition

S18 states that dining across the ship's eateries is included in the voyage proposition.

The Scarlet Lady ship page also names current venues and highlights Lucky Lotus by Razzle Dazzle among the refreshed dining offer.

This supports the public proposition that dining is a core part of the voyage rather than a secondary add-on.

It does not establish:

- equal availability across venues;
- whether premium items carry additional charges;
- seat availability at preferred times;
- portion or service quality;
- actual menu consistency.

## Reservation-based dining

Signature dining operates through reservation windows.

For bookings made on or after 7 October 2025, published dining access differs by fare and accommodation class:

- Lock It In or Base, Standard Cabins — 15 days before sailing;
- Essential, Standard Cabins — 45 days;
- Premium, Standard Cabins — 60 days;
- RockStar and Mega RockStar Quarters — 120 days.

This means the same voyage can contain different advance-access windows for the same dining inventory.

That difference is a timing distinction, not proof of unequal completed dining outcomes.

## Walk-in and non-reservation alternatives

Virgin's dining FAQ identifies several options that do not require reservations, including:

- The Galley;
- The Dock / Dock House;
- Sun Club Café;
- The Pizza Place;
- The Social Club;
- Quickeze;
- Ship Eats.

This is important because it provides an alternative service layer when reservable restaurants are full or when the guest prefers flexibility.

The existence of alternatives does not establish equivalent menu choice, service environment or convenience.

## Waitlist and recovery

Virgin states that dining space is limited.

When preferred reservations are unavailable, Sailors may:

- continue checking the app for cancellations;
- check later for additional inventory;
- once onboard, approach restaurants or Sailor Services about waitlist options.

Virgin explicitly states that waitlist seating remains subject to availability.

This creates a documented recovery mechanism without guaranteeing recovery.

KHDS should distinguish:

**fallback route exists**

from

**desired dining experience is recovered.**

## Ship Eats

Virgin advertises Ship Eats as a 24/7 in-cabin dining option.

The Sailor App FAQ also shows that Ship Eats depends on connection to the ship Wi-Fi.

If Ship Eats does not work, Virgin directs Sailors to:

1. confirm Wi-Fi connection;
2. restart the app;
3. contact Sailor Services if the issue remains.

Dining therefore intersects with the digital-service architecture studied in `stay.md`.

A network or app problem can become a food-service access problem.

No Ship Eats delivery-time or order-accuracy evidence has been inspected.

## Dietary needs

Virgin publishes a dedicated dietary-requirements policy.

S19 states that Sailors with special medical diets or food allergies should contact Sailor Services before the voyage.

Virgin asks for special dietary requests to be submitted at least 45 days before sailing.

For requests made with less notice, Virgin says it will make best efforts but cannot guarantee accommodation.

That creates a pre-arrival dependency for some dining needs.

## Allergen limits

Virgin explicitly states that its ships do not have allergen-free kitchens.

The operator also states that:

- it is not an allergen-free environment;
- cross-contact exposure remains possible;
- accommodation may vary because ships are supplied in different regions.

This is important because a published accommodation route must not be interpreted as a safety guarantee.

The evidence supports:

**dietary support process**

not

**zero-risk dietary execution.**

## Available and unavailable special diets

Virgin states that it can arrange some specific medical diet accommodations, including no-sugar and low-sodium meals upon request.

Current dietary guidance also states that Halal and Kosher dining options are not offered.

This is a concrete service boundary rather than an inference.

## Guest-provided food

Virgin states that Crew members are prohibited from storing, preparing, cooking or reheating food brought onboard by Sailors.

That means a guest cannot assume that bringing their own food creates a kitchen-service fallback.

For certain dietary needs, this narrows the available recovery options.

## Restaurant-level handoff

The dietary FAQ states that Crew can help guests understand available choices.

The actual guest journey may therefore include:

pre-voyage dietary disclosure
→ onboard dining venue
→ server / Crew discussion
→ menu selection
→ kitchen preparation
→ service

Each stage can involve a different person or team.

The public evidence does not establish whether a pre-voyage dietary request is automatically visible to every dining venue.

That continuity remains a major unresolved question.

## Dining availability versus dining entitlement

Scarlet Lady creates an important distinction between:

**included dining**

and

**guaranteed access to a particular venue/time.**

The voyage may include dining broadly while specific signature restaurants remain capacity-constrained.

This is similar to the Case #26 reservation distinction:

purchase
does not automatically equal
preferred inventory.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports these bounded findings:

1. Scarlet Lady uses a distributed multi-venue dining model.
2. Virgin advertises 20+ eateries rather than a traditional buffet/main-dining-room model.
3. Signature dining uses timed advance reservation windows.
4. Reservation timing differs by fare/accommodation.
5. Several dining alternatives do not require reservations.
6. Ship Eats provides 24/7 in-cabin dining and depends on ship Wi-Fi/app access.
7. Waitlist recovery is possible but not guaranteed.
8. Special medical diets and allergies have a published pre-voyage request route.
9. Virgin asks for dietary requests at least 45 days in advance.
10. Virgin does not operate allergen-free kitchens.
11. Halal and Kosher dining are not currently offered.
12. Crew may not store, prepare or reheat guest-provided food.

## What remains unresolved

The current evidence does not establish:

- restaurant capacity by voyage;
- waitlist conversion rates;
- actual wait times;
- menu stock-outs;
- Ship Eats delivery times;
- order accuracy;
- dietary-request transfer between pre-voyage and restaurant teams;
- cross-contamination controls in practice;
- premium-item pricing;
- restaurant-specific cancellation behavior;
- whether reservation asymmetry materially affects completed dining access.

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady's dining system combines:

advance reservation
→ limited inventory
→ walk-in alternatives
→ app-mediated delivery
→ dietary accommodation
→ human fallback.

This gives the institution several resilience paths.

It also creates several coordination dependencies.

A useful KHDS question is:

**When the preferred dining path fails, does the alternative preserve the guest's underlying need or only provide some form of food access?**

For example, a walk-in venue may solve hunger but not recover:

- a preferred experience;
- a dietary requirement;
- a social reservation;
- a specific dining time.

KHDS should therefore evaluate dining recovery at the level of the guest need, not merely whether another food source exists.

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

The public dining architecture, reservation system and dietary limitations are documented in meaningful detail.

Availability, execution, safety performance and recovery outcomes remain unverified.
