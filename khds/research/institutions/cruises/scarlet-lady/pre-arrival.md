# Pre-arrival

## Research question

What must a Scarlet Lady Sailor prepare before reaching the terminal, including check-in, identity, accessibility, medical needs and special-assistance requests?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S2 — Virgin Voyages Port Information FAQ
- S3 — Virgin Voyages My Account FAQ
- S10 — Virgin Voyages Accessibility FAQ
- S12 — Virgin Voyages Accessibility page

Supporting evidence:

- S5 — Sailor App FAQ

This file describes published preparation requirements and assistance channels. It does not establish successful document verification, accommodation fulfilment or terminal execution.

## Pre-arrival is an operational stage

Scarlet Lady's guest journey begins before the Sailor reaches the port.

Current Virgin guidance requires or strongly depends on several preparatory actions:

- accessing the booking/account;
- using the Sailor App;
- completing Ready to Sail check-in;
- selecting a Terminal Arrival Time;
- preparing required travel documentation;
- arranging accessibility or medical accommodations where relevant;
- understanding port-specific embarkation instructions.

The pre-arrival stage therefore determines whether several later transitions can occur smoothly.

## Ready to Sail and app preparation

Virgin directs Sailors to complete Ready to Sail check-in through the Sailor App.

The app is also used for Terminal Arrival Time selection and later voyage-management functions.

That creates an early digital dependency.

The public evidence establishes the intended workflow but does not establish:

- app compatibility across devices;
- account-recovery performance;
- offline fallback;
- failed-document-upload recovery;
- assistance for Sailors unable to use the app independently.

Those remain unresolved.

## Terminal Arrival Time

Virgin instructs Sailors to select and follow a Terminal Arrival Time rather than treating the published general boarding time as unrestricted arrival access.

This distinction matters.

A published general boarding start does not mean every Sailor is expected to arrive at that time.

The operative planning unit is the Sailor's assigned or selected arrival slot.

For KHDS, this separates:

**institution opening time**

from

**individual access time.**

That distinction should remain explicit in later arrival analysis.

## Accessibility preparation

Virgin publishes dedicated accessibility channels for Sailors who require accommodation or assistance.

S10 and S12 describe support for needs including:

- accessible cabins;
- mobility-device use;
- embarkation and disembarkation assistance;
- sign-language interpretation;
- alternative-format materials;
- dietary requirements;
- service animals;
- oxygen and certain medical equipment;
- deaf or hard-of-hearing communication support;
- other reasonable accommodation requests.

The current evidence also makes clear that some accommodations depend on advance notice.

## Accessible cabins

Virgin states that accessible cabins are available for Sailors with mobility needs.

Sailors using mobility devices are advised to book an accessible cabin appropriate to their requirements.

Virgin also asks Sailors or their First Mate to complete the Accessibility and Medical Request Form so the Accessibility Team can understand the request and explore reasonable accommodations.

The FAQ says to make every effort to submit this form at least 45 days before sailing.

This is important because accessibility is not represented solely as an onboard service request.

Some needs must be communicated before embarkation so the institution can prepare.

## Time-sensitive requests

Different accommodations may have different preparation windows.

Examples in the current public evidence include:

- sign-language interpreter requests: at least 45 days' notice is requested;
- accessibility and medical form: Virgin asks Sailors to make every effort to complete it at least 45 days before the voyage;
- distilled water requested for CPAP use: the FAQ specifies a request at least 14 days before sailing.

These are not interchangeable deadlines.

KHDS should therefore avoid reducing accessibility preparation to a single generic "contact us in advance" instruction.

## Mobility and embarkation assistance

Virgin states that wheelchair assistance is available at home ports on embarkation and disembarkation days.

The published assistance boundary runs from the terminal entrance to the entrance of the gangway.

The same FAQ states that this assistance is not available during ordinary port calls.

This creates an important scope boundary:

**home-port embarkation/disembarkation assistance**

is not the same service as

**mobility support during destination port calls.**

That distinction should be preserved in both arrival and Shore Things analysis.

## Medical equipment and personal responsibility

Virgin permits some medical equipment onboard but places specific preparation responsibilities on the Sailor.

Examples include oxygen arrangements, CPAP equipment and mobility devices.

The accessibility FAQ states that Sailors using oxygen must make their own rental, delivery and return arrangements and that some equipment types or quantities are restricted.

This demonstrates that accommodation does not necessarily mean full institutional provision.

A more accurate KHDS model is:

request
→ acceptance or guidance
→ preparation responsibility
→ handoff
→ onboard support

The responsibility owner can change at each stage.

## Dietary preparation

Virgin states that it can support a range of dietary needs and food allergies but also explicitly states that:

- ships are not allergen-free environments;
- kitchens are not allergen-free;
- ingredient availability can vary by region.

Crew are expected to ask about allergies when presenting menus and guide Sailors through available options.

This means the pre-arrival system can gather or communicate dietary needs, but public evidence does not establish zero-risk fulfilment.

That distinction becomes important in `dining.md`.

## Pre-voyage to onboard handoff

S12 states that pre-voyage Sailor Services gathers accommodation information, while onboard Sailor Services becomes responsible for managing accessibility needs after embarkation.

That is one of the clearest responsibility transitions in Case #26.

The guest should experience one continuous voyage, but internally the service owner changes:

pre-voyage Accessibility / Sailor Services
→ terminal operations
→ onboard Sailor Services

This makes information transfer a material KHDS concern.

No evidence currently proves that every request is transferred correctly.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports these bounded findings:

1. Ready to Sail preparation is app-mediated.
2. Sailors select a Terminal Arrival Time before embarkation.
3. Accessibility and medical requests have dedicated pre-voyage channels.
4. Some accommodations require substantial advance notice.
5. Accessible cabin selection can need to occur during booking rather than after arrival.
6. Some medical equipment remains the Sailor's responsibility to arrange.
7. Home-port wheelchair assistance has a defined terminal-to-gangway boundary.
8. Accessibility responsibility shifts from pre-voyage teams to onboard Sailor Services.

## What remains unresolved

The current research does not establish:

- how failed Ready to Sail completion is recovered;
- what happens when required documentation is incomplete;
- whether accommodation requests receive formal acceptance confirmations;
- how request details are transferred between pre-voyage, terminal and onboard teams;
- what happens when a Sailor makes a late accessibility request;
- whether app-independent check-in support is equally usable;
- actual wheelchair-assistance wait times;
- individual suitability of any accessible cabin or service;
- whether dietary or medical arrangements are executed consistently onboard.

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady makes pre-arrival preparation part of service delivery rather than a simple reminder stage.

Several later rights or opportunities depend on actions completed beforehand.

A useful provisional structure is:

booking
→ account/app access
→ identity and document preparation
→ arrival-slot selection
→ accommodation/medical disclosure
→ institutional preparation
→ terminal handoff
→ onboard service ownership

This creates a central Case #26 question:

**Can the system preserve the Sailor's needs and entitlements when responsibility moves between teams and channels?**

The current evidence establishes the intended handoff but not its reliability.

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

The operator documents preparation, accessibility channels and several advance-notice requirements clearly.

The evidence does not establish successful fulfilment, exception recovery or continuity between pre-voyage and onboard teams.
