# Stay

## Research question

How do cabin systems, digital identity, connectivity, housekeeping, service channels and physical ship spaces operate together during a Scarlet Lady voyage?

Primary evidence:

This file studies the published onboard operating model. It does not establish cabin comfort, service speed, app uptime, network reliability or successful recovery.

## The stay is a connected system

Scarlet Lady's stay experience is not represented as a cabin plus separate amenities.

Virgin's public materials connect:

- The Band;
- Sailor App;
- cabin tablet;
- onboard Wi-Fi;
- cabin host;
- Sailor Services;
- housekeeping;
- onboard account;
- restaurants and other ship spaces.

The guest therefore moves between digital and human service layers throughout the voyage.

## The Band

The Band is published as a voyage-specific credential for:

- cabin access;
- touchless onboard payments;
- identity confirmation.

Its encrypted ID is associated with the Sailor's security photograph.

This gives one wearable device several operational roles.

For KHDS, that means Band failure could potentially affect more than one part of the stay.

The evidence does not establish how quickly a lost or failed Band is replaced or how fallback access works during replacement.

## Cabin technology

Virgin publishes several cabin functions that depend on in-room technology.

S13 states that cabin tablets can control functions including:

- shades;
- television;
- pre-programmed room modes.

Virgin also states that bed-mode assistance can be requested through Cabin Services in the app.

The cabin is therefore not purely physical accommodation. It contains a local digital-control layer connected to broader service channels.

No tablet reliability or accessibility test has been performed.

## Human service fallback

The stay does not depend exclusively on the app.

Virgin publishes several human fallback routes:

- cabin host;
- Sailor Services on Deck 5;
- Crew members;
- restaurant staff;
- dedicated service desks.

Examples include:

- requesting bed configuration through Sailor Services or the cabin host;
- seeking Wi-Fi troubleshooting from Sailor Services;
- reporting lost property to the Sailor Services desk;
- requesting certain cabin items or service help from Crew.

This is significant because the institution provides parallel digital and human interfaces.

The existence of a fallback channel does not establish its response time or capacity.

## Housekeeping and laundry

Virgin states that there is no self-service launderette.

Instead, full laundry service is available.

Instructions are provided through a cabin laundry slip, while questions can also be routed through the app.

Pressing services are handled through the same service structure.

Housekeeping therefore combines:

- physical cabin instructions;
- app communication;
- cabin-host or service-team execution.

No turnaround-time or lost-item performance has been tested.

## Cabin configuration

Virgin states that Sailors can request that the seabed remain configured as a bed and can make the request through Sailor Services or their cabin host.

The cabins FAQ also lists special-request items including pillow alternatives and steamers.

This provides evidence of a configurable cabin rather than a completely fixed product.

The public evidence does not establish:

- response time;
- inventory availability;
- whether every request is fulfilled;
- whether the configuration is preserved correctly across housekeeping cycles.

## Emergency route

Virgin states that the cabin's house phone can be used to dial 911 and connect to the ship's emergency response team.

If the phone cannot be accessed, Sailors are told to open the cabin door and locate the nearest Crew member.

That is important because it demonstrates an intentionally non-app-dependent safety fallback.

KHDS should distinguish this from ordinary convenience-service flows.

## Wi-Fi as infrastructure

Connectivity is an important part of the onboard service architecture because several guest-facing functions depend on the Sailor's device.

Virgin states that all Sailors receive some form of Wi-Fi access, but the included tier varies by fare for bookings made on or after 7 October 2025.

Published tiers include:

- Basic;
- Classic;
- Premium;
- Work from Sea.

The exact entitlement depends on fare and accommodation category.

This introduces another fare-dependent service distinction.

## Wi-Fi authentication

S16 states that Sailors connect to the ship network and authenticate using:

- cabin number;
- date of birth.

They can then select the applicable Wi-Fi plan.

If the normal login page does not appear, Virgin provides a direct web address.

If troubleshooting fails, the published fallback is Sailor Services on Deck 5.

This provides a documented recovery path.

## Connectivity limitations

Virgin does not publish guaranteed Wi-Fi speed or latency.

The FAQ states that performance can vary according to:

- satellite availability;
- weather;
- location;
- other factors.

Premium or Work from Sea service can also be unavailable in some areas because of geography, weather or government restrictions.

This matters because some onboard functions require network access.

For example, the Sailor App FAQ states that ShipEats requires connection to ship Wi-Fi.

A connectivity problem can therefore affect more than internet browsing.

## App dependency and ShipEats

S5 gives a concrete example of digital dependency.

If ShipEats is not functioning, Virgin instructs Sailors to:

1. verify connection to ship Wi-Fi;
2. restart the Sailor App;
3. contact Sailor Services through app messaging or visit Deck 5 if the problem remains.

This creates an observable recovery chain:

network check
→ app restart
→ remote human support
→ physical human support.

That is a useful KHDS mechanism because it shows how a digital failure is meant to escalate into human service.

No actual recovery time has been measured.

## Onboard financial identity

Onboard purchases are normally attached to the Sailor's personal onboard account.

The Band is used to post charges to that account.

The Sailor App provides access to wallet information such as Bar Tab balance, while Sailor Services can resolve some billing relationships and account changes onboard.

This creates another shared identity chain:

Sailor
→ Band
→ onboard account
→ payment method
→ app wallet
→ final settlement.

A mismatch between those layers could create a financial service problem even if the physical stay is otherwise unaffected.

No billing reconciliation test has been performed.

## Cross-system pressure

Case #26 creates a distinctive KHDS environment because several service systems are interdependent.

For example:

Wi-Fi
→ Sailor App
→ ShipEats / messaging / digital functions

Band
→ cabin access
→ purchases
→ onboard account

cabin tablet
→ room controls
→ local cabin experience

Sailor Services
→ fallback for multiple system failures.

This concentration may improve convenience when systems work.

It may also create correlated failure when one shared layer becomes unavailable.

That is a hypothesis requiring operational testing.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports these bounded findings:

1. The Band combines onboard access, payment and identity roles.
2. Cabins contain tablet-controlled functions.
3. Some cabin requests can be initiated through the app.
4. Cabin hosts and Sailor Services provide human fallback channels.
5. Laundry and pressing are institution-provided services rather than self-service facilities.
6. Wi-Fi entitlement varies by fare or accommodation.
7. Wi-Fi performance is not guaranteed and can vary with operating conditions.
8. ShipEats depends on ship Wi-Fi.
9. Sailor Services is the published escalation point for several digital and physical service problems.
10. Emergency response has a cabin-phone and direct-Crew fallback independent of ordinary app use.

## What remains unresolved

## KHDS interpretation

Scarlet Lady appears to operate through a **layered service architecture**:

physical cabin and ship
→ local cabin controls
→ Band identity/access layer
→ ship network
→ Sailor App
→ human service teams
→ onboard account and transaction systems.

The system is not purely digital and not purely human.

Its resilience depends on whether one layer can compensate when another fails.

A useful Case #26 question is therefore:

**Does the guest have a clear fallback path when the normal digital layer stops working?**

Current evidence shows several published fallback routes, especially Sailor Services and Crew assistance.

It does not demonstrate that those fallbacks operate quickly or consistently.

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

The intended onboard service architecture is documented in meaningful detail.

Reliability, synchronization, response time and actual guest outcomes remain unverified.
