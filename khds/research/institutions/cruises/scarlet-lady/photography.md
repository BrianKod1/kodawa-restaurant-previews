# Photography

## Research question

Do Scarlet Lady's public images and visual information help a prospective Sailor understand the ship and guest journey as well as promote it?

## Evidence used

Primary evidence:

- S1 — Scarlet Lady ship page
- S25 — Scarlet Lady photo gallery
- S26 — Scarlet Lady deck plans

Supporting evidence:

- S17 — Cabins overview
- S18 — Cruise Dining
- S22 — Cruise Entertainment

This file evaluates the available visual-information architecture at public-evidence scope.

It is not a complete image audit.

Individual gallery assets, responsive crops, mobile presentation and representative sequencing have not yet been examined in sufficient depth for stronger photographic claims.

## Visual prominence

Scarlet Lady's public presentation is strongly image-led.

The current ship page contains a dedicated photo-gallery section and explicitly highlights spaces such as:

- Richard's Rooftop;
- the pool deck;
- The Manor nightclub.

Other sections of the same page use visual presentation to introduce dining, entertainment and refreshed ship spaces.

The visual layer is therefore a material part of discovery rather than decorative content added after practical information.

## Promotional role

Virgin describes Scarlet Lady as visually distinctive and uses imagery to communicate:

- design character;
- nightlife;
- leisure;
- dining;
- outdoor spaces;
- premium areas;
- social environments.

This supports an aspirational role for photography.

The current evidence does not establish whether the gallery is representative of ordinary conditions, crowd levels, cabin categories or all parts of the voyage.

## Practical visual information

Scarlet Lady also has a separate practical visual system.

Virgin publishes 3D deck plans intended to help Sailors find:

- cabins;
- pool areas;
- bars;
- restaurants;
- other ship venues.

This is distinct from promotional photography.

For KHDS, the two visual systems serve different purposes:

**photography**
→ expectation and desire

**deck plans**
→ orientation and navigation.

A strong guest-information system may need both.

## Deck-plan structure

The publicly accessible Scarlet Lady deck-plan PDF labels venues by deck and includes orientation/circulation information.

Examples include:

- stairwells;
- elevators;
- Sailor areas;
- Crew areas;
- accessible heads;
- individual restaurants and entertainment venues.

That makes the deck plan operationally richer than a simple decorative ship illustration.

However, the publicly accessible PDF inspected for this case carries an older version date.

Later Scarlet Lady refreshes mean KHDS should not assume every historical label or configuration remains current without corroboration.

## Photography versus wayfinding

The current public site separates glamour imagery from formal navigation information.

That separation can be useful.

A promotional image of The Manor can communicate atmosphere.

A deck plan can communicate where The Manor is.

Neither necessarily explains:

- how to reach it from a specific cabin;
- whether elevators provide the most accessible route;
- what conditions exist during an event;
- how crowding changes circulation;
- how the venue relates to embarkation or emergency movement.

Those are different information needs.

## Journey-stage representation

Based on the current page structure, visual coverage is strongest around the onboard experience.

Prominent imagery is associated with:

- ship spaces;
- pool areas;
- nightlife;
- dining;
- leisure.

The current evidence does not establish similarly strong visual explanation for:

- terminal arrival;
- check-in;
- luggage handoff;
- accessibility assistance;
- gangway transition;
- Shore Things return;
- disembarkation;
- post-voyage recovery.

That creates a potential imbalance between:

**visualizing the desired experience**

and

**visualizing operational transitions.**

This remains a hypothesis until a fuller visual sample is inspected.

## Expectation setting

Photography affects more than aesthetics in a ship environment.

Images may shape expectations about:

- space;
- privacy;
- crowd density;
- furniture;
- daylight;
- atmosphere;
- dress;
- activity level;
- cabin size;
- social interaction.

KHDS should therefore avoid treating photography as a purely branding concern.

If images omit important practical conditions, the guest can arrive with an incomplete mental model even when the images are factually authentic.

No such mismatch has been demonstrated here.

## Cabins

Virgin's cabin pages also rely heavily on visual presentation.

The public cabin proposition includes design and technology framing alongside descriptions of cabin categories.

However, the current Case #26 research has not yet performed a systematic comparison between:

- promotional cabin images;
- deck-plan cabin categories;
- actual room dimensions;
- accessible cabin layouts;
- configuration states such as bed versus lounger.

That should remain unresolved rather than inferred.

## Mobile and responsive presentation

The current evidence does not establish:

- how gallery images crop on mobile;
- whether captions survive responsive layouts;
- whether practical labels remain readable;
- whether image order changes by viewport;
- whether important information is embedded only inside images;
- whether alt text meaningfully describes the content.

This is a significant limitation because much of the Virgin journey later depends on mobile interaction.

## Accessibility of visual information

The deck-plan PDF includes symbols for accessible facilities in some versions.

That is useful practical representation.

However, a symbol does not establish:

- accessible route quality;
- distance;
- gradient;
- door width;
- elevator availability;
- temporary obstructions.

Likewise, promotional images should not be used to infer accessibility.

KHDS should preserve the distinction between:

visual indication

and

verified accessibility.

## What public evidence establishes

Current evidence supports these bounded findings:

1. Scarlet Lady has a dedicated public photo gallery.
2. Virgin uses imagery prominently to represent key onboard spaces.
3. The current visual emphasis includes nightlife, pool, premium and leisure environments.
4. Virgin separately provides deck plans for practical orientation.
5. Deck plans identify venues and some circulation/accessibility features.
6. Promotional imagery and navigational diagrams serve different guest-information purposes.
7. The publicly accessible deck-plan artifact inspected carries an older version date and should not automatically be treated as proof of every current configuration.

## What remains unresolved

The current evidence does not establish:

- representative image sequencing;
- individual image composition across the full gallery;
- responsive/mobile crops;
- image accessibility;
- current accuracy of all deck-plan labels;
- whether arrival and departure transitions are visually explained;
- whether accessible routes are visually understandable;
- whether cabin imagery accurately communicates spatial constraints;
- whether crowding and ordinary-use conditions are represented.

## KHDS interpretation

Case #26 exposes two different visual jobs:

**desire creation**

and

**operational comprehension.**

Scarlet Lady's public materials clearly invest in the first.

The deck-plan layer shows that Virgin also recognizes the second.

The unresolved KHDS question is whether those layers are sufficiently connected.

A guest may know:

"This space looks exciting"

without yet knowing:

"Where is it?"
"When should I go?"
"How do I reach it?"
"What will it feel like during an event?"
"What is the accessible route?"
"What happens if the plan changes?"

Photography alone cannot answer those questions.

## Evidence rating

**Partial.**

A substantial visual and spatial-information layer is publicly available, but the current research has not completed a representative image or responsive-interface audit.
