# Reservation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do hotel, Prestige, dining, spa, pool, entertainment, group and third-party reservation relationships differ in commitment, capacity and cancellation conditions?

## Evidence

Hotel accommodation is governed by its own reservation relationship. Public hotel material distinguishes room rates, resort fees, cancellation conditions, check-in and checkout timing, early or late departure conditions and group-booking thresholds. [S2][S3]

Some rates can be non-cancellable, while other reservation conditions depend on the booked product or rate. [S2]

The hotel also distinguishes direct reservations from bookings made through third parties. [S2]

Large accommodation requirements are not treated identically to ordinary individual bookings. Groups of 20 or more suites are routed to Group Sales. [S2]

Other resort products create separate commitment mechanisms.

The Venetian Pool Deck is available to hotel guests, but cabanas and daybeds are separately reservable products layered onto that eligibility. [S4]

Prestige is an upgraded accommodation relationship with additional benefits rather than a universal feature of every hotel booking. [S5]

Canyon Ranch permits separately purchased spa access and qualifying treatments, including access relationships available to non-hotel guests. [S8]

Dining introduces restaurant reservations, walk-in relationships and separate arrangements for larger groups. [S9]

TAO Beach includes venue-specific admission and reservable premium products, while hotel-guest benefits remain conditional rather than guaranteed. [S10]

## Interpretation

The resort therefore contains more than one commitment mechanism.

A room reservation does not automatically reserve every other resort product.

A hotel reservation may establish eligibility for certain amenities without establishing capacity or admission at every venue.

A separate spa, restaurant, cabana, daybed or venue reservation may create a relationship independent of lodging.

This supports several bounded distinctions:

Accommodation reservation != resort-wide reservation.

Eligibility != capacity reservation.

Hotel booking != Prestige relationship.

Individual booking != group booking.

Direct hotel booking != third-party booking relationship.

Hotel stay != spa booking.

Hotel stay != restaurant reservation.

Hotel-guest benefit != unconditional venue admission.

These distinctions are compatible with the provisional Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture, but compatibility is not proof.

## Competing explanation

The same evidence can be explained by conventional integrated-resort operations.

Large resorts normally maintain separate booking systems or product rules for rooms, restaurants, spas, entertainment and premium amenities.

Under that explanation, multiple reservation pathways are product segmentation rather than evidence of a deeper responsibility architecture.

The stronger KHDS claim would require evidence that the different relationships materially alter:

- customer identity;
- entitlement;
- capacity allocation;
- responsibility for recovery;
- priority;
- cross-product coordination;
- continuation or termination of the relationship.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish whether hotel, spa, dining, pool and entertainment products share reservation infrastructure.

It does not establish whether capacity is synchronized across products.

It does not comprehensively establish modification, cancellation, no-show and refund rules across every resort relationship.

No live booking transaction was completed.

No third-party booking transaction was compared against a direct booking transaction.

No group-sales process was tested.
