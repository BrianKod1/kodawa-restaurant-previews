# Pre-arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What must different resort users know, possess or verify before arrival, and how much does preparation depend on their specific resort relationship?

## Evidence

Hotel arrival requires more than possession of a reservation confirmation.

Published hotel terms require the coded guest to present valid identification and the credit card associated with the reservation at check-in. Deposit and incidental requirements can vary by reservation and suite type. [S3]

Requested suite type, location or amenities may remain requests until assignment rather than guaranteed pre-arrival outcomes. [S3]

Prestige creates another pre-arrival relationship because the guest must hold the relevant upgraded accommodation status to receive the associated lounge, private check-in and concierge benefits. [S5]

Venetian Rewards creates a persistent membership relationship whose benefits depend on tier and qualifying activity rather than merely on physical arrival at the resort. [S6][S7]

TAO Beach illustrates a more specific credential boundary. The published hotel-guest benefit requires valid suite-key verification and remains subject to specified days, times, availability and blackout conditions. [S10]

Canyon Ranch access can depend on the purchased spa relationship, treatment qualification, age conditions and whether the visitor is a hotel or non-hotel guest. [S8]

Dining may introduce venue-specific reservation, dress, age and group requirements. [S9]

## Interpretation

Pre-arrival preparation is therefore relationship-dependent.

Different users may need different combinations of:

- hotel reservation;
- government-issued identification;
- booking credit card;
- deposit or incidental authorization;
- Prestige booking status;
- loyalty account or tier;
- suite-key verification;
- spa booking or day pass;
- restaurant reservation;
- group arrangement;
- age eligibility;
- dress compliance.

A single resort brand does not imply a single preparation requirement.

This supports the bounded distinction:

Shared destination != shared pre-arrival requirements.

It also supports:

Reservation confirmation != guaranteed fulfillment of every requested accommodation attribute.

Hotel-guest status != proof of every venue entitlement.

Loyalty membership != identical benefit eligibility.

## Competing explanation

These differences may still reflect ordinary resort complexity rather than a distinct architecture.

Hotels commonly require identification and payment credentials.

Premium-room products commonly carry extra benefits.

Spas, restaurants and entertainment venues commonly maintain their own entry requirements.

Loyalty programs commonly differentiate benefits by tier.

The architecture question therefore remains unresolved.

## Limits

The current source set does not establish how clearly these requirements appear in confirmation emails, reminder messages, app notifications or pre-arrival instructions.

It does not establish how the resort handles a guest who:

- lacks the original booking card;
- arrives without required identification;
- misunderstands a requested versus guaranteed suite feature;
- expects a loyalty benefit for which they are not eligible;
- arrives at a restricted venue without the correct credential.

No confirmation email or app-based pre-arrival journey was systematically inspected.

## Next evidence

Inspect pre-arrival communication for hotel, Prestige, spa, dining and venue relationships.

Record:

- required credentials;
- payment requirements;
- entitlement conditions;
- restrictions;
- timing;
