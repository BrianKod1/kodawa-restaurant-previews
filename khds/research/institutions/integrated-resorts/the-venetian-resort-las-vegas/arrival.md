# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How do different resort relationships converge on physical spaces while requiring different forms of verification, priority or admission?

## Evidence

Ordinary hotel arrival uses a formal check-in relationship requiring the coded guest, valid identification and booking credit card. [S3]

Prestige alters that arrival experience by offering private check-in and personal concierge service. [S5]

This means two hotel guests entering the same broader resort can encounter different arrival pathways based on booked relationship.

The Venetian Pool Deck introduces another boundary because access is reserved for hotel guests. [S4]

Canyon Ranch can admit both hotel and non-hotel guests under separately defined access arrangements. [S8]

TAO Beach provides another form of verification: a hotel-guest promotional benefit can require presentation of a valid suite key and remains subject to availability and blackout conditions. [S10]

Higher Venetian Rewards status can also affect priority and other resort benefits. [S7]

## Interpretation

Arrival at the institution is not one event.

There are multiple arrival moments:

- arrival at the resort;
- hotel check-in;
- Prestige check-in;
- entry to hotel-guest-only amenities;
- spa admission;
- restaurant arrival;
- entertainment or nightlife admission;
- use of loyalty-tier priority benefits.

The same person can move through several of these relationships during one visit.

This produces several useful boundaries:

Arrival at resort != hotel check-in.

Hotel check-in != Prestige check-in.

Physical proximity != entitlement.

Hotel-guest status != unconditional venue access.

Loyalty status != hotel reservation status.

Venue eligibility != guaranteed capacity.

These distinctions are directionally compatible with role-partitioned hospitality, but they do not establish it.

## Competing explanation

A conventional integrated resort naturally contains many checkpoints.

Private check-in can be treated as a premium accommodation service.

Pool eligibility can be treated as a normal hotel amenity rule.

Spa and nightlife admission can be treated as ordinary venue controls.

Priority benefits can be treated as standard loyalty treatment.

The decisive question is whether these checkpoints reflect separate operational responsibility relationships or merely separate products inside one resort.

## Limits

The evidence does not establish:

- actual queue structures;
- average waiting times;
- frontline identity-verification systems;
- whether eligibility is checked manually or digitally;
- whether staff can see cross-domain guest status;
- how denied access is resolved;
- whether recovery responsibility transfers between hotel and venue teams.

No physical arrival was observed.

No staff interaction was tested.

No denied-entry scenario was tested.
