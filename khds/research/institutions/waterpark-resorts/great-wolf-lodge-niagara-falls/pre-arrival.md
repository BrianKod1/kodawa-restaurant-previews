# Pre-arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What information and entitlements are established between booking and arrival, and how are optional resort purchases layered onto the confirmed stay?

## Evidence

The registered guest is expected to arrive with valid identification and the credit card used for the reservation. [S7]

All occupants must be included on the reservation. [S9]

This is operationally significant because registered-guest status determines included waterpark eligibility. [S2]

The public evidence also shows that several optional products can be purchased before arrival.

These include:

- attraction passes; [S4][S5]
- cabanas; [S5][S6]
- dining packages; [S5][S10]
- birthday products; [S5]
- late checkout. [S5]

The Inclusive Meal Deal illustrates a particularly defined pre-arrival entitlement because it can be added after the guest selects stay dates, guests and suite, and creates a per-person, per-day meal benefit with stated inclusions and exclusions. [S10]

## Interpretation

The period between booking and arrival can therefore change the guest's entitlement stack.

A guest may begin with:

- suite reservation;
- registered occupants;
- included waterpark access.

Before arrival, additional purchases may add:

- attraction entitlement;
- cabana entitlement;
- dining entitlement;
- extended room-possession time;
- other experience products.

This supports several bounded distinctions:

Reservation confirmed != entitlement stack complete.

Registered guest != holder of every optional entitlement.

Pre-arrival purchase != base lodging inclusion.

Accommodation data != complete activity and dining entitlement data.

## Pre-arrival research significance

Case #35 creates a useful test of whether the resort treats booking-to-arrival as an active operational phase rather than a passive waiting period.

The current public source set demonstrates purchasable pre-arrival products, but it does not establish the communication sequence used to present them.

The evidence therefore does not establish whether Great Wolf systematically uses confirmation, reminders or personalized pre-arrival communication to:

- reinforce reservation confidence;
- verify registered occupants;
- explain included access;
- sell relevant add-ons;
- collect preferences;
- reduce arrival uncertainty.

Those remain research questions rather than findings.

## Competing explanation

Optional products offered before arrival may simply represent conventional upselling.

A resort can sell attractions, meals and cabanas in advance without creating a distinct pre-arrival relationship architecture.

The stronger architecture claim would require evidence that the resort uses pre-arrival interaction to materially alter customer identity, entitlement, preparation, operational responsibility or service recovery.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish:

- confirmation-email content;
- reminder cadence;
- app notification cadence;
- personalization;
- preference collection;
- upsell targeting;
- pre-arrival service-recovery ownership;
- whether optional purchases are automatically associated with individual registered guests.

No confirmation email was inspected.

No pre-arrival email sequence was systematically reviewed.

No app-based pre-arrival journey was tested.
