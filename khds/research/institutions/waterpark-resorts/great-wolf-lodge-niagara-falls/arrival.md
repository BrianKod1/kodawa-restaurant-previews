# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does arrival convert a lodging reservation into active room, waterpark and payment entitlements?

## Evidence

The registered guest is instructed to check in with valid identification and the credit card used for the booking. [S7]

The RFID wristband is a central arrival mechanism.

Public arrival guidance states that the wristband can function as:

- waterpark ticket;
- room key;
- purchase credential when configured. [S7]

Waterpark access can begin on arrival day before the guest room itself is necessarily available. [S2]

This means arrival can activate one part of the stay relationship before another.

A guest may therefore become an active waterpark user before obtaining physical room possession.

The resort is also described as cashless, increasing the potential operational importance of the wristband and other electronic payment methods. [S7]

## Interpretation

Arrival appears to transform the booked relationship into several operational permissions.

At least conceptually, one credential can represent:

- identity or registered status;
- waterpark access;
- room access;
- optional purchasing authority.

This supports several useful boundaries:

Reservation != activated credential.

Waterpark access start != room-availability time.

Room key != only function of the wristband.

Registered-guest status != physical room possession.

Shared credential != identical entitlement for every user.

The wristband is especially important for KHDS because it may consolidate several relationships into one physical interface.

However:

Credential consolidation != proof of operational-system unification.

A single wristband may front several separate backend systems.

## Competing explanation

This can be explained conventionally.

Family resorts commonly issue RFID wristbands that combine room access, admission and cashless payment for convenience.

That integration does not by itself establish Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture.

The stronger question is whether different guest entitlements are separately calculated and governed behind the shared credential.

## Limits

The evidence does not establish:

- whether every registered guest receives identical permissions;
- whether children and adults receive different purchasing permissions;
- how payment authorization is configured;
- whether attraction passes or meal entitlements are encoded on the same wristband;
- whether cabana entitlement is encoded electronically;
- what happens when a wristband fails;
- how unregistered occupants are handled;
- whether waterpark and room systems share one backend guest record.

No check-in was observed.

No wristband was tested.

No failed-credential scenario was tested.

## Next evidence

Inspect available arrival guidance and entitlement mechanics for:

- registration verification;
- wristband issuance;
- room-key activation;

Determine whether the shared wristband represents one unified entitlement system or merely one interface over several separate relationships.
