# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How clearly does Great Wolf distinguish the base lodging-plus-waterpark relationship from narrower paid attraction, dining and cabana relationships?

## Evidence

The resort presents an overnight stay as a bundled family proposition combining accommodation, waterpark access and complimentary family activities. [S1][S3]

The waterpark relationship is especially explicit.

Public material states that the waterpark is reserved for registered guests and that passes are included for each registered guest. [S2]

This makes the lodging reservation more than a room purchase: it also establishes access eligibility for a major resort environment.

At the same time, several other experiences remain separately purchasable.

Wolf, Paw and Pup passes bundle different sets of paid attractions beyond the included waterpark relationship. [S4]

The resort also presents additional packages including dining products, birthday products, cabanas and late checkout. [S5]

Cabanas are separately reservable spaces within the waterpark and add private seating and other amenities to guests who already possess base waterpark eligibility. [S6]

The Inclusive Meal Deal creates another optional relationship because it is purchased per person per day and covers defined meal components subject to exclusions. [S10]

## Interpretation

The discovery layer therefore presents a relatively clean entitlement hierarchy.

At minimum, the public evidence distinguishes:

- registered overnight guests;
- registered guests with included waterpark access;
- guests who purchase attraction passes;
- guests who rent cabanas;
- guests who purchase meal packages;
- guests who use ordinary dining without a prepaid meal entitlement.

The strongest bounded distinction is:

Base stay entitlement != every resort entitlement.

A second important distinction is:

Included waterpark access != inclusion of every attraction, dining or premium-space product.

This makes Case #35 useful for testing whether layered entitlement alone is sufficient evidence for multi-relationship architecture.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation remains strong.

Great Wolf Lodge may simply be one conventional family resort with:

- lodging bundled with waterpark admission;
- optional ancillary attractions;
- optional food packages;
- optional premium seating;
- standard upsell products.

Under that explanation, the different entitlements are ordinary resort packaging rather than evidence of a distinct responsibility architecture.

The case must therefore test whether the relationships differ materially in:

- identity;
- responsibility;
- capacity;
- recovery;
- persistence;
- credentialing;
- operational ownership.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish how clearly included versus optional products are distinguished across every desktop and mobile discovery path.

It does not establish whether first-time guests understand:

- that waterpark access depends on registered-guest status;
- that optional attractions are not necessarily included;
- that cabanas are separate reservations;
- that meal packages create specific prepaid entitlements;
- that occupancy must be correctly registered.

The evidence also does not establish whether add-ons are presented primarily as guest-experience tools, revenue products, or both.

Discovery clarity therefore remains partial.

## Next evidence

Inspect the full desktop and mobile discovery journey.

Record how the resort presents:

- suite booking;
- included waterpark access;
- registered-guest eligibility;
