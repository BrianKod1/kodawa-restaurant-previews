## Research question

Does official photography clarify what is included with the stay versus what requires additional purchase?

## Research discipline

Textual evidence about inclusion or access must not be converted into photographic findings.

For example:

Registered-guest-only waterpark access != proof that waterpark photography communicates guest-only eligibility.

Cabana imagery != proof that viewers understand cabanas require separate reservation.

Attraction imagery != proof that viewers understand those activities may require additional purchase.

Meal-package marketing != proof that dining photography distinguishes prepaid entitlement from ordinary dining.

## Interpretation

The key photographic question is whether the resort visually separates:

- included experiences;
- optional paid experiences;
- premium reserved spaces.

A highly unified family-resort visual identity could make the resort feel like one all-inclusive environment even when actual entitlement is layered.

That possibility is analytically relevant.

It is not established by the current evidence.

## Competing explanation

Promotional photography is generally intended to communicate enjoyment and atmosphere rather than operational rules.

If adjacent text clearly explains inclusions and additional charges, visual unity alone would not establish a guest-experience problem.

Photography therefore needs to be evaluated together with surrounding explanatory content.
