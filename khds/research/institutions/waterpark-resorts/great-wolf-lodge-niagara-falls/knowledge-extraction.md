# Knowledge Extraction

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Case purpose

Case #35 tests whether the provisional Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture recurs in a family waterpark resort where one base lodging relationship establishes substantial included entitlement and narrower optional products are layered onto that stay.

Primary falsification question:

Does Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture recur in a family waterpark resort where the lodging relationship creates base waterpark entitlement while cabanas, attraction passes, dining packages and other add-ons create narrower entitlements, or are those differences fully explained by conventional resort bundling and ancillary sales?

## Extracted institutional relationships

### 1. Registered overnight guest relationship

The suite reservation establishes accommodation and registered occupancy. [S3][S9]

All occupants regardless of age must be included on the reservation. [S9]

Registered-guest status matters because waterpark passes are included for registered guests and the waterpark is reserved for those guests. [S2][S3]

The evidence therefore supports:

Lodging reservation -> registered-guest status -> included waterpark entitlement.

This is the clearest base relationship in Case #35.

### 2. Included waterpark relationship

Waterpark access is not presented as a separately purchased day-pass relationship for the studied property.

Instead, it is tied to registered lodging status. [S2]

Access can begin on arrival day and continue through waterpark closing on departure day. [S2]

The evidence supports:

Registered guest != general visitor.

Accommodation relationship != room possession alone.

Waterpark-access duration != room-possession duration.

### 3. Attraction-pass relationship

Wolf, Paw and Pup passes create additional paid attraction entitlements beyond the included waterpark relationship. [S4][S5]

The evidence supports:

Waterpark entitlement != attraction-pass entitlement.

Included resort experience != every resort experience.

### 4. Cabana relationship

Cabanas are separately reservable spaces inside the waterpark and provide additional private seating and amenities. [S6]

The guest must therefore already have access to the broader waterpark environment while holding a narrower entitlement to the cabana product.

The evidence supports:

Waterpark admission != cabana entitlement.

Physical presence in included space != entitlement to every premium space inside it.

### 5. Dining relationship

The resort contains ordinary dining relationships and some restaurant reservation pathways. [S8]

Prepaid dining products can also create more specific entitlement.

The Inclusive Meal Deal is sold per person per day and covers defined meal components subject to exclusions. [S10]

The evidence supports:

Hotel guest != meal-plan holder.

Ordinary dining purchase != prepaid meal entitlement.

Restaurant reservation != prepaid dining entitlement.

### 6. Late-checkout relationship

Late checkout can be purchased separately. [S5]

This modifies the duration of room possession without necessarily changing every other resort entitlement.

The evidence supports:

Base checkout condition != extended room-possession entitlement.

Late checkout != automatic extension of every resort benefit.

### 7. RFID credential relationship

The RFID wristband can function as waterpark ticket and room key and can support purchases when configured. [S7]

The wristband is analytically important because one physical credential may expose several different permissions.

The evidence supports:

Shared credential != proof of identical entitlement.

Shared credential != proof of one backend system.

Credential function != underlying responsibility architecture.

## Architecture signal

Case #35 is directionally compatible with the provisional Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture.

The architecture signal comes from differentiated entitlements that can vary by:

- registered status;
- occupancy;
- purchased product;
- reserved premium space;
- duration;
- credential permissions.

The same household or travelling party can therefore contain people or products with different entitlement conditions.

The RFID wristband may also provide a shared interface across room, waterpark and payment functions. [S7]

However, none of this establishes the underlying responsibility architecture.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation is conventional resort bundling.

Under that explanation:

- lodging is the primary commercial relationship;
- waterpark access is a bundled hotel amenity;
- attraction passes are ancillary upsells;
- cabanas are premium seating products;
- meal plans are prepaid dining products;
- late checkout is a normal accommodation add-on;
- the RFID wristband is a convenience mechanism joining several ordinary resort functions.

This explanation accounts for the public evidence without requiring a distinct architecture theory.

It remains a strong and sufficient explanation at current evidence scope.

## Why Case #35 matters for falsification

Case #35 is useful precisely because it shows how easily layered entitlement can emerge from ordinary hospitality packaging.

The existence of:

- multiple prices;
- multiple add-ons;
- different access levels;
- one shared credential;
- different expiration times

must not automatically be interpreted as architectural partitioning.

For stronger support, KHDS would need evidence that the different relationships materially alter:

- operational ownership;
- responsibility for recovery;
- customer identity;
- system permissions;
- capacity allocation;
- escalation paths;
- persistence after the core stay changes.

## Pre-arrival significance

Public evidence shows that guests can add attraction, dining, cabana and other products before arrival. [S4][S5][S6][S10]

That means the guest's entitlement stack can change between initial booking and arrival.

The evidence supports:

Booking state != final arrival-state entitlement.

However, the current source set does not establish Great Wolf Lodge Niagara Falls' actual communication cadence between confirmation and arrival.

It does not establish whether the property uses a structured pre-arrival journey for:

- confidence reinforcement;
- registered-guest verification;
- preference capture;
- transportation planning;
- personalized upselling;
- final-arrival preparation.

External hospitality-industry frameworks concerning pre-arrival engagement are therefore not treated as institution-specific Case #35 evidence.

## Departure significance

Case #35 also shows that entitlements can end at different times.

Waterpark use can continue through departure day even as the accommodation relationship is ending. [S2]

Late checkout can separately extend room possession. [S5]

The evidence therefore supports:

One guest identity != one entitlement expiration time.

This is relevant to KHDS because a shared wristband may represent permissions with different temporal boundaries.

Actual wristband expiration mechanics remain unverified.

## Falsification result

Case #35 does not falsify the provisional architecture.

It also does not materially strengthen it enough to justify governance movement.
