# Reservation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does the core suite reservation establish registered-guest entitlement, and how do optional products create separate commitment and capacity relationships?

## Evidence

Great Wolf operates as a pre-deposit resort. Public booking terms state that all occupants, regardless of age, must be included on the reservation. [S9]

That registration requirement matters because waterpark access is tied to registered-guest status rather than simply to physical presence at the resort. [S2]

Waterpark passes are included for each registered guest. [S2][S3]

Suite type and occupancy therefore affect more than sleeping accommodation: they help define the group entitled to the included waterpark experience. [S3][S9]

Additional-person charges can apply when the base occupancy of a suite is exceeded, subject to maximum suite occupancy. [S9]

Beyond the core reservation, several additional products create narrower commitments.

Wolf, Paw and Pup passes bundle paid attractions not presented as part of the base waterpark entitlement. [S4]

Cabanas are separately reservable products inside the waterpark. [S6]

Dining packages can also be added separately, including defined per-person, per-day meal entitlements. [S5][S10]

Other package products can include birthday experiences, late checkout and additional extras. [S5]

## Interpretation

The reservation structure creates a clear distinction between base and incremental commitment.

The suite reservation establishes:

- accommodation;
- registered occupancy;
- waterpark eligibility for registered guests.

Additional purchases can then establish narrower entitlements for:

- attractions;
- cabanas;
- dining;
- late checkout;
- other resort products.

This supports several bounded distinctions:

Lodging reservation != every resort entitlement.

Registered occupancy != unlimited guest entitlement.

Waterpark inclusion != attraction-pass inclusion.

Waterpark eligibility != cabana reservation.

Accommodation purchase != meal-package entitlement.

Base reservation != every ancillary commitment.

## Competing explanation

This structure can be fully explained by conventional resort bundling.

A family resort can bundle room and waterpark admission while selling attractions, dining and premium seating separately.

Under that explanation, separate purchases do not demonstrate a separate hospitality architecture; they are simply ancillary products attached to a primary hotel reservation.

The stronger KHDS question is whether these products create materially different:

- customer identities;
- responsibility relationships;
- recovery paths;
- capacity rules;
- persistence;
- operational ownership.

## Limits

The current evidence does not establish whether room, cabana, attraction and dining inventories are managed through one reservation system.

It does not establish whether cancelling the lodging reservation automatically cancels every ancillary product.

It does not establish complete:

- refund rules;
- modification rules;
- no-show rules;
- capacity-allocation logic;
- add-on transfer rules.

No booking transaction was completed.

No ancillary purchase was transaction-tested.
