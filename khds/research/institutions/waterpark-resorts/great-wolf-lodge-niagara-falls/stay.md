# Stay

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does a base registered-guest entitlement differ from additional attraction, dining and cabana entitlements during the stay?

## Evidence

Registered-guest status establishes the core waterpark entitlement.

Public material states that the waterpark is reserved exclusively for registered guests and that passes are included for each registered guest. [S2][S3]

That creates a base stay relationship combining accommodation with waterpark access.

Additional products then create narrower entitlements.

Wolf, Paw and Pup passes bundle paid attractions beyond the included waterpark relationship. [S4][S5]

Cabanas create separately reservable private-space benefits inside the waterpark itself. [S6]

The Inclusive Meal Deal creates a defined per-person, per-day dining entitlement with specified meal components and exclusions. [S10]

The wristband can function as the waterpark ticket and room key and can also support purchases when configured. [S7]

## Interpretation

The stay contains an entitlement stack rather than one undifferentiated package.

At minimum:

Base registered-guest relationship:
- accommodation;
- included waterpark access;
- included family activities as publicly described. [S1][S2][S3]

Optional incremental relationships:
- attraction-pass entitlement; [S4][S5]
- cabana entitlement; [S6]
- meal-package entitlement; [S10]
- other purchasable add-ons. [S5]

This supports several bounded distinctions:

Registered guest != holder of every optional entitlement.

Waterpark eligibility != attraction-pass entitlement.

Waterpark eligibility != cabana entitlement.

Hotel stay != meal-package entitlement.

Shared credential != uniform entitlement.

Physical presence inside an included environment != entitlement to every premium product inside it.

## Architecture significance

Case #35 is useful because the base-versus-add-on structure is unusually explicit.

If all optional entitlements are merely simple ancillary products attached to one stay, the case may weaken the need for a broader architecture explanation.

If, however, they materially alter identity, capacity, operational responsibility, recovery or persistence, the case could support the provisional architecture more strongly.

The current evidence does not yet establish which explanation is better.

## Competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation remains conventional resort bundling.

Under that explanation:

- the room and waterpark are the base package;
- attraction passes are upsells;
- cabanas are premium seating;
- meal plans are prepaid dining;
- the wristband is a convenience interface.

That explanation remains fully viable.

## Limits

The evidence does not establish:

- whether attraction, dining and cabana entitlements are stored in one guest profile;
- whether all entitlements are recognized through the wristband;
- whether different family members can hold different permissions;
- how failed entitlements are recovered;
- which team owns recovery across products;
- whether optional products persist independently if the lodging relationship changes.

No stay was observed.

No attraction pass, cabana, meal package or wristband entitlement was tested.
