# Story

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Does Great Wolf Lodge demonstrate Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture, or is it better explained as one conventional family resort with bundled admission and ancillary upsells?

## Case narrative

The core public relationship at Great Wolf Lodge Niagara Falls is unusually clear.

An overnight reservation creates a registered-guest relationship. [S2][S3][S9]

Registered guests receive included waterpark access. [S2][S3]

The waterpark itself is reserved for registered guests, making accommodation status a gateway entitlement for a major part of the resort experience. [S2]

That base entitlement does not include every resort product.

Attraction passes create additional paid activity entitlements. [S4][S5]

Cabanas create separately reservable private-space entitlement inside the waterpark. [S6]

Dining packages create defined prepaid food entitlements. [S5][S10]

Late checkout can separately alter room-possession duration. [S5]

At arrival, the RFID wristband can combine waterpark-ticket, room-key and optional payment functions. [S7]

## What the case supports

The strongest public evidence supports a layered entitlement model.

Base relationship:
- overnight lodging;
- registered occupancy;
- included waterpark access.

Incremental relationships:
- attraction passes;
- cabana reservations;
- prepaid meal packages;
- other add-ons.

The strongest bounded distinctions are:

Registered guest != general visitor.

Lodging reservation != every resort entitlement.

Waterpark eligibility != attraction-pass entitlement.

Waterpark eligibility != cabana entitlement.

Hotel stay != prepaid dining entitlement.

Waterpark-access duration != room-possession duration.

Shared wristband credential != proof of identical entitlement.

Included experience != optional paid experience.

Physical presence inside the waterpark != entitlement to every premium product inside it.

## Architecture assessment

Case #35 is directionally compatible with the provisional Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture.

The case demonstrates that one institutional relationship can create a base entitlement while narrower entitlements are layered on top.

It also shows that entitlement can differ by:

- registration status;
- occupancy;
- purchased package;
- reserved premium space;
- duration;
- credential permissions.

That is relevant to the architecture hypothesis.

However, the architecture is not established.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation is especially strong in this case:

Great Wolf Lodge is one conventional family resort where room purchase bundles waterpark access and optional products are normal ancillary upsells.

Under that explanation:

- the waterpark is simply an included hotel amenity;
- attraction passes are upsells;
- cabanas are premium seating;
- meal plans are prepaid dining;
- late checkout is a standard hotel add-on;
- the RFID wristband is a convenience interface.

This explanation accounts for most of the observed public structure without requiring a separate architecture theory.

## Falsification result

Case #35 does not falsify the provisional architecture.

It also does not materially strengthen it enough to justify governance movement.

The bounded result is:

Great Wolf Lodge Niagara Falls demonstrates a clear base-entitlement-versus-incremental-entitlement structure, but conventional resort bundling remains a strong and sufficient competing explanation at public-evidence scope.

This case therefore provides useful falsification pressure precisely because it shows that layered entitlement can arise from ordinary resort packaging.
