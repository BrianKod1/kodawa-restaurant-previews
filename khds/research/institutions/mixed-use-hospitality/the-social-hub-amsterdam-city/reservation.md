# Reservation

## Research question

How are commitments created across hotel, student, coworking and meeting/event products, and which rights remain conditional after commitment?

## Evidence

The hotel pathway advertises direct booking, flexible-rate cancellation and room-type selection. [S2]

The student booking flow is structurally separate, with location/date, room type, booking details and confirmation stages. At the time inspected, Amsterdam City displayed no availability and offered an availability alert rather than a room commitment. [S8]

Coworking memberships distinguish flexible, dedicated-desk and private-office products. Published rules state that access rights vary by membership, and access at another Social Hub location is subject to availability. [S3][S4]

Meeting spaces are booked through a dedicated booking engine or contact route, and an overrun does not displace a subsequent valid reservation. [S4][S5]

## Interpretation

Case #30 strongly separates commercial relationship from guaranteed local capacity.

```text
Membership
!=
Guaranteed Space

Cross-Property Access Right
!=
Guaranteed Local Availability

Availability Alert
!=
Reservation

Hotel Booking
!=
Coworking Entitlement
```

The institution therefore appears to govern several parallel commitment systems rather than one universal inventory contract.

## Limits

No public evidence establishes internal inventory allocation, oversell controls, priority between product categories, waitlist decision logic, or recovery when one commercial use conflicts with another.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

This case applies pressure to the narrower membership-gated architecture from Cases #27–29. Conditional entitlement persists even where the institution is substantially open to the public.
