# Dining

## Research question

How does food and beverage function when the same venue serves public visitors, hotel guests, students, coworkers and event users?

## Evidence

The house rules state that the lobby and restaurant/bar are open to everyone, including non-members. [S4]

Coworking/community members receive published food-and-drink benefits, while event catering and private dining operate through event-specific arrangements. [S4][S5]

The property page treats food and beverage as part of the broader hub experience rather than an isolated restaurant product. [S1]

Published menu material provides food/drink offerings and allergen-related guidance, but does not establish operational dietary-safety performance. [S6]

## Interpretation

Dining is simultaneously:

- a public-access hospitality service;
- a benefit layer for some ongoing relationships;
- an event-support function;
- and part of the institution's social-space strategy.

```text
Public Access
!=
Identical Commercial Terms
```

and:

```text
Published Allergen Information
!=
Verified Dietary-safety Outcome
```

## Limits

There is no public evidence here for peak-demand prioritization, table-allocation conflicts, discount execution, food-safety performance or service recovery.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Dining demonstrates that openness and role differentiation can coexist within the same operating environment.
