# Stay

## Research question

How are multiple simultaneous relationships governed while people use the same property?

## Evidence

The property combines hotel rooms, student accommodation, coworking, shared/public areas, meetings/events, food and community programming. [S1][S5][S9]

Coworking membership categories carry different access rights and benefits. Flexible members have limited access hours; dedicated-desk and private-office memberships include broader access. [S3][S4]

The house rules establish behavior, guest, security, technical-issue, workspace, meeting-room, mail/package, key-card and escalation rules. Repeated complaints can lead to escalating warnings and eventual termination. [S4]

Technical issues may be reported to reception or community management, with urgent issues prioritized. [S4]

## Interpretation

The property is not one undifferentiated hospitality state. It supports overlapping persistent and transactional relationships.

```text
Shared Environment
!=
Shared Permission Set
```

The evidence suggests a governance model based on role, product, time window, booked capacity and behavioral compliance.

This makes Case #30 a useful test of a broader architecture:

**Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture** — research direction only, not a formal pattern.

## Limits

Public rules demonstrate intended governance, not actual compliance, representative experience, service quality, staffing adequacy, conflict frequency or recovery effectiveness.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

If the same entitlement-separation mechanism survives here, then the Cases #27–29 finding may be broader than private-membership hospitality.
