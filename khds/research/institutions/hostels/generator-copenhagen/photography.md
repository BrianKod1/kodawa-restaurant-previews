## Purpose

This file studies what photography must prove for a guest considering Generator Copenhagen.

Photography carries an unusually high evidence burden in a hostel because the guest may be selecting:

* one bed rather than one room;
* shared rather than private space;
* shared bathrooms;
* shared storage;
* public social areas;
* alcohol-led environments;
* events;
* and proximity to unknown guests.

The guest needs more than visual appeal.

They need evidence of how the stay works.

```text
Attractive Hostel Photography

≠

Usable Stay Evidence
```

---

## Core Question

> How should hostel photography demonstrate shared accommodation, privacy, access, atmosphere and participation without disguising density or using visible guests as proof of belonging?

Primary boundary:

```text
Social Activity Visible

≠

Social Outcome Proven
```

Supporting boundary:

```text
Bed Photographed

≠

Dormitory Understood
```

## Photography Scope

For this case, photography includes proof of:

* location and exterior identity;
* entrance and reception;
* complete room categories;
* dormitory scale;
* bed-level features;
* lockers and luggage storage;
* shared and en-suite bathrooms;
* private and family rooms;
* accessible rooms and routes;
* bars, dining and terrace areas;
* games;
* events;
* day and night conditions;
* social and quiet use;
* resident and public boundaries;
* and the complete journey between these environments.

Photography also creates ethical questions involving:

* consent;
* privacy;
* representation;
* staging;
* minors;
* alcohol;
* and the use of guests as promotional evidence.

The strongest image is not necessarily the most dramatic image.

It is the image that resolves the uncertainty relevant to the decision.

| Guest uncertainty                           | Required photographic proof                                         |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What does one booked dormitory bed include? | Complete bed unit, light, shelf, charging point and privacy feature |
| How close are the beds?                     | Whole-room view with representative bed spacing                     |
| How much privacy exists?                    | Bed boundary in both open and closed or occupied-use states         |
| Will my luggage fit?                        | Locker location, opening and useful scale                           |
| Is the room crowded?                        | Representative room configuration without misleading cropping       |
| Is the bathroom shared or en-suite?         | Bathroom relationship, fixture count and circulation                |
| Can I reach an upper bunk?                  | Ladder, clearance and access view                                   |
| Is the room accessible?                     | Full accessible route and room configuration                        |

| Where do public visitors stop?              | Threshold and controlled circulation evidence                       |
| What does a private room add?               | Meaningful comparison with dormitory conditions                     |

Photography should answer these questions before attempting to create emotional aspiration.

## Evidence Layers

Generator Copenhagen requires several photographic layers.

### Layer 1 — Location and Threshold

Photography should establish:

* exterior identity;
* street relationship;
* main entrance;
* step-free entrance where provided;
* reception visibility;
* and the transition from city to property.

This helps the guest recognise the building and understand the first physical threshold.

### Layer 2 — Accommodation Category

Each room category should be represented separately:

* four-bed dormitory;
* six-bed dormitory;
* eight-bed dormitory;
* women-only dormitory;
* private four-, six- or eight-bed room where available;
* private double or twin room;
* and family room.

A photograph labelled only “shared room” is insufficient when density and configuration differ materially.

### Layer 3 — Bed Unit

The individual bed should show:

* mattress and bedding;
* privacy board;
* reading light;
* shelf;
* charging point or USB connection;
* ladder where relevant;
* and relationship to personal storage.

### Layer 4 — Whole Dormitory

The guest should see:

* all or most beds;
* floor space;
* circulation;
* window relationship;
* luggage position;
* distance between bunks;
* and the route to the exit or bathroom.

### Layer 5 — Shared Facilities

Photography should show:

* bathroom type;
* shower;
* toilet;
* sinks;
* privacy separation;
* dressing or drying space where present;
* and the relationship between facilities and sleeping space.

### Layer 6 — Social and Dining Spaces

Photography should demonstrate:

* bar;
* restaurant;
* terrace;
* Pétanque Bar;
* shuffleboard;
* PlayBar;
* Chill Out areas;
* and alternative low-intensity use.

### Layer 7 — Accessibility

Photography should show:

* step-free entrance;
* elevator access;
* accessible bedroom;
* roll-in shower;
* accessible restroom;
* and routes into major public spaces.

### Layer 8 — Temporal Conditions

Photography should show how the property changes across:

* morning;
* daytime;
* evening;
* events;
* late-night activity;
* and overnight rest.

## Room Category Integrity

A room image must correspond to the room being sold.

```text
Representative Hostel Room

≠

Booked Room Category
```

The booking interface should not use:

* a private-room image for a dormitory product;
* a four-bed configuration to represent an eight-bed room;
* an empty social suite to represent an ordinary dormitory;
* or an accessible-room image without identifying it as such.

Useful image metadata includes:

* room name;
* bed count;
* bed type;
* bathroom type;
* accessibility status;
* and whether the image is exact or representative.

---

## Dormitory Scale

Hostel photography can reduce apparent density through:

* wide-angle distortion;
* corner framing;
* empty-room staging;
* hiding upper bunks;
* cropping circulation;
* or showing only one finished bed.

A complete evidence set should include:

1. a whole-room establishing image;
2. a bed-level image;
3. the storage relationship;
4. the bathroom relationship;
5. and a realistic circulation view.

```text
One Well-Styled Bunk

≠

Eight-Bed Dormitory Proof
```

Scale should be visible through architectural context rather than an identifiable guest used only as a measuring object.

---

## Bed-Level Privacy

Generator Copenhagen publishes dormitory beds with privacy boards and personal utilities.

Photography should demonstrate:

* the board’s size;
* what direction it shields;
* the remaining open sides;
* visual exposure from nearby beds;
* overhead exposure;
* and the relationship between the bed and room circulation.

```text
Privacy Feature Visible

≠

Privacy Complete
```

Photography cannot prove protection from:

* noise;
* smell;
* touch;
* room entry;
* or unwanted conversation.

Those limitations require textual explanation and operational evidence.

---

## Upper and Lower Bunks

Upper and lower bunks create different physical conditions.

Photography should show:

* ladder placement;
* climbing angle;
* handholds;
* ceiling clearance;
* under-bed or nearby storage;
* personal lighting;
* and how another guest uses the adjacent level.

```text
Same Bed Size

≠

Same Bed Experience
```

Where guests cannot select a bunk in advance, photography should help them understand both possible states.

---

## Storage Proof

Generator states that dormitory rooms contain under-bed lockers and publishes approximate dimensions through its FAQ.

Photography should prove:

* where the locker is;
* how it opens;
* whether opening requires moving other belongings;
* the useful internal scale;
* whether a padlock is required;
* and whether the locker remains accessible when another guest is using the room.

```text
Locker Present

≠

Guest’s Bag Fits
```

The strongest proof combines:

* photography;
* dimensions;
* and explicit padlock information.

A bag photographed inside the locker may provide scale, but it should not substitute for measurements.

---

## Bathroom Proof

Bathrooms are central to room compatibility.

Photography should distinguish:

* shared bathroom outside the room;
* shared bathroom inside the room;
* private en-suite bathroom;
* accessible bathroom;
* and the number of fixtures available.

Useful proof includes:

* shower enclosure;
* toilet separation;
* sink area;
* hooks or dry storage;
* changing privacy;
* door relationship;
* and accessible clearances.

```text
Bathroom Photograph

≠

Bathroom Allocation Understood
```

Text must explain who shares the facility.

---

## Private-Room Difference

Private-room photography should demonstrate the operational difference from dormitory accommodation.

It should show:

* complete sleeping configuration;
* private circulation;
* en-suite relationship where applicable;
* towels or included amenities;
* storage;
* work or seating surface;
* and room boundary.

```text
More Refined Styling

≠

Private-Room Difference Proven
```

The proof should explain what control the private room adds, not merely make it look more premium.

---

## Social-Space Photography

Generator’s social spaces may be photographed through:

* empty architectural images;
* lightly occupied use cases;
* active games;
* dining;
* events;
* and nightlife.

Each state proves something different.

| Image state      | What it can prove                       | What it cannot prove          |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Empty space      | Layout, furniture and design            | Normal atmosphere or capacity |
| Light occupation | Possible individual and small-group use | Peak conditions               |
| Active games     | Equipment and participation mechanism   | Availability or inclusion     |
| Busy bar         | Crowd scale and social intensity        | Safety or enjoyment           |
| Event image      | Event capability                        | Current programme             |
| Terrace image    | Outdoor environment and seating         | Weather availability          |
| Solo use         | Independent participation possibility   | Guaranteed quiet              |
| Group use        | Group capability                        | Universal access              |

A balanced evidence set should not show only peak sociability.

---

## Photography of Non-Participation

A social hostel should also demonstrate that a guest can:

* sit alone;
* work;
* eat without a group;
* observe without joining;
* use a quieter area;
* or return to the room.

```text
Non-Participation

Is Also

A Valid Use Case
```

If every image shows groups smiling, drinking and interacting, the photography may imply that sociability is required.

---

## Atmosphere Through Time

Daytime and nighttime photographs may reveal materially different conditions.

Photography should distinguish:

* daytime common-area use;
* evening dining;
* game activity;
* event conditions;
* bar intensity;
* terrace state;
* corridor lighting;
* and bed-level night use.

```text
Daytime Space

≠

Night-Time Condition
```

A bright empty room photographed during the day cannot prove quiet during a late-night event.

---

## Events and Currentness

Generator’s Copenhagen event page publishes recurring activities including:

* game nights;
* social programming;
* live music;
* happy-hour activity;
* DJ-led Friday and Saturday programming;
* and lower-intensity Sunday use.

Event photography may demonstrate that the property can support these activities.

It does not prove:

* that a pictured event is currently scheduled;
* admission;
* capacity;
* accessibility;
* age eligibility;
* or the conditions during a particular stay.

```text
Historical Event Photograph

≠

Current Event
```

Event imagery should include a date or context when it is being used as current programme evidence.

---

## Photography and Alcohol

Bar and event photography may prominently feature alcohol.

This can accurately demonstrate one participation route.

It should not imply that:

* alcohol is required;
* every social activity is alcohol-centred;
* minors or non-drinkers lack alternatives;
* or drinking proves successful participation.

```text
Drink in Hand

≠

Belonging
```

The image system should also demonstrate:

* non-alcoholic participation;
* food;
* games;
* passive presence;
* and lower-intensity use.

---

## Public and Resident Boundaries

Photography can show:

* public bar and event areas;
* reception;
* controlled doors;
* elevators;
* room corridors;
* and room thresholds.

It should avoid revealing security details in a way that weakens access control.

Photography alone may not establish the complete boundary.

```text
Door Visible

≠

Access Control Proven
```

A combination of:

* spatial photography;
* route diagrams;
* and text

may better explain how public participation is separated from sleeping accommodation.

Photography should show:

* transfer space;
* bed approach;
* shower entry;
* seating;

Measurements and direct confirmation remain necessary.

## Photography and Safety Claims

Photography may show:

* lockers;
* reception;
* lighting;
* controlled thresholds;
* and bed-level boundaries.

It cannot prove:

* absence of theft;
* absence of harassment;
* staff-response quality;
* effective surveillance;
* roommate compatibility;
* or felt safety.

```text
Security Feature Visible

≠

Safety Outcome Verified
```

Images should support bounded infrastructure claims only.

---

## Consent

Hostel photography creates heightened consent risk because people may be photographed while:

* travelling;
* eating;
* drinking;
* socialising;
* sleeping;
* using a dormitory;
* or attending an event.

Consent should be:

* informed;
* specific;
* revocable where practical;
* separated from accommodation;
* and free from participation pressure.

```text
Entering a Shared Space

≠

Consenting to Marketing
```

A guest should not need to abandon a paid common area merely to avoid becoming promotional content.

---

## Dormitory Photography Ethics

Dormitories are semi-private residential spaces during a stay.

Institutional photography should not occur in an occupied dormitory without strong consent and operational safeguards.

Risks include exposing:

* faces;
* bodies;
* belongings;
* bed assignments;
* travel status;
* medication;
* documents;
* devices;
* and personal routines.

```text
Shared Room

≠

Public Photography Zone
```

Staged dormitory photography is generally more appropriate for durable room-category proof.

If staged, it should not be presented as documentary evidence of actual guest experience.

---

## Minors

Generator permits some minor stays under defined conditions.

Images involving minors require stronger consent, safeguarding and usage boundaries.

A group leader’s operational authority should not automatically be treated as unlimited promotional consent for every minor.

```text
Permission to Attend

≠

Permission to Publish
```

## Staged and Documentary Images

Both staged and documentary photography can be useful when correctly labelled.

### Staged photography can prove:

* room configuration;
* furniture;
* bed features;
* storage;
* bathroom design;
* and intended use.

### Documentary photography can provide evidence of:

* actual event configuration;
* crowd density;
* activity;
* and operating relationships.

Neither should be used beyond its evidence capacity.

```text
Staged Use

≠

Observed Routine Use
```

```text
Single Observed Moment

≠

Representative Experience
```

Photography may become inaccurate when:

* rooms are renovated;

* game equipment changes;
* accessibility features change;
* event layouts change;
* operating partners change;

Useful governance includes:

* capture date;
* room category;
* location;
* state;
* and review date.

```text
Image Still Online

≠

Image Still Current
```

## Evidence-Medium Fit

Photography is not the best medium for every claim.

| Claim or uncertainty   | Best evidence                                         |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Room appearance        | Photography                                           |
| Exact room dimensions  | Measurements or floor plan                            |
| Bed allocation         | Booking text                                          |
| Locker size            | Dimensions plus photography                           |
| Sound                  | Video or audio context plus operating information     |
| Quiet-hour rule        | Text                                                  |
| Accessible route       | Photography, measurements and route description       |
| Event schedule         | Current structured data or text                       |
| Staff availability     | Operating information                                 |
| Public/resident access | Route diagram and policy                              |
| Allergen safety        | Current written process                               |
| Guest satisfaction     | Representative outcome research                       |
| Felt safety            | Direct guest evidence, not infrastructure photography |
| Social belonging       | Cannot be established by promotional imagery          |

```text
More Photography

≠

More Proof
```

The correct evidence medium matters more than image volume.

## Strongest Photographic Strengths

The published property architecture offers strong photographic potential through:

1. **Material room differences**
   Dormitories, women-only accommodation, family rooms and private rooms create meaningful comparison opportunities.

2. **Bed-level features**
   Privacy boards, personal lights, shelves, charging and lockers can be visually demonstrated.

3. **Distinct participation spaces**
   Pétanque, shuffleboard, PlayBar, dining and terrace environments provide recognisable use cases.

4. **Day-and-night variation**
   Recurring programming creates meaningful temporal conditions to document.

5. **Accessibility infrastructure**
   Published accessible routes and facilities create specific proof requirements.

6. **Multiple participation intensities**
   Photography can demonstrate social activity, passive use and withdrawal.

These are evidence opportunities.

They do not establish that the current image system fulfils every requirement.

## Case-Level Research Directions

The following are research directions only.

They are not formal patterns.

### Bed-in-Room Proof

Hostel photography should show both the individual inventory object and its relationship to the complete shared room.

### Privacy-Limit Photography

A privacy feature should be shown with its boundaries rather than framed as complete privacy.

### Representative Density

Images should demonstrate credible occupancy scale without using identifiable guests as decorative evidence.

### Non-Participation Photography

A social institution should represent quiet, individual and observational use alongside active group participation.

### Temporal Condition Proof

Day and night should be treated as different evidence states.

### Semi-Private Consent

Photography governance should recognise dormitories as shared but residential spaces.

### Image-State Labelling

Room category, capture date, use state and accessibility configuration should remain identifiable.

These directions require recurrence and contradiction testing before formal maturity consideration.
