## Purpose

This file studies departure as the closure and transfer of multiple guest states.

At Generator Copenhagen, departure may require the guest to:

* leave a private room or dormitory bed;
* remove possessions from shared storage;
* return or deactivate an access credential;
* settle outstanding charges;
* confirm late-checkout entitlement;
* store luggage;
* report unresolved problems;
* leave quietly while others sleep;
* coordinate with a group;
* and move from resident access to public or former-guest access.

Departure is therefore not one moment at the front door.

```text
Departure

≠

Physical Exit Alone
```

---

## Core Question

> How should a shared-stay institution close bed, access, payment and responsibility states without transferring avoidable operational burden to the departing guest or the remaining dormitory occupants?

Primary boundary:

```text
Room Vacated

≠

Stay Successfully Closed
```

Supporting boundary:

```text
Checkout Deadline

≠

Complete Departure System
```

## Departure States

A guest may leave through several materially different routes.

| Departure state                | Description                                             | Primary risk                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Standard departure             | Guest leaves by the normal deadline                     | Queueing or incomplete closure                              |
| Early departure                | Guest leaves before ordinary morning activity           | Reduced staff contact or limited food and transport support |
| Late checkout                  | Guest has authorised additional room time               | Unclear confirmation or fee                                 |
| Unauthorised late departure    | Bed or room remains occupied after the deadline         | Conflict, charge or housekeeping delay                      |
| Group departure                | Multiple guests leave under coordinated arrangements    | Individual possessions or needs may be overlooked           |
| Disrupted departure            | Sleep, safety or service failure affects departure      | Institution treats consequence as ordinary checkout         |
| Emergency departure            | Guest leaves because remaining is unsuitable or unsafe  | Payment and recovery remain unresolved                      |
| Partial departure              | Bed is released but luggage remains stored              | Resident and luggage-customer states are confused           |
| Continued public participation | Former resident remains in the café, bar or public area | Access boundary becomes unclear                             |
| Extended stay                  | Guest wants another night                               | Availability may require a new booking or room change       |

These routes should not be governed as if they were one identical checkout event.

## Checkout Time

Generator’s booking terms state that checkout at European properties is before **10:00 local time**.

A clear deadline supports:

* housekeeping planning;
* bed turnover;
* room preparation;
* arrival readiness;
* and capacity accuracy.

The deadline should be visible:

* during booking;
* in confirmation;
* before arrival;
* at check-in;
* in the room or dormitory;
* and before the departure morning.

```text
Policy Published Somewhere

≠

Deadline Understood
```

A guest should not discover the checkout requirement only after missing it.

## Checkout and Shared Rooms

Dormitory departure creates additional responsibilities because one guest may leave while others remain asleep.

The departing guest may need to:

* pack;
* open lockers;
* remove bedding where required;
* use lighting;
* move luggage;
* enter and leave repeatedly;
* and navigate a shared bathroom.

The institution should reduce conflict by clarifying:

* what must be completed in the room;
* whether linen must be removed;
* where waste should go;
* where luggage can be staged;
* whether quiet packing space exists;
* and what can be completed the previous evening.

```text
Individual Departure Task

Can Become

Shared-Room Disturbance
```

A checkout system designed only around the front desk ignores the dormitory environment.

## Bed Release

In a hostel, the primary inventory object may be one bed rather than one whole room.

Departure must therefore close:

* the guest’s bed assignment;
* locker association;
* access credential;
* breakfast or other remaining entitlements;
* and the institution’s responsibility for that sleeping position.

```text
Guest Left Building

≠

Bed State Closed
```

If the system does not recognise departure correctly, it may affect:

* housekeeping;
* room readiness;
* new-arrival allocation;
* lost-property handling;
* and security.

## Late Checkout

Generator states that late checkout at European properties may run until **13:00**.

The price varies by property, and the service may be requested:

* through the website during checkout;
* or at reception during check-in.

This provides more than one request route.

However:

```text
Late Checkout Requested

≠

Late Checkout Confirmed
```

The guest should receive explicit confirmation containing:

* revised checkout time;
* price;
* room or bed covered;
* access-credential validity;
* any breakfast or luggage implications;
* and the consequence of remaining beyond the authorised time.

For dormitory guests, late checkout may also affect:

* cleaning;
* remaining occupants;
* and incoming bed allocation.

The public evidence does not establish whether late checkout is available for every room or bed type at Generator Copenhagen.

## Early Departure

A guest may leave early because of:

* transport schedules;
* changed travel plans;
* illness;
* noise;
* safety concerns;
* room incompatibility;
* service failure;
* or dissatisfaction.

These reasons should not be collapsed.

```text
Guest Left Early

≠

Guest Voluntarily Forfeited Every Remedy
```

The system should distinguish:

* planned early checkout;
* unused nights under the booked rate;
* institution-caused early departure;
* and emergency exit.

Where the institution materially caused the departure, ordinary cancellation language may not be sufficient recovery.

## Personal Possessions

Departure from a shared room creates a higher risk of:

* taking another guest’s belongings;
* leaving possessions behind;
* missing items in an under-bed locker;
* confusing similar bags;
* or losing access before retrieval is complete.

A useful departure reminder should cover:

* bed area;
* locker;
* charging point;
* bathroom;
* shared shelf;
* reception storage;
* and rented locker.

```text
Room Visually Empty

≠

Guest Possessions Recovered
```

The process should also make lost-property reporting visible after departure.

Public evidence does not establish Generator Copenhagen’s complete lost-property process, retention period or return-shipping terms.

## Access Credential Closure

Departure may involve:

* physical key return;
* keycard return;
* mobile credential expiry;
* wristband removal;
* or automatic access deactivation.

The public research does not establish Generator Copenhagen’s exact credential format for every guest.

The system should nevertheless communicate:

* whether return is required;
* where it is returned;
* when access ends;
* whether late checkout extends access;
* whether luggage-storage users retain any access;
* and how a former guest retrieves stored belongings.

```text
Checkout Complete

≠

Access Boundary Clear
```

An access credential should not remain active beyond the justified guest state.

It should also not expire before authorised departure tasks are complete.

* accommodation balance;
* late-checkout fee;
* breakfast or dining charges;
* bar purchases;
* activity charges;
* luggage storage;
* damage charges;
* group-account allocation;
* or refunds.

The guest should be able to understand:

* what was charged;
* when it was charged;
* which payment method was used;
* whether any amount remains pending;
* and how to dispute an error.

```text
Card Charged

≠

Account Understood
```

Where a card was pre-authorised or used during arrival, departure should clarify whether:

* the hold remains;
* an additional amount was captured;
* a refund is pending;
* or no further action is required.

Public evidence does not establish Generator Copenhagen’s complete live payment-closure procedure.

## Receipt and Invoice

The guest may need proof of payment for:

* personal records;
* employer reimbursement;
* group accounting;
* insurance;
* tax;
* or a dispute.

A complete departure system should make clear:

* whether the receipt is automatic;
* which email address receives it;
* whether a printed copy is available;
* how group charges are separated;
* and how corrections are requested.

```text
Booking Confirmation

≠

Final Receipt
```

The final record should reflect the fulfilled stay, not only the original reservation.

Generator states that lockers are available before and after a stay:

* for a charge;
* subject to availability;
* priced by locker size and duration;
* with published periods such as 1, 6 and 24 hours.

This extends the guest journey beyond checkout.

```text
        ↓
Luggage-Customer State Begins
        ↓
City or Travel Participation Continues
        ↓
Luggage Retrieval
        ↓
```

A guest using luggage storage has left the room but has not completed the institutional relationship.

## Luggage Capacity

“Luggage storage available” does not establish that:

* a locker is free;
* the bag fits;
* multiple bags fit;
* oversized luggage can be accepted;
* accessible lockers are available;
* the rental period matches the guest’s need;

```text

≠
```

After checkout, a guest may remain at Generator Copenhagen to:

* store luggage;
* use the café or bar;
* meet someone;
* wait for transport;
* use Wi-Fi;

The person is no longer entitled to the bed or resident-only accommodation areas.

```text
Resident Status Ended

≠

Hospitality Relationship Ended
```

The institution should make the access transition understandable without treating the former guest as either:

* still fully resident;
* or immediately unwelcome.

## Departure Failure and Recovery

| Failure                                               | Minimum recovery requirement                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Checkout time was not clearly disclosed               | Explain and avoid disproportionate penalty                        |
| Confirmed late checkout is not recognised             | Restore access or refund and provide equivalent recovery          |
| Access expires too early                              | Immediate credential restoration or assisted access               |
| Guest cannot retrieve belongings                      | Secure access and document custody                                |
| No suitable luggage locker is available               | Explain alternatives without claiming guaranteed storage          |
| Locker fails                                          | Human access and charge correction                                |
| Incorrect charge appears                              | Itemised explanation and dispute route                            |
| Receipt is inaccurate                                 | Corrected final record                                            |
| Group checkout omits an individual issue              | Preserve a direct individual escalation route                     |
| Accessibility route fails                             | Immediate assisted departure                                      |
| Event or noise disruption damages departure readiness | Stay-level recovery                                               |
| Safety concern causes early departure                 | Protect exit and escalate the underlying incident                 |
| Lost property is discovered later                     | Visible reporting and traceable follow-up                         |
| Transport guidance is outdated                        | Correct the information and assist with replanning where possible |
| Stored luggage cannot be retrieved on time            | Escalate custody and transport consequences                       |

The recovery should match the consequence.

```text
Departure Apology

≠

Departure Recovery
```

## Strongest Departure Strengths

1. **Clear standard deadline**
   Checkout before 10:00 provides a visible operational boundary.

2. **Late-checkout route**
   Generator provides an extension route until 13:00 at European properties, subject to local conditions.

3. **Multiple late-checkout request points**
   The guest may request the service online or at reception.

4. **Post-checkout luggage infrastructure**
   Rental lockers extend practical participation beyond room release.

5. **Time-based locker options**
   Published rental periods recognise different post-checkout needs.

6. **Human assistance availability**
   A 24-hour reception provides a visible potential departure-support point.

7. **Continued public participation**
   Food, drink and social spaces may allow the former resident to remain a legitimate public participant.

These are architecture strengths.

They do not verify smooth departure or successful recovery.

## Case-Level Research Directions

The following are research directions only.

They are not formal patterns.

### Bed Release / Stay Closure Separation

Releasing the inventory object does not automatically close payment, access, luggage or recovery states.

### Post-Checkout Guesthood

A former resident may remain a legitimate luggage, dining, event or recovery participant.

### Departure-State Legibility

The system should distinguish standard, early, late, partial, disrupted and emergency departure.

### Rest-Damage Carryover

A failure during the night may materially affect the guest’s ability to complete departure.

### Luggage-State Transfer

Room checkout and luggage custody should be treated as separate responsibility states.

### Departure Recovery Proportionality

A departure failure affecting transport, safety or property requires more than a procedural apology.

These directions require recurrence and contradiction testing before maturity consideration.
