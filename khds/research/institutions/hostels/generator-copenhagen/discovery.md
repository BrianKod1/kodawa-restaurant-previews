## Purpose

This file studies how a prospective guest first discovers and interprets Generator Copenhagen before entering a booking or contacting the institution.

Discovery includes:

* identifying the institution;
* understanding what kind of hospitality it provides;
* determining whether it serves the guest’s intent;
* locating the property;
* identifying available accommodation classes;
* recognizing shared-space conditions;
* discovering social and dining facilities;
* identifying accessibility information;
* and deciding whether further investigation is justified.

Discovery does not establish:

* room availability;
* final price;
* eligibility;
* booking acceptance;
* allocation;
* accessibility fulfilment;
* security performance;
* service quality;
* or guest satisfaction.

```text
Discoverable Institution

≠

Bookable Appropriate Stay
```

## Core Discovery Question

> Can a prospective guest understand that Generator Copenhagen combines accommodation, shared sleeping, private rooms, social spaces, dining, games and events before being asked to make a commitment?

## Primary Discovery Proposition

Generator Copenhagen publicly presents a layered proposition:

1. centrally located Copenhagen accommodation;
2. design-led, budget-conscious hospitality;
3. private and shared room choices;
4. opportunities for social interaction;
5. food and beverage access;
6. games and entertainment;
7. group travel support;
8. and a base for exploring the city.

The strongest initial promise is not only:

> stay in Copenhagen.

It is closer to:

> stay in a centrally located, designed and socially active institution that offers several levels of privacy and participation.

That proposition creates a discovery obligation.

A guest must be able to determine whether the institution is:

* a hostel;
* a hotel;
* a bar;
* an event venue;
* a group accommodation provider;
* or a combination of these.

The official page uses both hostel and hotel language.

This may broaden familiarity, but it also creates category ambiguity.

```text
Familiar Hotel Language

≠

Complete Hostel Understanding
```

A guest familiar with hotels may not automatically understand:

* booking one bed rather than one room;
* sharing a room with strangers;
* room-level versus bed-level privacy;
* under-bed storage;
* optional towel charges;
* shared social expectations;
* group-allocation rules;
* or age restrictions.

Published private accommodation includes:

* Deluxe King;

* Superior King;

* Private 6-bed Room;
* and Private 8-bed Room.

Published shared accommodation includes:

* a bed in an 8-bed dormitory;
* a bed in a 6-bed dormitory;
* a bed in a 4-bed dormitory;
* and a bed in a 6-bed women-only dormitory.

This is strong product-category evidence because the guest can discover that Generator Copenhagen does not sell only conventional rooms.

The distinction between these offers is material.

```text
Private Multi-Bed Room

≠

Multiple Beds in a Shared Dormitory
```

A private six-bed room may give one booking party control of the room.

Six separately sold beds may place unrelated guests inside the same sleeping environment.

The total number of beds can be identical while the privacy and participation conditions are different.

```text
Same Bed Count

≠

Same Hospitality Condition
```
