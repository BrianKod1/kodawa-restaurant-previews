# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Where does responsibility shift when a passenger arrives at Changi and must resolve immigration, transit status, terminal movement, baggage, onward boarding, accessibility and hospitality access?

## Arrival is not one threshold

Arrival at Changi can mean materially different things.

A person may be:

- arriving into Singapore;
- connecting through the transit area;
- moving to another terminal;
- collecting baggage;
- seeking an onward boarding pass;
- entering the public area;
- remaining airside;
- attempting to reach a hotel, lounge, dining outlet or attraction. [S2][S3][S4][S5][S15]

Therefore:

Arrival != One Airport State.

## Flight arrival versus hospitality readiness

A flight can arrive successfully while the passenger still needs to resolve:

- onward boarding;
- baggage;
- immigration;
- terminal;
- transfer route;
- hospitality eligibility. [S2][S3]

Therefore:

Flight Arrival != Hospitality-Ready State.

Physical arrival does not establish that the next hospitality service is reachable.

## Onward boarding state

The transit guide distinguishes passengers who already have an onward boarding pass from passengers who may need to use a transfer desk. [S2]

Therefore:

Onward Flight != Onward Boarding Credential.

A valid onward itinerary can still require another operational step before the passenger can continue.

## Transit versus entry into Singapore

A passenger remaining in transit and a passenger entering Singapore occupy different controlled states.

Jewel is in the public area and a transit passenger wishing to visit it must clear arrival immigration. [S3]

Transit hotels can instead require the qualifying guest to remain in the transit area. [S4]

Therefore:

Same Arrival Airport != Same Immigration Path.

And:

Hospitality Destination Can Change Arrival Process.

## Terminal movement

Movement among airport terminals is not one universal route.

Public-area and transit-area transfer paths differ, and Terminal 4 uses different transfer infrastructure from Terminals 1-3. [S3]

Therefore:

Terminal Arrival != Universal Transfer Route.

A passenger can be correctly located at Changi while still being operationally distant from the intended hospitality service.

## Baggage state

Baggage creates a separate arrival relationship.

The public evidence distinguishes:

- departure baggage tracking;
- arrival baggage tracking;
- transfer baggage tracking. [S12]

Transit eligibility can also depend on baggage being through-checked. [S2]

Therefore:

Baggage Ownership != Baggage Operational Control.

And:

Passenger Arrival != Baggage Journey Completion.

## Baggage recovery ownership

Airport-premises lost property and airline baggage enquiries can route through different recovery channels. [S13]

Therefore:

Missing Item != One Recovery Owner.

The evidence does not establish the complete handoff among airline, ground handler, baggage system and airport support for every failure state.

## Accessibility

Airport arrival can involve:

- accessible transport infrastructure;
- Special Assistance immigration lanes;
- Care Ambassadors;
- information counters;
- airline-arranged assistance. [S7][S8]

Therefore:

Accessible Arrival != One Assistance Owner.

The public evidence establishes available roles and facilities.

It does not establish live fulfilment quality.

## Hospitality access after arrival

Reaching Changi does not establish access to:

- transit hotel;
- lounge;
- Jewel;
- public-area service;
- tour. [S3][S4][S5][S9]

Each may depend on a different combination of:

- passenger state;
- immigration state;
- terminal;
- baggage;
- capacity;
- documentation.

Therefore:

Airport Arrival != Hospitality Admission.

## Responsibility chain

A simplified arrival chain can include:

Flight
→ passenger state
→ boarding credential
→ immigration
→ baggage
→ terminal movement
→ hospitality access.

Potential responsibility owners can include:

- airline;
- Changi Airport Group;
- immigration or government process;
- baggage or ground-handling operation;
- hotel;
- lounge operator;
- another service provider. [S7][S13][S16][S17]

Therefore:

One Passenger Arrival != One Responsibility Owner Automatically.

## Failure states

Material arrival failures include:

- no onward boarding pass;
- baggage not through-checked;
- wrong immigration decision;
- wrong controlled zone;
- wrong terminal;
- missed transfer route;
- delayed or missing baggage;
- inaccessible route;
- unavailable assistance;
- lounge at capacity;
- hotel inaccessible from current passenger state.

The current public evidence does not establish complete recovery ownership for each.

## Strongest arrival boundaries

Arrival != One Airport State

Flight Arrival != Hospitality-Ready State

Onward Flight != Onward Boarding Credential

Same Arrival Airport != Same Immigration Path

Terminal Arrival != Universal Transfer Route

Baggage Ownership != Baggage Operational Control

Airport Arrival != Hospitality Admission

One Passenger Arrival != One Responsibility Owner Automatically

## Competing explanation

These states may be fully explained by conventional international-airport processing.

Airports necessarily coordinate:

- transfers;
- immigration;
- terminals;
- baggage;
- accessibility;
- passenger services.

That remains a sufficient explanation.

The stronger KHDS interpretation requires evidence that hospitality identity, entitlement and recovery must persist coherently across these otherwise separate systems.
