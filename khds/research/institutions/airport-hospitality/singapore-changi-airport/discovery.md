# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How clearly can a prospective Changi user determine which airport experience is actually available to them based on passenger state, terminal, public/transit location, immigration status, itinerary, baggage state and service-specific eligibility?

## Discovery is passenger-state discovery

Changi is not one hospitality product.

A person may be discovering the airport as:

- an arriving passenger;
- a departing passenger;
- a transit passenger;
- a visitor;
- a lounge guest;
- a transit-hotel guest;
- a Jewel visitor;
- a diner;
- an attraction user;
- a person requiring assistance. [S1][S2][S4][S5][S7][S15]

Airport Visit != One Hospitality Product.

## Passenger state

The main public interface explicitly distinguishes:

- arriving;
- departing;
- transiting;
- visiting. [S1]

That is operationally significant.

The correct hospitality path may depend on what kind of airport user the person currently is.

Same Person != Same Airport Role.

## Transit eligibility

Transit is not simply:

arrive at Changi
→ wait
→ board another flight.

The public transit guidance ties normal transfer eligibility to conditions including:

- same itinerary;
- through-checked baggage;
- onward boarding state. [S2]

Connecting Flight != Guaranteed Transit Process.

A passenger may therefore have a valid onward journey while still requiring immigration, baggage collection or check-in actions.

## Public versus transit discovery

A major Case #40 discovery boundary is the distinction between:

- public area;
- transit area. [S3][S4][S5][S6]

These categories materially change hospitality eligibility.

A public-area facility may be accessible to a visitor with no flight.

A transit-area facility may require a valid passenger state.

Physical Presence at Changi != Universal Facility Access.

## Jewel

Jewel is a particularly important discovery object.

The public guidance states that Jewel is in the public area. [S3]

A transit passenger wishing to visit Jewel must therefore clear arrival immigration.

This creates a discovery responsibility:

The passenger must understand that "at Changi" does not mean "available without changing immigration state."

Same Airport Campus != Same Controlled Zone.

## Transit hotels

Transit hotel discovery creates the opposite condition.

The transit hotel guidance places hotel inventory inside the transit areas of airport terminals and warns qualifying users not to clear immigration before using the airside hotel. [S4]

Therefore:

Hotel at Airport != Landside Hotel.

And:

Hotel Booking != Permission to Ignore Immigration State.

## Lounge discovery

The representative T4 lounge supports several access pathways, including:

- walk-in purchase;
- boarding or travel credentials;
- participating card or programme access. [S5]

However, the lounge remains located within the transit environment and is capacity-dependent.

Lounge Discoverable != Lounge Accessible.

## Public-area hospitality

Hub & Spoke provides a useful counterexample.

It is in the public area and can serve:

- travellers;
- non-travellers. [S6]

A boarding pass may create a commercial benefit without being required for basic physical access.

Boarding Pass != Universal Airport-Hospitality Requirement.

## Dining discovery

Dining inventory is distributed across:

- terminals;
- public areas;
- transit areas;
- Jewel. [S10][S15]

A dining result therefore needs more than cuisine and opening-hours information.

It also needs to be understood in relation to:

- terminal;
- passenger state;
- controlled zone;
- available transfer time.

Restaurant Exists != Restaurant Reachable.

## Experience discovery

The Free Singapore Tour demonstrates layered eligibility.

A traveller may need appropriate:

- transit state;
- layover duration;
- entry documentation;
- baggage state;
- registration timing;
- remaining capacity. [S9]

Eligible Passenger != Guaranteed Experience.

## Digital discovery

The Changi App can combine:

- flight information;
- attractions;
- dining;
- bookings;
- payments;
- rewards;
- baggage tracking. [S11]

This may reduce navigation burden.

However:

One App Surface != One Underlying Operating System.

The public evidence does not establish shared inventory, shared recovery or shared responsibility ownership across all services.

## First-time traveller burden

A first-time traveller may need to answer:

- Am I arriving, departing, transiting or visiting?
- Am I in the public area or transit area?
- Which terminal is relevant?
- Is my baggage through-checked?
- Do I have my onward boarding pass?
- Must I clear immigration?
- Will clearing immigration make another service inaccessible?
- Is the facility passenger-only?
- Is capacity guaranteed?
- Is an airline, airport or third party responsible?

That may be an unavoidable airport burden.

The current evidence does not establish whether Changi resolves it effectively for representative users.

## Competing explanation

The observed complexity may be sufficiently explained by normal airport wayfinding and passenger segmentation.

Airports necessarily distinguish:

- passenger types;
- terminals;
- security zones;
- immigration states;
- service eligibility.

That remains a sufficient explanation.

Case #40 becomes architecturally stronger only if these distinctions materially affect:

- hospitality responsibility;
- information continuity;
- service recovery;
- entitlement;
- relationship persistence.
