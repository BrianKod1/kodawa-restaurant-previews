# Departure / Disembarkation

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

When does the ferry journey actually end when vessel arrival, passenger disembarkation, vehicle unloading, accessibility sequencing, terminal exit, onward transport and lost-property recovery can complete at different times?

## Vessel arrival is not passenger journey completion

BC Ferries separately reports scheduled/estimated vessel departure and arrival states. [S20]

Therefore:

Vessel Arrived
!=
Passenger Journey Complete.

Passengers and vehicles still need to disembark and leave the terminal environment.

## Loading order does not guarantee unloading order

Vehicle placement is determined by vessel balance, deck layout, reservations, overheight traffic, vehicle type and accessibility needs. BC Ferries does not present reservation as a guarantee of loading or offloading order. [S5][S15]

Therefore:

Reserved Vehicle
!=
First Vehicle Off Automatically.

And:

Arrival Order
!=
Guaranteed Disembarkation Order.

## Accessibility loading can alter departure sequence

BC Ferries states that vehicles requiring elevator access may be loaded early for placement purposes and may, in many cases, be unloaded later. [S15]

Therefore:

Accessibility-Supportive Boarding
Can Change
Disembarkation Timing.

This is a direct example of one service accommodation affecting a later journey state.

## Vehicle position remains relevant after arrival

A vehicle's location on the vessel affects when and how it can be released during unloading. [S5][S15]

Therefore:

Vessel Arrival
!=
Vehicle Immediately Available for Exit.

## Foot passenger and vehicle closure differ

Foot passengers do not depend on vehicle-deck unloading, while vehicle passengers do. [S1][S4][S5]

Therefore:

One Sailing Arrival
!=
One Departure Sequence.

## Terminal exit can introduce another service owner

Parking at both major terminals can involve independently managed services rather than BC Ferries alone. [S6][S7][S18][S19]

Therefore:

Disembarked
!=
All Journey Services Closed.

A traveller may still be in a parking, taxi, transit or onward-transport state.

## Lost property can outlive the sailing

BC Ferries tells travellers to contact terminals for lost property and notes that items lost onboard are sent to the ferry's home terminal. [S17]

Therefore:

Physical Departure
!=
Guest-Service Relationship Necessarily Closed.

The recovery relationship can continue after the sailing ends.

## Lost-property ownership can cross locations

A traveller may pass through two terminals while an onboard lost item can be routed to the vessel's home terminal. [S17]

Therefore:

Location Where Problem Is Discovered
!=
Location Owning Recovery Automatically.

This is strong pressure toward Multi-Owner Recovery / Responsibility Continuity.

## Baggage and injury claims can persist after travel

BC Ferries maintains post-travel processes for missing/damaged baggage and property-damage or injury claims. [S17]

Therefore:

Sailing Completed
!=
Administrative Journey Closed.

## Sailing status and passenger closure are different states

A vessel can be recorded as arrived or its sailing completed while customer-level recovery, parking, baggage or onward transport remains unresolved. [S17][S20]

Therefore:

Operational Sailing Closure
!=
Customer Journey Closure.

## Strongest departure boundaries

1. vessel arrival versus passenger journey completion;
2. reserved vehicle versus first vehicle off;
3. arrival order versus guaranteed unloading order;
4. accessibility-supportive boarding versus identical departure timing;
5. vessel arrival versus vehicle immediately available;
6. one sailing arrival versus one departure sequence;
7. disembarked versus all journey services closed;
8. physical departure versus guest-service relationship closed;
9. problem discovery location versus recovery-owner location;
10. sailing completed versus administrative journey closed;
11. operational sailing closure versus customer journey closure.

## Multi-Owner Recovery / Responsibility Continuity pressure

Departure and post-travel recovery can involve:

- vessel crew;
- terminal staff;
- independently operated parking;
- onward transport providers;
- customer service;
- lost-and-found handling;
- baggage/claims processes. [S17][S18][S19]

Therefore:

One Ferry Journey
!=
One Closure Owner.

Public evidence establishes distinct service relationships, not actual recovery performance.

## Mobility-Coupled Hospitality pressure

The crossing now exposes a full mobility-coupled chain:

Booking
→ Terminal Arrival
→ Check-In
→ Loading
→ Vessel / Deck State
→ Onboard Service State
→ Vessel Arrival
→ Unloading
→ Terminal Exit
→ Post-Travel Recovery if needed.

Therefore:

Transport Asset Arrival
!=
Hospitality Journey Closure.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation is that ferry passengers naturally need time to unload and leave after arrival.

That remains plausible.

The stronger KHDS interpretation requires evidence that state mismatches across vessel, vehicle, terminal and recovery ownership materially affect continuity, cost or guest experience.

## Evidence limitations

The public record does not establish:

- average unloading time by deck;
- accessibility-related unloading delay distribution;
- terminal egress congestion;
- lost-property recovery rate;
- baggage-claim resolution time;
- customer-service ownership across terminal/vessel boundaries;
- onward transport missed-connection frequency.
