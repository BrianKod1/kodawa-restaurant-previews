# Story

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does BC Ferries tell one story of connection, lifeline service and seamless travel when a single crossing depends on passenger, vehicle, terminal, vessel, capacity, accessibility and disruption states?

## The core story is connection

BC Ferries states that its mission is to connect communities and customers to people and places important in their lives. [S21][S27]

Therefore:

BC Ferries Story
!=
Marine Vehicle Transport Story Only.

## The network is framed as essential infrastructure

BC Ferries describes many routes as lifelines for customers and coastal communities. [S21][S23][S27]

Therefore:

Ferry Hospitality
!=
Optional Leisure Journey Only.

The same system supports commuting, medical travel, tourism, goods movement and community access. [S23]

## The institutional story emphasizes scale and complexity

BC Ferries describes itself as one of the largest and most complex ferry systems in the world, operating dozens of vessels and terminals across 1,600 kilometres of coastline. [S21][S22][S23]

Therefore:

One Customer Crossing
Exists Inside
A Large Networked System.

## End-to-end customer experience is an explicit strategic objective

BC Ferries publicly states a goal of delivering a smooth and seamless end-to-end customer experience informed by feedback and the public interest. [S21]

## Mobility-Coupled Hospitality Architecture

Case #46 introduces a non-canonical research direction:

**Mobility-Coupled Hospitality Architecture**

Working premise:

A hospitality journey can depend on synchronized state across a moving transport asset, a passenger, an optional vehicle, terminals, capacity, time and service availability.

Potential state chain:

Booking State
→ Pre-Arrival Information State
→ Terminal Arrival State
→ Check-In State
→ Loading State
→ Vessel / Deck State

→ Arrival State

## Strongest competing explanation

A simpler explanation is that BC Ferries is a transportation operator with normal reservation, terminal and vessel procedures.

That remains credible.

A stronger hospitality-architecture interpretation requires evidence that these state transitions materially affect continuity, recovery, customer experience or commercial outcomes.

## Falsification standard

Mobility-Coupled Hospitality Architecture would weaken if:

- transport-asset state rarely affects hospitality access;
- passenger and vehicle states do not meaningfully diverge;
- check-in timing rarely changes entitlement;
- vessel assignment rarely changes service availability;
- accessibility needs do not interact with capacity or loading sequence;
- disruptions are handled without meaningful cross-state recovery;
- customer journey closure is effectively simultaneous with vessel arrival.

Current public evidence does not establish those falsifying conditions.

## Current bounded conclusion

Case #46 supports a bounded conclusion:

BC Ferries presents the Tsawwassen–Swartz Bay crossing as one connected coastal journey while publicly documenting distinct booking, check-in, passenger, vehicle, capacity, vessel, deck, accessibility, amenity and closure states.

This is materially relevant to KHDS.

It does not justify formal pattern promotion.
