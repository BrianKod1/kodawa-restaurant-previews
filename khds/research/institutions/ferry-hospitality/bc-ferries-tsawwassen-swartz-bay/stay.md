# Stay / Onboard Passage

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does the onboard passenger experience change when vessel assignment, vehicle-deck placement, accessibility, seating, work, pet, family and safety states differ within one booked crossing?

## Boarding does not normalize the journey

BC Ferries identifies different vessels on the Tsawwassen–Swartz Bay route, including Spirit of British Columbia, Spirit of Vancouver Island and Coastal Celebration. Current Conditions ties each sailing to a vessel. [S10][S20]

Therefore:

Same Route
!=
Same Vessel.

And:

Boarded Sailing
!=
Identical Onboard Hospitality Product.

## Vessel-specific amenities matter

Spirit of British Columbia publicly lists accessible washrooms, elevators, a kids play area, pet area and work stations, alongside vessel-specific food, lounge and retail amenities. [S11]

Spirit of Vancouver Island has a similar but independently documented amenity set. [S12]

Therefore:

Route Entitlement
!=
Every Vessel Amenity Entitlement Automatically.

## Vehicle-deck placement changes passenger behaviour

Passengers on enclosed lower vehicle decks must leave their vehicles while the vessel is underway. Upper-deck placement cannot be guaranteed because loading depends on reservations, overheight traffic and deck availability. [S5]

Therefore:

Vehicle Boarded
!=
Passenger May Remain With Vehicle in Every Deck State.

And:

Vehicle Reservation
!=
Specific Deck Experience Guaranteed.

## Accessibility depends on loading position

BC Ferries asks customers needing elevator access to identify that requirement at the ticket booth so loading crews can try to place the vehicle close to an elevator. Parking next to elevators is limited. [S15]

Therefore:

Accessible Vessel
!=
Elevator-Adjacent Vehicle Position Guaranteed.

## Accessibility capacity can change the sailing outcome

If elevator-adjacent vehicle spaces are already occupied and that position is essential, BC Ferries states that the customer may need to be accommodated on the next available sailing. Once cars are loaded, they cannot simply be offloaded to create an elevator-adjacent space. [S15]

Therefore:

Valid Booking
+
Accessibility Requirement
!=
Current Sailing Guaranteed.

This is a strong Case #46 capacity/entitlement boundary.

## Accessibility feature availability is state-dependent

Spirit-class ferries have onboard elevators and accessible washrooms, but BC Ferries states elevators may be unavailable during extreme weather and are unavailable in an emergency. [S11][S12][S15]

Therefore:

Accessible Vessel
!=
Every Accessible Feature Available in Every Operating State.

## Boarding assistance is itself capacity-constrained

BC Ferries states that manual wheelchairs are limited and boarding-assistance bookings are required. Assistance can take time per customer, and customers without advance arrangements may not be accommodated on their sailing of choice. [S15]

Therefore:

Accessibility Need
!=
Immediate Assistance Capacity Automatically.

## Work and leisure states coexist

Spirit-class vessels include work stations, kids areas, pet areas, lounges and food outlets. [S11][S12][S13]

Therefore:

Passenger Onboard
!=
One Uniform Use State.

The same crossing can function as transport, work time, family time, dining time or rest time.

## Emergency state overrides ordinary hospitality state

BC Ferries states that during emergencies passengers must follow crew instructions, elevators are unavailable and vehicle access may be prohibited. [S11][S12]

Therefore:

Normal Hospitality Entitlement
!=
Emergency-State Entitlement.

Safety state can supersede ordinary service access.

## Same crossing can contain different accessibility journeys

A foot passenger using wheelchair assistance follows a different sequence from a vehicle passenger needing elevator-adjacent parking. [S15]

Therefore:

One Accessibility Category
!=
One Accessibility Journey.

## Strongest stay / onboard boundaries

1. same route versus same vessel;
2. boarded sailing versus identical hospitality product;
3. route entitlement versus every vessel amenity;
4. vehicle boarded versus passenger vehicle access in every deck state;
5. vehicle reservation versus specific deck experience;
6. accessible vessel versus elevator-adjacent position;
7. valid booking plus accessibility need versus current sailing guaranteed;
8. accessible vessel versus every feature available in every operating state;
9. accessibility need versus immediate assistance capacity;
10. passenger onboard versus one uniform use state;
11. normal hospitality entitlement versus emergency-state entitlement;
12. one accessibility category versus one accessibility journey.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation is that ferries naturally have different vessels, decks and accessibility procedures.

That remains plausible.

The stronger KHDS interpretation requires evidence that these local vessel and loading states materially affect continuity, recovery or the perceived hospitality journey.

## Evidence limitations

The public record does not establish:

- onboard seating occupancy;
- frequency of elevator-adjacent parking shortages;
- accessibility deferral rates to later sailings;
- amenity uptime by vessel;
- passenger movement patterns between decks;
- onboard service-recovery performance;
- passenger satisfaction by vessel assignment.
