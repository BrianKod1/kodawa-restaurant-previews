# Stay / In-House Participation

## Research question

How are access, conduct, privacy and service responsibilities maintained during a member visit?

## Continuing membership does not remove visit-level rules

A member enters through an ongoing relationship, but use of the House remains governed by location-specific and global rules.

Toronto publishes restrictions or conditions involving laptops, calls, children and rooftop use. [S2]

House Rules also establish conduct, privacy and guest-responsibility expectations. [S3]

Therefore:

```text
Membership
!=
Rule-Free Participation
```

## Member responsibility for guests

House Rules make members responsible for the conduct of their guests and for guest bills. [S3]

This creates a distinctive allocation of responsibility:

```text
Guest Behaviour
→
Member Responsibility
```

But this does not establish:

```text
Member Responsibility
=
Operator Responsibility Absent
```

The institution still defines the rules, controls admission and may enforce consequences.

## Area-specific participation

Toronto-specific information shows that what a person may do can vary by location, time and activity. [S2]

House Rules also allow access to areas to be restricted during busy periods. [S3]

This means participation is layered:

```text
House Admission
→
Area Access
→
Activity Permission
```

Each layer may have different constraints.

## Digital service layer

The Soho House app supports member booking and relationship functions. [S5]

This indicates that the in-House experience may depend partly on a digital account layer, but the current evidence does not establish:

- app uptime;
- device-failure recovery;
- whether all member services have an equivalent human route;
- or how digital and staff-held information remain synchronized.

## Privacy as an operating rule

House Rules include privacy and recording expectations. [S3]

The privacy notice also establishes data collection connected to membership, visits, reservations and purchases. [S9]

This creates two different privacy questions:

1. interpersonal privacy and conduct inside the House;
2. institutional collection and use of member/guest data.

Neither published rule set proves privacy outcomes in practice.

## Enforcement boundary

Published behavioural rules do not establish enforcement consistency.

The public evidence may show:

- what conduct is expected;
- what authority the institution claims;
- and possible sanctions.

It does not establish:

- frequency of enforcement;
- equal application;
- staff response time;
- successful de-escalation;
- or representative member experience.

## Accessibility gap

The current source set does not establish Toronto-specific accessibility fulfilment during a visit.

## Rating

**Partial.**

The House publishes a substantial governance framework for participation, but live enforcement, digital recovery, accessibility and service outcomes remain unverified.
