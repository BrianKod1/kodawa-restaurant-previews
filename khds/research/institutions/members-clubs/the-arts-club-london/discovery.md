# Discovery

## Public proposition

The Arts Club presents itself as a private members' club in Mayfair with membership, cultural programming, food and drink, private events, hotel rooms and wellness-related access.

The discovery problem is whether a prospective participant can distinguish which relationship they are entering: member, invited guest, hotel guest, dining participant, event attendee or wellness user.

## Evidence

S1, S2 and S6 show distinct routes for membership, hotel accommodation, dining, cultural programming and wellness.

Public membership information distinguishes full, second-person, young-person and international arrangements with different eligibility and fee structures.

The hotel route is distinct and asks for a membership number only where applicable.

## Research finding

```text
One Institution
!=
One Entry Relationship
```

The club identity unifies the proposition, but public evidence does not establish that every participant has the same rights, preparation requirements, inventory access or recovery route.

## Failure states

Potential failures include assuming membership is required for every proposition, assuming hotel status creates the same privileges as membership, or assuming one membership category grants identical access everywhere.

## Evidence boundary

Public material explains proposition and categories, but not representative user comprehension.
