# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does Moxy NYC Times Square present hotel stays, dining, social space, coworking, events and local participation before a user chooses a relationship with the property?

## Evidence

Marriott presents Moxy NYC Times Square as a hotel while also foregrounding food-and-beverage destinations, social space, flexible meeting space and recurring programming. The first institutional layer therefore exposes more than a lodging-only proposition. [S1]

Bar Moxy is presented as the social hub of the hotel and combines bar, lounge and coworking-style functions. It is positioned as a place where users can work, socialize or do both rather than as a hotel-guest-only amenity. [S1][S2]

The Marriott events material describes more than 22,000 square feet of flexible event space and presents Bar Moxy as a work-and-play environment for both guests and locals. [S3]

The Moxy-owned site separates bedrooms, dining and drinking, meetings and events, event programming and room-booking pathways. These are distinct discoverable routes before a user commits to a hotel stay. [S4][S5][S6]

The public discovery layer therefore exposes several possible relationships with one physical property:

Hotel stay
Bar and social use
Work or coworking-style use
Dining
Rooftop use
Meetings and private events
Public programming

## Interpretation

Discovery at Moxy NYC Times Square is not limited to selecting a room.

A user can encounter the institution as a prospective hotel guest, diner, rooftop customer, event organizer, event attendee, local visitor or user of shared social and work space.

This is relevant because these multiple pathways appear without private membership serving as the primary organizing mechanism.

The strongest bounded statement at discovery stage is:

Shared institutional identity != single discoverable relationship.

This does not yet establish that every pathway forms an independently governed relationship or that Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture recurs in full.

That distinction must be tested through reservation, entitlement, access, capacity, lifecycle and responsibility evidence.

## Limits

Public webpages establish how the operator presents the property, not how representative users understand it.

The reviewed public evidence does not establish:

- how frequently users enter through each pathway;
- whether users correctly understand guest versus public access before arrival;
- conversion rates between social, dining, event and hotel relationships;
- whether users understand which on-site services are hotel-operated, partner-operated or independent;
- whether the different discovery pathways share customer identity or data internally.

The existence of multiple navigation paths should not itself be treated as proof of multiple operating architectures.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Case #31 provides transfer pressure against a membership-specific explanation.

The discovery layer shows a lifestyle hotel exposing room, social, dining, work and event relationships under one property identity. [S1][S2][S3]

This is directionally compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture, but discovery evidence alone is insufficient for recurrence.

The stronger falsification test is whether later stages demonstrate materially distinct commitment mechanisms, access rights, capacity rules, user-role benefits, responsibility boundaries and relationship lifecycles.

No governance movement is warranted from discovery evidence alone.
