# Arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How are hotel guests, visitors, diners, rooftop customers, event attendees and users of shared social space distinguished or routed when they physically arrive at Moxy NYC Times Square?

## Evidence

Marriott states that Bar Moxy also performs the hotel front-desk function. A hotel guest therefore enters a social bar and lounge environment while also completing the formal hotel-arrival process. [S1]

The hotel guest-information material distinguishes public and guest-controlled areas inside the same property. The second-floor area is available for hanging out or working, while guest-room floors require hotel key-card access. [S4]

The fitness center is also controlled through hotel key-card access. [S4]

Visitors associated with hotel guests do not automatically inherit hotel-guest access. Moxy requires visitors of hotel guests to register through the hotel and provide identification. [S4]

Magic Hour has another arrival and access process. Hotel guests can present a Moxy room key for priority access to the rooftop venue. [S2][S4]

That priority remains conditional. The hotel states that Magic Hour entry remains subject to capacity restraints, private events and doorman discretion. [S4]

The public record therefore shows several arrival states inside one property:

- Bar Moxy and second-floor shared space can accommodate public, social and work use;
- hotel check-in occurs within Bar Moxy;
- guest-room floors require hotel credentials;
- the fitness center requires hotel credentials;
- visitors associated with hotel guests require registration;
- Magic Hour uses venue-specific admission conditions;
- hotel-guest status changes rooftop priority without guaranteeing admission.

## Interpretation

Physical arrival at Moxy NYC Times Square does not create uniform access to the property.

The same building contains public social space, hotel-controlled circulation, credential-restricted facilities, registered visitor access and a separately conditioned rooftop venue.

The strongest bounded finding is:

Physical co-location != uniform access.

Hotel status also does not operate as a universal credential.

A room key can serve several different functions depending on context:

- access to guest-room floors;
- access to the fitness center;
- evidence of hotel-guest status;
- evidence supporting Magic Hour priority.

But:

Room key != guaranteed Magic Hour entry.

The visitor-registration rule adds another distinction:

Association with hotel guest != hotel-guest entitlement.

These access differences are stronger evidence than the mere existence of multiple amenities because the operator publicly distinguishes access by role, credential, venue and capacity.

At arrival stage, the evidence is compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture.

## Limits

Public evidence does not establish:

- the exact staff decision process when Magic Hour reaches capacity;
- how doorman discretion is governed;
- whether event attendees use additional credentials or guest lists;
- how registered visitors are recorded internally;
- whether restaurant customers can access any hotel-controlled areas;
- how denied hotel-guest access is recovered when a venue is full or private;
- whether hotel, rooftop and event staff share a unified identity system;
- how accessibility requirements interact with the different circulation and access paths.

Key-card and registration controls establish differentiated access but do not reveal the full internal security or authorization architecture.

Public shared space may contain several roles simultaneously; its public status does not imply that every user has the same relationship with the institution.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Arrival evidence creates strong transfer pressure for the broader architecture identified after Case #30.

Case #31 demonstrates differentiated physical access inside a lifestyle hotel without private membership serving as the principal organizing mechanism.

The strongest supported boundaries are:

Physical co-location != uniform access.

Hotel relationship != unrestricted property access.

Guest priority != guaranteed venue admission.

Association with a guest != equivalent guest entitlement.

These findings support the broader Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality hypothesis more strongly than a simple undifferentiated amenities model would predict.

No governance movement is warranted at the arrival stage. The next test is whether these access distinctions persist during the stay through guest benefits, shared public space and service-specific responsibility.
