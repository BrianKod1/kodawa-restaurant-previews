# Departure

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

Which relationships at Moxy NYC Times Square clearly terminate with hotel checkout, which services remain separately available, and what evidence exists for relationship continuity after physical departure?

## Evidence

Marriott publishes a defined hotel checkout time, establishing a clear temporal boundary for the occupied-room relationship. [S1][S8]

Moxy separately provides luggage storage and publishes a distinct liability limitation for stored bags and their contents. [S4]

This establishes a luggage-storage service with a responsibility condition distinct from the occupied-room relationship. The source does not specify whether storage occurs before or after checkout.

The public record therefore establishes at least two departure-related states:

Hotel room relationship -> defined checkout boundary.

Luggage-storage relationship -> separate custody condition and responsibility limitation.

Other property relationships are not defined solely by room checkout.

Bar Moxy is publicly accessible and does not require a reservation. [S2][S4]

Magic Hour accepts reservations and walk-ins and operates under its own capacity and admission rules. [S2][S4]

Legasea uses restaurant-specific reservation conditions. [S2]

The second-floor social and work area is described as public space rather than as an area available only to occupied-room guests. [S4]

These facts establish that several property experiences use access mechanisms independent of possession of a hotel room.

However, the reviewed sources do not establish whether a person who has just checked out retains any hotel-guest-specific privilege when using those services.

The evidence therefore supports the bounded statement:

End of room occupancy != proof of termination of every possible property relationship.

It does not support the stronger claim that checkout automatically preserves hotel-guest entitlement elsewhere in the property.

The hotel also documents guest-specific benefits during the stay, including key-card-linked access and selected privileges. [S4]

The reviewed public evidence does not state precisely when every such benefit expires following checkout.

## Interpretation

Departure at Moxy NYC Times Square appears to involve relationship-specific closure rather than one demonstrated institution-wide termination event.

The clearest lifecycle is the room relationship:

Booking -> arrival -> occupied stay -> checkout -> room entitlement ends.

Other services operate through different relationship mechanisms:

- dining through venue-specific access or reservation;
- rooftop use through reservation, walk-in and capacity control;
- public social space through public access conditions;
- luggage storage through a separate custody condition.

The strongest supported boundary is:

Physical checkout != demonstrated closure of every co-located relationship.

This should not be interpreted as proof that the hotel relationship itself continues after checkout.

Instead, it shows that the property contains relationships whose lifecycle is not necessarily defined by the room lifecycle.

Luggage storage is useful because it establishes a separate responsibility condition for an additional hotel service, but the public source does not establish its timing relative to checkout.

This supports:

Room lifecycle != universal service lifecycle.

Shared customer != uniform responsibility condition.

## Limits

Public evidence does not establish:

- the exact moment at which a room key becomes invalid;
- whether Magic Hour hotel-guest priority remains valid after checkout;
- whether destination-fee benefits remain redeemable after checkout;
- whether former guests may use hotel-controlled facilities after checkout;
- the exact temporal rules for luggage storage;
- whether hotel, restaurant and event customer profiles remain linked after departure;
- how post-stay service recovery is divided among hotel and venue operators;
- whether post-stay marketing represents continuity of the same operating relationship;
- whether Marriott Bonvoy activity should be treated as property-level continuity, brand-level continuity or a separate loyalty relationship.

Public access to dining or social spaces must not be interpreted as continued hotel-guest entitlement after checkout.

Likewise, luggage storage should not be treated as proof of a continuing room relationship.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Departure evidence adds lifecycle separation to the Case #31 architecture.

Earlier stages show differentiated discovery pathways, commitment mechanisms, access rights, capacity rules, guest benefits and responsibility signals.

Departure adds evidence that at least some service relationships have lifecycle and responsibility conditions distinguishable from the room relationship.

The strongest supported boundaries are:

Room lifecycle != universal property-service lifecycle.

Physical departure != proof of institution-wide relationship closure.

Shared customer != uniform service responsibility.

The second statement must remain narrowly bounded.

Case #31 does not establish durable post-departure relationship continuity. It establishes only that room checkout cannot be assumed to define the lifecycle of every separately accessible service in the property.

This is compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture and creates further pressure against a simple bundled-amenities model.

No governance movement, confidence increase, validation or principle creation is warranted from departure evidence alone.
