# Stay

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What does the Moxy NYC Times Square hotel-stay relationship include, and which co-located social, dining, rooftop, visitor and partner services remain separate, conditional or independently governed?

## Evidence

The hotel stay provides the core lodging relationship: a guest room, hotel check-in and checkout, key-card access to guest-room floors and access to hotel-controlled facilities such as the fitness center. [S1][S4][S8]

The hotel guest-information material identifies benefits tied specifically to hotel-guest status. These include complimentary drip coffee during stated morning hours and selected destination-fee-related benefits. [S4]

Some benefits require proof of hotel status or hotel-side administration rather than flowing automatically from physical presence in the building. [S4]

The room key also operates as evidence of hotel-guest status outside the room corridor. At Magic Hour, presenting the room key creates priority access. [S2][S4]

That benefit remains conditional. Hotel-guest priority is subject to capacity restraints, private events and doorman discretion. [S4]

The second-floor social and work area is publicly usable, so hotel-guest status is not required for all shared-space use within the property. [S4]

Bar Moxy reinforces this mixed-role environment. Marriott describes it as part bar, part lounge and part coworking-style environment while also functioning as the social hub and hotel front desk. [S1][S2]

The dining ecosystem also remains partly outside the core room relationship. Marriott states that room service is not offered and instead directs users toward the property dining options. [S2]

Café d'Avignon provides a particularly clear boundary. Marriott lists it as an on-site café and documents a hotel-guest coffee benefit while also stating that the café is unaffiliated with Moxy Times Square. [S2]

The hotel stay therefore coexists with several different service states:

- core lodging entitlement;
- credential-controlled hotel areas;
- guest-specific benefits;
- conditional cross-service privileges;
- public or shared space;
- separately governed dining or rooftop services;
- on-site service that is explicitly unaffiliated with Moxy Times Square.

## Interpretation

The hotel stay is a defined relationship rather than a universal entitlement to everything physically contained within Moxy NYC Times Square.

The strongest supported distinction is:

Hotel stay != uniform entitlement across the property.

The room key is especially useful because it operates as a role credential rather than a universal access credential.

It grants or evidences access to guest-controlled areas, hotel-guest identity and selected benefits, but it does not establish guaranteed Magic Hour capacity, automatic restaurant reservations, unrestricted visitor access or unified control over every on-site service.

This suggests a layered stay relationship:

Core stay entitlement + role-specific benefits + conditional cross-service privileges + public/shared spaces + separately governed services.

The Café d'Avignon evidence adds another distinction:

Guest benefit relationship != unified operator responsibility.

A hotel can provide a guest benefit involving an on-site service without that service becoming institutionally identical to the hotel.

This is more consistent with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture than with a model in which all property services are bundled uniformly into the room product.

## Limits

Public evidence does not establish:

- the full legal or contractual relationship among Moxy, Marriott, TAO Group and Café d'Avignon;
- whether hotel and dining systems share customer profiles or transaction data;
- how destination-fee credits are technically processed;
- how service recovery works when a hotel guest has a problem with another on-site operator;
- whether hotel staff can override restaurant or rooftop capacity;
- how guest preferences move between room, dining and event systems;
- whether every public area remains equally public during private events;
- whether some hotel packages create stronger cross-service entitlements.

The absence of room service establishes a service boundary but does not by itself prove separate operators.

Likewise, Café d'Avignon being explicitly unaffiliated should not be generalized to every food-and-beverage venue without separate evidence.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Stay-stage evidence strengthens the Case #31 transfer test.

The hotel relationship clearly creates room entitlement, controlled-area access, guest credentials and guest-specific benefits, but those rights stop short of uniform control over the wider property ecosystem.

The strongest supported boundaries are:

Hotel stay != uniform property entitlement.

Guest credential != universal access credential.

Guest benefit != guaranteed service capacity.

Physical co-location != unified operator responsibility.

Case #31 is therefore increasingly difficult to explain as only a hotel with undifferentiated ancillary amenities.

The public evidence instead shows several relationships sharing one property while retaining distinct access, capacity, benefit and responsibility conditions.
