# Pre-arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

What access rules, benefits, expectations and responsibility boundaries can be established before physical arrival for hotel guests, visitors, dining customers and event customers at Moxy NYC Times Square?

## Evidence

Moxy maintains public guest information that defines several conditions attached to the hotel-stay relationship. Hotel guests receive priority access to Magic Hour when presenting a room key, while entry remains subject to capacity restraints, private events and doorman discretion. [S4]

The same guest information distinguishes hotel-guest status from visitor status. Visitors associated with hotel guests must be registered through the hotel rather than automatically receiving equivalent access. [S4]

Public and controlled spaces are also differentiated before use. The second-floor area is described as available for hanging out or working, while guest-room floors and the fitness center require hotel key-card access. [S4]

Hotel-guest status also carries selected benefits, including complimentary drip coffee during stated hours and other destination-fee-related benefits documented by the hotel. [S4]

Group reservations can follow different benefit-administration instructions from ordinary individual stays, showing that even hotel relationships are not necessarily prepared identically. [S4]

Moxy contact routing separates general hotel enquiries and group or meeting enquiries from restaurant, rooftop and event contacts. Magic Hour and Legasea contacts use TAO Group addresses. [S5]

Event customers enter another pre-arrival process. Event enquiries request event-specific information such as date, guest count and service requirements, and venue materials define capacity before physical arrival. [S5][S7]

The public evidence therefore shows different pre-arrival information states:

Hotel guest -> room relationship, guest benefits, key-card expectations and conditional rooftop priority.

Visitor -> hotel registration requirement rather than automatic guest entitlement.

Event customer -> event inquiry, venue capacity and event-specific contact pathway.

Dining or rooftop customer -> venue-specific reservation or inquiry conditions.

## Interpretation

The pre-arrival layer is not uniform across all users of the property.

Different relationships can carry different information, benefits, permissions and preparation requirements before physical arrival.

The strongest bounded statement is:

Shared destination != shared pre-arrival relationship.

Hotel-guest status creates identifiable benefits and access conditions, but those conditions do not automatically extend to visitors, rooftop capacity, event inventory or every co-located service.

The visitor-registration rule is particularly useful because association with a registered hotel guest does not create equivalent hotel-guest entitlement.

The event-planning pathway also demonstrates that a relationship can begin before physical arrival and can contain capacity and service conditions distinct from the room-booking relationship.

This is compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture because relationship type affects preparation and expected entitlement before arrival.

## Limits

The existence of public guest-information pages does not establish that every guest receives all of that information automatically before arrival.

Public evidence does not establish:

- the exact confirmation-email sequence after room booking;
- whether restaurant or rooftop reservations are linked to the hotel reservation record;
- whether TAO Group and hotel systems share customer identity or preference data;
- how room keys are technically provisioned;
- whether event attendees receive individual credentials;
- how changes or cancellations propagate across hotel, dining and event systems;
- whether guest-benefit eligibility is checked automatically or manually;
- how partner-operated services receive or use hotel-guest information.

Public pre-arrival information must not be treated as proof of backend integration.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Pre-arrival evidence extends Case #31 beyond a simple multiple-venue observation.

The record shows relationship-specific differences in benefits, eligibility conditions, access expectations, contact routes, capacity information and planning requirements.

The strongest supported boundaries are:

Relationship type != uniform entitlement or preparation.

Association with a hotel guest != equivalent hotel-guest access.

These findings remain compatible with Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture but do not establish how every role is recognized operationally at arrival.

No pattern promotion, confidence movement, validation or principle creation is warranted from pre-arrival evidence alone.
