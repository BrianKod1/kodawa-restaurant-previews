# Dining

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How are Moxy NYC Times Square food-and-beverage experiences related to the hotel, and where do reservation, entitlement, capacity, guest benefit and operator responsibility remain distinct?

## Evidence

Marriott presents several food-and-beverage experiences within the Moxy Times Square property ecosystem, including Bar Moxy, Legasea, Café d'Avignon and Magic Hour. [S2]

These venues do not operate under one visible customer rule set.

Bar Moxy is described as part bar, part lounge and part coworking-style environment. Marriott states that no reservation is required. [S2]

Legasea uses restaurant-specific reservation conditions. Marriott documents additional requirements for larger parties, including credit-card and availability conditions. [S2]

Magic Hour follows another model. Reservations are encouraged, walk-ins are accepted and hotel guests may present a Moxy room key for priority access. [S2][S4]

Hotel-guest priority at Magic Hour does not create guaranteed rooftop or dining capacity. Entry remains subject to capacity restraints, private events and doorman discretion. [S4]

Magic Hour also supports private and semi-private event use, including venue-specific capacities and full-buyout configurations. [S7]

Café d'Avignon creates a different boundary. Marriott describes it as located on-site and documents a complimentary drip-coffee benefit for hotel guests. [S2]

The same Marriott material explicitly states that Café d'Avignon is unaffiliated with Moxy Times Square despite being located on-site. [S2]

This supports the bounded distinction:

On-site service != institutional affiliation.

The guest-benefit relationship also remains distinct:

Hotel guest benefit involving an on-site café != proof that the café is institutionally identical to the hotel.

Contact routing differs across the property. Moxy general hotel contacts are distinct from restaurant, rooftop and event contacts, with Magic Hour and Legasea using TAO Group addresses. [S5]

Marriott also states that room service is not offered, so the hotel room relationship does not automatically absorb the wider dining ecosystem into in-room service. [S2]

The public dining structure therefore shows:

- Bar Moxy with no reservation requirement;
- Legasea with restaurant-specific reservation conditions;
- Magic Hour with reservations, walk-ins, hotel-guest priority and event conversion;
- Café d'Avignon as on-site but explicitly unaffiliated with Moxy Times Square;
- differentiated hotel versus venue contact routing.

## Interpretation

Dining at Moxy NYC Times Square is not one uniform hotel amenity relationship.

The public evidence supports several distinct boundaries:

Physical co-location != uniform operator relationship.

Hotel guest benefit != institutional affiliation.

Hotel guest status != guaranteed dining or rooftop capacity.

One property dining ecosystem != one reservation model.

The Café d'Avignon evidence is especially important because the operator itself distinguishes physical presence from institutional affiliation.

Magic Hour provides a different distinction: hotel-guest status changes priority but does not override venue-specific capacity, event or admission conditions.

Legasea adds another commitment layer through restaurant-specific reservation rules.

Bar Moxy sits closer to the hotel experience in public presentation but still operates under a different access model from Legasea and Magic Hour.

Taken together, the dining evidence is more consistent with differentiated relationships than with a single bundled hotel-dining entitlement.

## Limits

Public evidence does not establish:

- the legal ownership structure of every food-and-beverage venue;
- the full contractual relationship between Moxy, Marriott and TAO Group;
- whether TAO Group operates every venue whose contact uses a TAO Group address;
- how revenue is shared between hotel and dining operators;
- whether hotel and restaurant systems share customer profiles, payment data or preferences;
- whether hotel staff can override restaurant or rooftop reservations;
- how complaints are allocated between hotel and dining operators;
- how the hotel guest coffee benefit involving Café d'Avignon is financially or operationally settled;
- whether all dining benefits apply under every hotel booking type;
- how private-event conversion affects ordinary guest or public dining capacity.

TAO Group contact routing is evidence of differentiated customer-facing responsibility, but it is not sufficient proof of legal ownership or contractual control.

The explicit unaffiliated statement applies specifically to Café d'Avignon and must not be generalized to the other venues without separate evidence.

## Synthesis-gate relevance

Dining evidence provides some of the strongest Case #31 transfer evidence.

A single hotel property contains food-and-beverage relationships with materially different reservation rules, capacity conditions, guest privileges, event uses, contact channels and affiliation signals.

The strongest supported boundaries are:

Physical co-location != unified operator responsibility.

Guest benefit != institutional affiliation.

Hotel relationship != uniform dining entitlement.

Shared property environment != shared service capacity.

This creates substantial support for Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture outside a membership-led institution.

No governance movement is warranted from dining evidence alone.
