Purpose

KHDS hotel research tests hospitality across a longer and more distributed guest journey than the restaurant research programme.

Hotel hospitality may begin before a booking and continue beyond physical checkout.

The research field includes:

discovery,

comparison and qualification,

direct or mediated reservation,

pre-arrival preparation,

external travel,

first institution-owned handoff,

physical arrival,

check-in,

room settlement,

service requests,

private and shared-space use,

dining,

activities and partner delivery,

recovery,

departure,

onward travel,

and post-stay relationship.

Hotel research asks whether restaurant-derived KHDS patterns transfer when hospitality involves:

overnight responsibility,

multiple days,

repeated staff handoffs,

private-space access,

more personal information,

several capacity stocks,

transport dependencies,

distributed service delivery,

and route-dependent closure.

Primary research question

Do restaurant-derived KHDS patterns transfer to a remote boutique hotel whose hospitality journey extends across discovery, booking, transport, multiple days, rooms, food, activities, community relationships and departure—and what additional architecture is required to preserve care across that expanded field?

Selection rule for future hotel cases

Future hotels should be selected for contradiction and transfer value rather than prestige alone.

Selection should vary at least one material condition:

remote versus urban,

boutique versus large-scale,

luxury versus limited service,

independent versus group-controlled,

full board versus room-only,

human-led versus technology-intensive,

private versus communal accommodation,

destination-led versus functional,

conventional ownership versus community or charitable structure,

and short-stay versus long-stay.

The next hotel should not merely confirm Fogo Island Inn's model.

It should reveal which Case #21 findings depend on:

remoteness,

full board,

place-led story,

boutique scale,

charitable ownership,

or hotel hospitality generally.
