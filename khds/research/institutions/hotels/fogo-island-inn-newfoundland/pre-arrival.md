1. Purpose of this file

This file studies the period between reservation confirmation and physical arrival at Fogo Island Inn.

That period is not treated as administrative waiting time.

It is a hospitality stage in which a commercially confirmed stay must become:

reachable,

understood,

personally appropriate,

operationally prepared,

resilient to disruption,

and ready to transfer from digital coordination to human welcome.

For a conventional urban hotel, a guest may independently arrange transport and arrive at the address.

For Fogo Island Inn, the public journey can involve:

a commercial flight,

an airport choice,

ground travel,

a ferry schedule,

a sea crossing,

a private charter alternative,

seasonal conditions,

local transport,

and institutional coordination.

The guest also enters a multi-day full-board relationship involving:

meals,

dietary requirements,

mobility requirements,

outdoor activity,

clothing and equipment,

community encounters,

personalised itinerary choices,

and weather-dependent change.

Pre-arrival must therefore answer two linked questions.

Can the Guest Reach the Inn?

and:

Are the Guest and the Inn Ready for One Another?

The purpose of this file is to identify:

what must be fixed before travel,

what may remain flexible,

what information must be remembered,

what requires human judgement,

what can change with weather or season,

and what evidence is still unavailable from public pages.

2. Pre-arrival boundary

Pre-arrival begins when the reservation becomes commercially confirmed or enters active itinerary planning.

It can also begin before deposit payment when:

transport feasibility,

accessibility,

dietary safety,

family suitability,

or room fit

must be resolved before commitment.

Pre-arrival ends when responsibility transfers into the physical arrival system.

That handoff may occur:

at Gander International Airport,

when a chauffeur meets the guest,

at a charter departure point,

at Farewell ferry terminal,

on Fogo Island,

or at the Inn's entrance,

depending on the chosen route and institutional involvement.

The dimension therefore overlaps reservation and arrival.

It does not duplicate them.

2.1 Reservation boundary

Reservation establishes:

commercial commitment,

room inventory,

dates,

party,

and payment state.

Pre-arrival turns those records into an executable plan.

Booking Data
≠

2.2 Arrival boundary

Arrival begins when the guest starts encountering the live receiving system.

Pre-arrival should make that encounter legible before it occurs.

Instructions Sent
≠

2.3 Working definition

For this case:

Pre-arrival is the coordinated period in which the institution and guest convert a confirmed stay into a viable route, acknowledged set of needs, adaptable experience plan and safe handoff into physical hospitality.

47. Falsification programme

The following tests are required before stronger conclusions are justified.

F01 — Booking-instruction audit

Review the travel instructions actually sent after booking.

Would falsify: the assumption that public information is transformed into actionable guest-specific guidance.

F02 — Advisor introduction test

Observe when and how an Advisor enters an online booking.

Would falsify: the assumption that human planning is an integrated handoff.

F03 — Advisor ownership test

Submit a complex route question and trace responsibility to resolution.

Would falsify: the assumption that availability becomes ownership.

F04 — Out-of-hours test

Model a major travel disruption outside published Advisor hours.

Would falsify: the assumption that urgent support remains clear.

F05 — Gander timing test

Compare actual airport-to-terminal journeys across seasons and modes.

Would falsify: either public duration if it is not operationally reliable.

F06 — Flight-to-ferry test

Map published flights against ferry schedules with realistic buffers.

Would falsify: the assumption that common arrivals create viable same-day connections.

F07 — Ferry-status test

Trace how a guest is directed from itinerary to current schedule and live status.

Would falsify: the assumption that static guidance remains safely connected to operations.

F08 — Missed-ferry simulation

Model a delay that makes the planned sailing impossible.

Would falsify: the assumption that the journey has a recovery path.

F09 — Chauffeured-transfer audit

Review pickup, luggage, delay, child, accessibility and cancellation terms.

56. Working conclusion

Fogo Island Inn turns pre-arrival into a visible part of the hospitality product because the stay cannot be separated from the route required to reach it.

The public system identifies:

gateway airports,

ground options,

ferry access,

charter alternatives,

Guest Itinerary Advisors,

seasonal activity,

available outdoor equipment,

family support,

mobility contact,

dietary disclosure,

and personalised itinerary planning.

Those mechanisms demonstrate that preparation extends beyond a confirmation email.

They do not prove the quality of execution.

The public evidence does not reveal:

the actual post-booking sequence,

the guest-specific itinerary,

the service-memory system,

the disruption protocol,

or the physical handoff.

The strongest pre-arrival finding is:

The Guest Does Not Need Every Experience Fixed.

The Guest Does Need Every Consequential Dependency Understood.

This creates a new allocation of certainty.

Transport requires:

current facts,

viable timing,

clear ownership,

and recovery.

Dietary and accessibility needs require:

early disclosure,

human review,

acknowledgement,

and propagation.

Activities can often retain:

flexibility,

weather responsiveness,

local recommendation,

and spontaneous discovery.

The appropriate architecture is not maximum planning.

It is:

Fix What Has Consequence.
Monitor What Can Change.
Preserve What Benefits From Discovery.

That is the first institutional expression of Consequence-Based Planning.

It remains architecture, not a formal KHDS pattern.
