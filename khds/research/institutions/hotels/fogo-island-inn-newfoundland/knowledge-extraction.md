Central architecture family

Place-Extended Hospitality Continuity

Status: Architecture family only
Not a formal KHDS pattern

Definition

Place-Extended Hospitality Continuity is a system in which a hospitality institution maintains explicit responsibility, necessary information, usable care and recovery across every transition it owns—before, during and after the guest's physical presence at the property.

Why it is needed

The stay is distributed across:

digital surfaces,

commercial systems,

external transport,

human advisors,

property arrival,

private rooms,

shared spaces,

dining contexts,

activities,

community partners,

and departure routes.

No single interface can carry the whole relationship.

Core invariant

The Delivery Mode May Change.

The Responsible Owner Must Remain Legible.

The Minimum Care Outcome Must Survive.

Entry condition

The architecture begins when public information can materially affect whether the guest can make and use the stay.

Exit condition

The architecture ends when the guest reaches the next usable journey state the institution explicitly agreed to support.

Failure condition

Continuity fails when:

responsibility becomes ambiguous,

acknowledged information resets,

a handoff has no receiving owner,

the guest must reconstruct system state,

or the promised recovery route is unusable.

Evidence condition

The architecture cannot be established by breadth of offerings alone.

It requires evidence of:

handoff ownership,

information continuity,

live-state reconciliation,

failure recovery,

and completion confirmation.

Architecture components

Component 1 — Discovery as First Arrival Layer

Public knowledge begins preparing the guest before commitment.

Component 2 — Suitability Before Desire Escalation

Material limitations should surface before emotional and financial commitment rises.

Component 3 — Total Commitment View

Room price, excluded transport, time and policy exposure should become comparable.

Component 4 — Arrival-Ready Reservation

A booking should be both commercially valid and operationally usable.

Component 5 — Consequence Consent

The guest explicitly understands deposit, cancellation, credit and timing effects.

The institution publishes what should happen.

The system shows current availability, condition, delay or ownership.

Evidence shows the service or relationship operated.

Level 7 — Performance proof

Evidence shows timeliness, completion, accuracy or reliability.

Level 8 — Experience proof

Bounded evidence records what a particular person experienced.

Level 9 — Outcome proof

Evidence shows a durable change in guest, staff, partner, community or ecological condition.

Level 10 — Causal proof

Evidence supports that the institution or intervention caused the outcome.
