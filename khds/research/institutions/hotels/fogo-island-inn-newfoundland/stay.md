1. Purpose of this file

This file studies how Fogo Island Inn sustains hospitality after the guest has arrived and become settled enough to begin the stay.

The stay is not treated as:

possession of a room,

use of amenities,

consumption of included meals,

completion of an itinerary,

or passive time between check-in and checkout.

Those are parts of the stay.

None is the whole system.

For Case #21, the stay is a multi-day relationship spanning:

private room life,

housekeeping,

turndown,

laundry,

food throughout the day,

common spaces,

wellness spaces,

service requests,

itinerary changes,

outdoor equipment,

guided and self-guided activity,

transport across the island,

community encounters,

privacy,

rest,

work,

weather,

and preparation for departure.

The institution publicly describes the room as a refuge while also positioning the guest as a guest of the island.

That creates a productive tension.

Refuge
and
Participation

must coexist.

The stay must make it possible to:

withdraw without becoming unsupported,

participate without becoming over-programmed,

receive personalised help without surrendering control,

move beyond the building without losing institutional continuity,

and change course without rebuilding the relationship from the beginning.

The purpose of this file is to identify:

what the Inn publicly promises during the stay,

how those promises form one service system,

where responsibility moves between teams and places,

which service mechanics remain unpublished,

how guest agency can survive inclusive hospitality,

how institutional memory should operate over several days,

and which restaurant-derived KHDS patterns survive this longer pressure test.

2. Stay boundary

The stay begins when the guest can use the room and institution without continuing to negotiate arrival.

It does not begin at one universal administrative timestamp.

It begins when the guest has enough:

access,

orientation,

service clarity,

physical security,

and next-step confidence

to act as a resident guest rather than an arriving traveller.

The stay ends when departure preparation becomes the dominant service state.

That transition may begin before checkout through:

packing,

bill review,

ferry coordination,

luggage collection,

departure meal planning,

or route reconfirmation.

2.1 Arrival boundary

Arrival transfers the guest into a usable stay state.

Stay tests whether that state remains coherent.

Settled Once
≠
Supported Throughout

2.2 Dining boundary

Food is a continuous part of the stay.

The later dining.md file will examine food as a complete hospitality and meaning system.

This file examines food where it supports:

itinerary continuity,

dietary memory,

room privacy,

and movement across the island.

2.3 Departure boundary

Stay ends when the institution begins actively converting residence into onward movement.

Last Night
≠
Departure Prepared

2.4 Working definition

The stay is the sustained hospitality period in which the institution coordinates room, food, service, activity, rest, place and changing guest needs as one continuous relationship across time.

3. Primary research question

Once the guest is settled, how does Fogo Island Inn sustain a coherent hospitality relationship across multiple days, spaces, teams and changing conditions?

4. Secondary research questions

4.1 Room life

Can the guest operate the room without repeated assistance?

Which room systems require explanation?

Does the room support both refuge and connection to place?

How are maintenance failures reported and resolved?

Can service enter the room without weakening privacy?

4.2 Service access

Does the guest know whom to contact?

Is there one understandable request surface or many departmental surfaces?

Can a request travel across shifts without repetition?

Is response status visible?

What happens when a request cannot be fulfilled?

4.3 Daily rhythm

How do food, housekeeping, activities and rest coexist?

Does the institution create useful rhythm without imposing a schedule?

Can the guest choose a quiet day?

Can plans change with weather, energy or interest?

Who reconciles those changes?

4.4 Place participation

When does the stay move beyond the building?

Who owns transport, equipment and guide handoffs?

Does the Inn remain responsible when service occurs in the community?

Can the guest participate independently?

Does community involvement preserve local agency?

4.5 Inclusion

Can the guest tell what is included at the moment of use?

Which experiences cost extra?

Does inclusive pricing reduce transactional friction?

Can inclusion create pressure to consume more?

Are refusal and rest treated as valid uses of the stay?

4.6 Accessibility and dignity

Do acknowledged needs persist across the room, dining and activity systems?

Can the guest request adjustments discreetly?

Are public spaces and island experiences equally usable?

Does assistance require repeated disclosure?

Can the itinerary adapt without framing the guest as a problem?

4.7 Relationship continuity

What should one team remember from another?

What should not be broadly shared?

How are preferences distinguished from requirements?

Can the guest correct an inaccurate assumption?

Does recognition deepen across the stay without becoming intrusive?

20. Preference, requirement and live state

These three information types should not be treated equally.

Example

The strongest stay finding is:

Full-Board Hospitality Is Not a List of Inclusions.

It Is a Continuity Promise Across Time and Place.

That continuity must survive movement between:

room and Dining Room,

privacy and service entry,

independent time and hosted activity,

Inn and island,

plan and weather,

one shift and the next,

and a request and its resolution.
