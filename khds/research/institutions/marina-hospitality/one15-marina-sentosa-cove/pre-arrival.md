# Pre-arrival

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does a vessel prepare for arrival when berth suitability, vessel documents, passenger/crew manifests, regulatory approval, CIQ timing and marina support must be aligned before the vessel reaches Sentosa Cove?

## Vessel identity must be prepared in advance

The berth enquiry workflow requires vessel name, type and size, while CIQ requires vessel details including length, beam and draft plus registration and insurance documents. [S2][S3]

Therefore:

Guest Ready
!=
Vessel Administratively Ready.

## CIQ documentation is required before arrival

ONE°15 requires the CIQ reservation form together with vessel registration, insurance, crew list and passenger list at least one day before arrival or departure. [S3]

Therefore:

Vessel En Route
!=
Clearance Ready.

## Passenger and crew state is part of vessel readiness

The CIQ process requires captain, crew and passenger information and valid travel documents. [S3]

Therefore:

Vessel Documentation Complete
!=
Passenger / Crew Documentation Complete.

## Manifest changes can reset readiness

After ICA approval, crew or passenger-list changes require the submission to be cancelled and resubmitted, and a new CIQ request must be made. [S3]

Therefore:

Previously Approved Clearance State
!=
Current Clearance State After Manifest Change.

This is a strong state-reset boundary.

## Arrival time is operationally significant

Arrival-time changes should be communicated in advance where possible, and late arrival can create charges or rescheduling requirements. [S3]

Therefore:

Confirmed CIQ Time
!=
Flexible Arrival Window.

## Weather or mechanical issues can change clearance state

ONE°15 states that bad weather or mechanical issues may require a booking to be amended, postponed or cancelled, with clearance rescheduled subject to operational and regulatory requirements. [S3]

Therefore:

Vessel Journey Disruption
Can Change
Regulatory Service State.

## Berth planning and CIQ planning remain distinct

A vessel may use CIQ without staying overnight, while a vessel wishing to stay after clearance must separately arrange berth availability. [S3]

Therefore:

Clearance Ready
!=
Stay Ready.

## Large-vessel arrival requires additional spatial planning

Vessels above 125ft require CIQ berth-availability review. [S3]

Therefore:

Arrival Time Confirmed
!=
Arrival Position Suitable Automatically.

## Marina support has its own operating states

Marina staff responsibilities include berthing/unberthing support, towing, line handling, fuel operation, utility monitoring, security passes and authority liaison. [S4]

Therefore:

Berth / CIQ Ready
!=
Every Marina Support Service Ready Automatically.

Public evidence establishes service domains, not actual instantaneous availability.

## Strongest pre-arrival boundaries

1. guest ready versus vessel administratively ready;
2. vessel en route versus clearance ready;
3. vessel documents versus passenger/crew documents;
4. previous approval versus current state after manifest change;
5. confirmed CIQ time versus flexible arrival window;
6. vessel disruption versus unchanged regulatory state;
7. clearance ready versus stay ready;
8. arrival time confirmed versus arrival position suitable;
9. berth/CIQ ready versus every support service ready.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation is normal yacht-entry preparation and immigration compliance.

That remains plausible.

The KHDS relevance strengthens if a single hospitality arrival depends on simultaneous readiness across vessel, guest, berth and regulatory states.

## Evidence limitations

The public record does not establish:

- pre-arrival message sequence;
- berth-assignment timing;
- document-validation turnaround;
- frequency of manifest changes;
- weather-related rescheduling rates;
- coordination latency between marina and authorities;
- pre-arrival exception ownership.
