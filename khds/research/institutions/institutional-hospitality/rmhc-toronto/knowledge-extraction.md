# Knowledge Extraction

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Case purpose

Case #44 tests whether family-centred institutional hospitality requires coordination across clinical and non-clinical states when one child's medical journey creates multiple family roles, eligibility conditions, capacity states and support relationships.

Primary falsification question:

How does a family-centred institutional hospitality system maintain continuity when clinical treatment, caregiver roles, accommodation eligibility, room capacity, hospital proximity, meals, education, respite and family needs change independently throughout a child's medical journey?

## Core institutional mechanism

The strongest Case #44 mechanism is:

**Family-Centred Hospitality Continuity Across Clinical and Non-Clinical States**

One medical case can create simultaneous roles and states such as:

- patient;
- parent/legal guardian;
- sibling;
- other accompanying family member;
- minor parent with guardian;
- House applicant;
- wait-listed family;
- admitted House family;
- Family Room user;
- sleep-space user;
- school student;
- meal beneficiary;
- social-work referral subject. [S1][S3][S12][S13]

These states do not automatically activate together.

## Falsification requirements

### Care-Linked Hospitality Architecture

Would weaken if clinical state rarely affects hospitality eligibility, priority, timing or duration.

### Journey-State / Service-State Separation

Would weaken if one valid family state reliably activates all relevant House, Family Room and school services.

### Entitlement-Aware Hospitality

Would weaken if payment or simple presence is sufficient for access regardless of referral, role, capacity or unit.

### Multi-Owner Responsibility Continuity

Would weaken if one institution consistently owns the entire family transition.

### Role-Partitioned / Multi-Relationship Hospitality Architecture

Would weaken if one role sufficiently represents all family relationships.

### Institutional Journey Economics

Would weaken as an architecture concern if service configuration and handoff complexity have no meaningful relationship to cost.

No such cost test has been performed.
