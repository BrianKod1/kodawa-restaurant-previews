# Discovery

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Research question

How does a family discover which RMHC Toronto service fits its situation when the House, hospital Family Rooms, sleep spaces, school and family-support services have different eligibility, capacity, cost and operating rules?

## RMHC Toronto is not one hospitality product

RMHC Toronto publicly operates an 81-family House in downtown Toronto, seven Family Rooms across five hospitals, family-support programs and an RMHC Toronto School for eligible patients and siblings staying at the House. [S2][S3][S8]

Therefore:

RMHC Toronto Relationship != One Service Type.

## The hospitality unit is the family, not only the patient

House eligibility is defined around parents/legal guardians of a child age 18 or younger receiving treatment for a life-threatening or serious illness at SickKids or another qualifying hospital. Siblings and other family members may accompany the parents. [S1]

Therefore:

Medical Patient != Entire Hospitality Unit.

And:

One Child's Treatment != One Hospitality Role.

## House eligibility is conditional

Families who stay at the House must live at least 55 km away, be parents/legal guardians of an eligible child, have a referral from a Toronto hospital social worker and complete the application process. [S1][S2]

Therefore:

Child Receiving Treatment != House Eligibility Automatically.

## Family Room access uses a different eligibility model

Family Rooms are open to family members of children being treated in specified units at partner hospitals. Eligibility varies by hospital and unit. [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]

Therefore:

House Eligibility != Family Room Eligibility.

And:

Hospital Presence != Family Room Access Automatically.

## The patient and Family Room user are intentionally separated

RMHC Toronto states that Family Rooms are clinical-free spaces intended for families to rest and recharge, and asks that patients not use them. [S3]

Therefore:

Patient Role != Family Room Guest Role.

## Family Room offerings vary locally

Family Rooms can include lounge space, snacks, showers, laundry, sleep rooms, sleeping pods or napping areas, but the exact facilities differ by site. [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]

Therefore:

RMHC Family Room != Uniform Service Inventory.

## Cost does not define eligibility

House families are asked to contribute $15 per night, while Family Rooms are free to use. [S1][S2][S3]

Therefore:

Payment != Primary Entitlement Mechanism.

## Capacity is visible during discovery

RMHC Toronto explicitly warns that House rooms cannot be booked in advance and may not be available when a family needs one. [S1]

Therefore:

Eligible Family != Guaranteed Room.

## Alternative accommodation is part of the discovery problem

Families are told to make alternate arrangements because House availability cannot be guaranteed. [S1]

Therefore:

RMHC Referral Path != Complete Accommodation Certainty.

## Family Rooms can also be capacity-constrained

Family Room sleep rooms do not use a general reservation system. Some are assigned by hospital staff, while sleep pods at several sites are first-come, first-served. [S3]

Therefore:

Eligible Family Room User != Guaranteed Sleep Space.

## Scale

RMHC Toronto reported 4,331 families supported, 27,069 nights of comfort and 67,989 Family Room visits in 2024. [S8]

These figures establish service scale, not capacity sufficiency.

## Strongest discovery boundaries

1. RMHC Toronto relationship versus one service type;
2. patient versus entire hospitality unit;
3. treatment versus House eligibility;
4. House eligibility versus Family Room eligibility;
5. patient role versus Family Room guest role;
6. Family Room label versus uniform service inventory;
7. payment versus primary entitlement mechanism;
8. eligible family versus guaranteed House room;
9. eligible Family Room user versus guaranteed sleep space.

## Competing explanation

A simpler explanation is that RMHC Toronto operates several support programs with different entry criteria.

That remains plausible.

The KHDS relevance becomes stronger if family continuity requires coordination across hospital treatment, social-work referral, House capacity, Family Room access and family roles.

## Evidence limitations

The public record does not establish how families first learn about RMHC Toronto, referral conversion rates, representative comprehension of House versus Family Room rules, real-time room availability visibility, current unmet demand at each Family Room or whether one family identity is shared across systems.

## Next evidence

Inspect Reservation / Eligibility to determine how treatment context, social-work referral, background checks, wait-list priority and local Family Room allocation convert family need into hospitality access.
