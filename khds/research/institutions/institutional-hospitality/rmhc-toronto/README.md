# Ronald McDonald House Charities Toronto — KHDS Case #44

Public-evidence coverage: partial

## Institution

Ronald McDonald House Charities Toronto is being studied as a family-centred institutional hospitality system linked to a child's medical journey but operationally distinct from clinical care.

The institution includes:

- an 81-family House;
- seven Family Rooms across five hospitals;
- school, meal, laundry, wellness and family-support services;
- referral and wait-list processes tied to medical context;
- non-clinical respite spaces inside hospitals. [S1][S2][S3][S11][S12][S19][S31]

Case #44 tests whether hospitality continuity around care requires coordination across family roles, treatment state, referral, accommodation capacity, school, meals, respite and repeated episodes of care.

## Research date

Initial public-evidence research: 2026-11-06

## Research question

How does a family-centred institutional hospitality system maintain continuity when clinical treatment, caregiver roles, accommodation eligibility, room capacity, hospital proximity, meals, education, respite and family needs change independently throughout a child's medical journey?

## Competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation is conventional charitable accommodation:

hospital referral
+ room allocation
+ family support
+ meals
+ school
+ respite space.

Under that explanation, RMHC Toronto can be understood as a charitable residence and family-support organization operating near and inside hospitals.

Case #44 therefore tests whether the changing medical journey creates meaningful hospitality-state and responsibility dependencies beyond ordinary accommodation.

## Initial findings

### The hospitality unit is larger than the patient

House eligibility and family-support rules explicitly involve parents, legal guardians, siblings and other family members around the child's treatment. [S1]

Therefore:

Medical Patient != Entire Hospitality Unit.

### House access is not a commercial booking

Access depends on treatment context, distance, referral, adult clearance and capacity. [S1][S2]

Therefore:

Payment != Primary Entitlement Mechanism.

And:

Hospitality Need != Direct Self-Booking.

### Eligibility does not guarantee capacity

The House cannot be booked in advance and eligible families may remain on a wait list. [S1]

Therefore:

Eligible Family != Guaranteed Room.

### Wait-list priority depends partly on medical state

Priority is influenced by inpatient/outpatient/clinic status and distance, and can change as referrals and discharges change. [S2]

Therefore:

Wait-List Position != Simple First-Come Order.

### Hospital admission and House admission are different states

A child can be receiving care while the family remains without a House room. [S1][S2]

Therefore:

Hospital Admission != House Admission.

### Family Room access is separate from House access

Family Room eligibility depends on hospital/unit rules and is not the same as House referral. [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]

Therefore:

House Eligibility != Family Room Eligibility.

### Patient and Family Room user are different roles

Family Rooms are explicitly non-clinical spaces for family members rather than patients. [S3][S23]

Therefore:

Patient Role != Family Room Guest Role.

### Hospitality service does not imply clinical responsibility

Family Rooms are located inside hospitals and near the bedside but remain non-clinical. [S3][S23][S24]

Therefore:

Proximity to Care != Clinical Integration.

And:

Hospitality Service Begins != RMHC Assumes Clinical Responsibility.

### The House is a temporary home system, not just a bed

The House provides bedrooms, kitchen, food storage, laundry, school, family areas and multiple programs. [S11][S14][S22]

Therefore:

House Stay != Bedroom Occupancy Only.

### Education is a separate service state

School participation requires its own registration and can shift between full-day and adaptable learning. [S12][S13]

Therefore:

House Guest != School Student Automatically.

And:

Student Enrolled != Fixed Attendance Mode.

### Dining is multi-mode

Families may self-cater, use pantry items or receive prepared meals. [S11][S14]

Therefore:

RMHC Dining != One Meal Service.

### Medical scheduling and hospitality scheduling are not automatically synchronized

If an appointment or surgery changes, RMHC Toronto requires confirmation through the hospital social worker before changing expected arrival dates. [S13]

Therefore:

Medical Schedule Change != Hospitality Date Change Automatically.

### Repeated medical care can create repeated hospitality episodes

A new House referral is required for each visit. [S1]

Therefore:

Prior House Stay != Persistent Future Accommodation Entitlement.

And:

Medical Journey Continuity != Continuous Single Hospitality Stay.

### Departure can require institutional handoff

RMHC Toronto School sends school records or summaries back to the student's hometown school when the student leaves. [S13]

Therefore:

RMHC School Departure != Education Record Closure Without Handoff.

### The home-like atmosphere is deliberately designed

The House was designed around natural light, shared spaces, family visibility, chalkboards, gardens and household routines. [S21][S22]

Therefore:

Home-Like Atmosphere != Accidental By-Product.

### Narrative unity exceeds operational unity

RMHC Toronto presents one family-centred identity across House, Family Rooms and School, but referral, capacity, sleep allocation, education and clinical responsibility remain separately governed. [S1][S3][S12][S31]

Therefore:

Narrative Unity != Operational Unity.

## Working research mechanism

- adult clearance;

- room availability;

It is not a canonical KHDS pattern.

## Care-linked hospitality test

Case #44 introduces a specific institutional question:

Can hospitality remain continuous around a medical journey when the hospitality institution depends on clinical context but does not own clinical responsibility?

The public evidence shows that:

- clinical treatment context affects eligibility; [S1]
- hospital social workers participate in referral; [S1]
- clinical status affects wait-list priority; [S2]
- hospital/unit location affects Family Room access; [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]
- medical schedule changes require hospitality-date confirmation; [S13]
- hospital discharge can influence system capacity while not always ending the House stay immediately. [S2][S17]

Therefore:

Clinical Journey State != Every Hospitality Service State.

This generates a non-canonical research direction around Care-Linked Hospitality Architecture.

## Commercial Journey Economics

Case #44 is not primarily commercial hospitality, but it still has operating economics.

Relevant economic layers include:

- accommodation cost;
- low family contribution;
- donated meal support;
- school and program cost;
- Family Room operation;
- donor funding;
- volunteer-supported service delivery. [S11][S14][S22][S31]

The public record does not establish:

- cost per occupied family room;
- cost per Family Room visit;
- marginal cost of school or meal services;
- wait-list cost;
- failed-handoff cost;
- cost per family supported;
- donor acquisition economics.

Low Family Charge != Low Service Cost.

And:

Non-Commercial Guest Relationship != Non-Economic Operating System.
