Purpose

This file establishes how KHDS compares hospitality institutions that do not share one operating format.

It creates a comparison system for:

hotels,

inns,

resorts,

wellness and spa institutions,

hostels,

members' clubs,

food halls,

cultural hospitality venues,

event and conference venues,

retreats,

and later hospitality institutions selected for research value.

It exists because a restaurant comparison assumes a relatively concentrated visit.

Many hospitality institutions involve:

longer journeys,

repeated service loops,

multiple spaces,

private and public states,

overnight responsibility,

external transport,

multiple stakeholder roles,

several capacity systems,

partner delivery,

and delayed or route-dependent closure.

The comparison architecture must preserve enough common structure to identify transferable knowledge.

It must also preserve enough sector difference to avoid forcing every institution into a restaurant model.

Why the restaurant comparison files remain separate

The existing files:

khds/research/comparison/arrival.md,

khds/research/comparison/atmosphere.md,

khds/research/comparison/photography.md,

khds/research/comparison/reservation.md,

and khds/research/comparison/story.md

compare twenty independent restaurant cases.

They remain valid restaurant-domain evidence.

Institution cases should not be appended directly as though they were additional restaurants.

Doing so would create false equivalence around:

duration,

inventory,

service recurrence,

private-space responsibility,

transport dependency,

dining relevance,

participation,

and departure.

Restaurant findings may enter institution comparison as transfer hypotheses.

They do not enter as pre-validated universal rules.

Comparison objective

The institution-comparison system must answer five questions.

What mechanism recurs across institution types?

What changes because the sector, duration or guest role changes?

Which failure state becomes visible only in the expanded setting?

Which boundary survives the change of institution type?

What evidence would justify a pattern-level transfer decision?

The comparison is not a ranking.

It does not identify the “best” institution.

It identifies:

mechanisms,

conditions,

boundaries,

contradictions,

and proof obligations.

Unit of comparison

KHDS does not compare institutions as undifferentiated brands.

It compares structured units.

Unit 1 — Institution

The legal, operating or publicly accountable hospitality entity being studied.

Unit 2 — Institution type

The sector context that changes expected journey and responsibility.

Unit 3 — Service proposition

What the institution publicly offers a guest or participant.

Unit 4 — Guest intent

What the person is trying to accomplish.

Unit 5 — Stakeholder role

The role through which a person enters the institution.

Unit 6 — Journey state

The guest's current position in the hospitality relationship.

Unit 7 — Hospitality mechanism

The rule, process, role, interface or environment that supports an outcome.

Unit 8 — Capacity stock

The constrained resource consumed by participation.

Unit 9 — Entitlement

What the guest can expect after a particular agreement, payment or eligibility condition.

Unit 10 — Handoff

A transfer of information, care, custody, control or responsibility.

Unit 11 — Failure state

The condition in which the published or expected route cannot complete.

Unit 12 — Recovery route

The mechanism through which usable hospitality is restored.

Unit 13 — Evidence claim

A statement whose proof level, scope and date can be evaluated.

Unit 14 — Outcome

A bounded change in guest, staff, partner, community or environmental condition.

Unit 15 — Responsibility boundary

The explicit point at which institutional responsibility begins, changes or ends.

Handoff architecture

A handoff transfers one or more states.

Information handoff

Guest details, needs, plans or current state move to a new responsible role.

Care handoff

Responsibility for support moves.

Custody handoff

Responsibility for luggage, belongings, equipment or another physical item moves.

Control handoff

The institution, guest or partner gains or loses operational control.

Entitlement handoff

A partner or role becomes responsible for delivering something already promised.

Relationship handoff

The guest moves between institutional, partner and community contexts.

Each handoff must record:

sender,

receiver,

object or state transferred,

and recovery route.

Outcome-evidence architecture

Institution comparison uses ten proof levels.

Level 1 — Existence

A role, service, programme, space or relationship is named.

Level 2 — Rule

The institution states what should happen.

Level 3 — Procedure

The steps, owner and handoff are described.

Level 4 — Configuration

The service is tied to a specific guest, time or context.

Level 5 — Live state

Current availability, ownership, delay or condition is visible.

Level 6 — Activity

Evidence shows the mechanism operated.

Level 7 — Performance

Evidence shows completion, accuracy, reliability or timeliness.

Level 8 — Experience

Bounded evidence records what a particular person experienced.

Level 9 — Outcome

Evidence shows a durable change.

Level 10 — Causality

Evidence supports that the intervention caused the outcome.

Comparison must not average these levels into one score.

A case may have strong existence proof and weak outcome proof.

Both facts must remain visible.

Not-applicable protocol

Cross-sector comparison requires disciplined use of Not Applicable.

Dining may be absent from a wellness venue.

Overnight private-space controls may be absent from a food hall.

Membership renewal may be absent from a hotel.

The comparison should ask:

Is the dimension structurally relevant?

Is an equivalent responsibility carried elsewhere?

Is the mechanism absent or merely undisclosed?

Would absence create a material guest risk?

Use:

N/A — structurally irrelevant,

Equivalent mechanism — see [dimension],

Public evidence gap,

or Observed absence.

Do not use blank cells.

Scoring prohibition

The institution-comparison architecture does not produce a single numeric score.

A score would collapse:

guest consequence,

sector relevance,

mechanism strength,

and outcome uncertainty.

KHDS uses structured judgments instead:

support,

contradiction,

boundary,

transfer condition,

and governance decision.

Tables may count observed mechanisms.

Counts must not become quality rankings.

Comparison-judgment vocabulary

Each mechanism receives one or more labels.

Strong support

The mechanism is clearly evidenced and materially relevant.

Conditional support

The mechanism is supported only under named conditions.

Partial support

Only part of the mechanism or outcome is evidenced.

Contradiction

The case exposes a credible failure, exception or opposite condition.

Boundary strengthening

The case makes the pattern safer or more precise without increasing maturity.

Transfer pressure

The case makes cross-sector relevance plausible.

Sector-specific

The mechanism appears tied to institution type.

Not applicable

The mechanism is structurally irrelevant.

Evidence insufficient

The case cannot support a judgment.

Promotion-watch

The evidence may become maturity-relevant after more independent cases.

Before a restaurant-derived pattern can be treated as hospitality-wide, comparison must establish:

the mechanism appears in more than one institution type,

the mechanism serves a materially similar hospitality need,

its failure state is comparable,

its boundary survives sector change,

its evidence is independent,

and the wording does not preserve restaurant-only assumptions.

What Is the Mechanism
When the Restaurant Surface Is Removed?

Final architecture position

KHDS can compare different hospitality institutions without pretending their journeys are identical.

The shared unit is not:

room,

table,

treatment,

ticket,

or membership.

The shared unit is:

A Hospitality Responsibility
Carried Through a Defined Guest State
Under Known Conditions
With a Recoverable Failure Route.

This allows KHDS to compare:

a hotel room and a treatment appointment,

a meal entitlement and a member privilege,

a ferry handoff and venue admission,

a private refuge and a shared hall,

or a checkout and an aftercare transition

without claiming that the visible products are the same.
